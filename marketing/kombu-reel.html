That white film on your kombu isn't mold.

Most people wipe it off or throw the sheet out. Both are mistakes.

The Reveal

It's mannitol. A natural sugar.

It surfaces during drying — and it's the visible sign the kombu is actually ready to work.

The Rule

Cold water. Never boiling.

Kombu releases glutamate into cold water without heat. Boiling makes it bitter and slimy — this isn't style, it's chemistry.

The Method

Soak cold. Twenty minutes, minimum.

Submerge kombu in cold water, let it sit, then gently heat to just below a simmer before removing it.

The Translation

It replaces a parmesan rind and four hours.

Cold-soaked with a few dried shiitake, kombu gives an Italian soup the same depth — just a shorter route to the same place.
