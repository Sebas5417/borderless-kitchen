The exact same technique makes food better or worse depending on timing.

It's salt. Same chemistry every time — osmosis. The clock is what changes the outcome.

The Reveal

Osmosis. Salt pulls moisture, always.

Whether that's good or bad for your dish depends entirely on how long you wait and what you're salting.

Case One

Salt eggplant an hour ahead.

The moisture loss collapses spongy cell walls before cooking — eggplant stops drinking oil and turns silky instead.

→borderless kitchen
2
Case Two

Salt a steak 40 minutes before searing.

Moisture is drawn out first, then reabsorbed — carrying the salt deep into the meat as it goes back in.

The One Rule

Same chemistry. Opposite goal, opposite timing.

Vegetables: salt early to remove water. Meat: salt early to season deep. Never salt either right before the pan and expect the same result.
