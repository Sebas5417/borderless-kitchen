---

**The framework, assembled**

Here is everything in one place:

**Step 1: Choose a dish from one tradition.**
Start with something you know well — a dish you've cooked before, from either Italian or Japanese cooking. The more familiar, the better. You understand its structure.

**Step 2: Identify the function of each component.**
Not "what is it" but *what does it do*:
- What provides umami depth?
- What provides fat?
- What provides acid?
- What provides texture contrast?
- What is the aromatic base?
- What is the finishing element?

**Step 3: Find the parallel in the other tradition.**
Using the Flavor Pairing Matrix from Day 3, find the structural equivalent for each component from the other cuisine.

**Step 4: Decide — substitute or amplify?**
- **Substitute:** Replace one component with its parallel (miso instead of Parmigiano in a risotto)
- **Amplify:** Use both (Parmigiano + miso together for double umami depth)
- **Hybrid technique:** Use one tradition's technique with the other's ingredients

**Step 5: Cook it. Trust the structure.**
The flavor may be unfamiliar. The structure will hold. Good cooking is architecture first, flavor second — if the architecture is sound, the flavor will make sense.

---

**Example: Cacio e Pepe → Dashi Cacio e Pepe**

*Original components and their functions:*
- Pecorino Romano → fermented protein umami + fat + salt
- Black pepper → aromatic spice, bridge between flavors
- Pasta water → starch-emulsified fat carrier
- Technique: emulsification off-heat

*Japanese parallels:*
- Pecorino → white miso (same function, different fermented base)
- Pasta water → dashi + pasta water (adds inosinate to the glutamate in the cheese)
- Black pepper → sanshō pepper (Japanese mountain pepper — similar heat, more floral)

*Fusion approach:* Amplify — use both Pecorino and miso, replace half the pasta water with dashi.

*Result:* You made this on Day 2. It tastes like Cacio e Pepe that's been improved, not diluted.

**Your Day 5 assignment**

Pick one dish. Apply the framework. Cook it tonight.

Some starting points if you need them:

**Italian dishes that translate beautifully:**
- Amatriciana → add katsuobushi to the guanciale; replace some tomato with dashi
- Pesto → add white miso and a few drops of sesame oil; use shiso alongside basil
- Osso buco → replace white wine with sake; add kombu to the braising liquid
- Tiramisu → replace some espresso with hojicha (roasted Japanese green tea)

**Japanese dishes that translate beautifully:**
- Ramen broth → add Parmigiano rind to the dashi; finish with a knob of butter
- Gyoza → use a filling of pork + fennel + miso; serve with tomato ponzu
- Ochazuke → replace green tea with a light fish broth; add basil and olive oil
- Tempura batter → add Parmigiano to the dipping sauce; serve with lemon instead of grated daikon
