Dried shiitake mushrooms are one of East Asia's great sources of savory depth. Drying concentrates their flavor, and dried shiitake yield far more guanylate than fresh ones once soaked and cooked. Guanylate is an umami compound that multiplies the effect of the glutamate in kombu, soy sauce or Parmigiano.

## Using them

Rinse, then soak in cold water for several hours or overnight in the fridge (or in warm water for about 30 minutes if you're short on time). Squeeze them out, slice off the tough stems, and use the caps in stir-fries, braises, japchae or soups.

**Keep the soaking liquid.** Strained, it's a vegetarian stock in its own right, and it's the base of kombu-shiitake dashi.

## Buying

Thick, pale-gilled caps with deep cracks on top (*donko*) are prized for braising; thinner caps (*koshin*) rehydrate faster. Store dried shiitake airtight in a cool, dry place.
