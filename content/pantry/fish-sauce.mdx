Fish sauce is the liquid drained from anchovies (or other small fish) salted and fermented for months, often a year or more. It's the backbone seasoning of Vietnamese and Thai cooking, and close relatives are used across Southeast Asia and in Korean kimchi.

On its own it smells strong. In a dish it disappears into savory depth, the way anchovies do in an Italian sauce. A few drops in a vinaigrette, a soup or a tomato sauce add glutamate without tasting fishy.

## What to look for

- **Short ingredient list:** anchovies and salt, sometimes with a little sugar. Brands that list only fish and salt are usually the cleanest-tasting.
- **"First press"** traditionally means the first liquid drained from the barrel, prized for flavor, but the term isn't regulated, so treat it as a hint rather than a guarantee.
- **Color:** amber to reddish-brown. It darkens over time once opened, which is normal.

## Storage

Keep it tightly capped in a cool, dark cupboard; the salt keeps it stable for a long time. Refrigerating it slows the darkening.
