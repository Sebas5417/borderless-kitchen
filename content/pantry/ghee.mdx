Ghee is butter cooked until its water evaporates and its milk solids lightly brown, then strained. The result is a golden, nutty-tasting fat that's shelf-stable and can be heated far hotter than butter without burning. It's the classic cooking fat of Indian cuisine.

## Ghee vs clarified butter

Both remove the milk solids. Ghee is cooked longer so the solids toast before straining, which gives it a deeper, nuttier flavor than plain clarified butter.

## Using it

Use it to bloom spices for a *tadka* (tempering), to fry, or anywhere you want butter flavor at high heat. Unopened jars keep at room temperature; once opened, keep it covered and use a clean spoon.
