Glutinous rice, also called sweet rice or sticky rice, is rice whose starch is almost entirely amylopectin, which makes it very sticky and chewy when cooked. Despite the name, it contains **no gluten**: "glutinous" refers to its glue-like texture.

It's the rice behind Japanese mochi, Korean rice cakes and *yaksik*, Thai sticky rice and mango sticky rice, and Chinese *zongzi*. Ground, it becomes glutinous (sweet) rice flour, used for mochi and Korean *tteok*.

## Cooking

Glutinous rice is usually soaked for several hours or overnight and then **steamed** rather than boiled, so the grains stay separate and chewy instead of turning to mush.
