Soba are thin Japanese noodles made with buckwheat flour, usually blended with wheat flour for elasticity. They're eaten cold with a dipping sauce (*zaru soba*) or hot in dashi broth.

## Read the label

The buckwheat share varies a lot. **Juwari** soba is 100% buckwheat; **nihachi** is the classic 80% buckwheat and 20% wheat. Many supermarket soba contain more wheat than buckwheat.

**Soba is not automatically gluten-free.** Only noodles made from 100% buckwheat, in a facility without wheat, are free of gluten. Buckwheat is also a common allergen in Japan, and labels list it prominently.

## Cooking

Boil in plenty of water, then rinse well under cold water to wash off surface starch, which keeps the noodles from turning gummy. Save the cooking water (*soba-yu*) to stir into the leftover dipping sauce, a Japanese custom.
