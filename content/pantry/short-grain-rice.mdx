Short-grain rice (Japonica) has plump, rounded grains with a high share of amylopectin, the starch that makes cooked rice sticky and cohesive. It's the everyday rice of Japan and Korea, used for plain steamed rice, sushi, onigiri, kimbap and bibimbap.

## Short vs medium grain

Many popular US brands labeled "sushi rice" are actually **medium-grain** Japonica grown in California. They behave similarly and work well for most Japanese and Korean dishes. True short-grain rice is a little stickier and rounder.

## Cooking it

Rinse until the water runs nearly clear, soak for 20 to 30 minutes, then cook with slightly more water than rice by volume (or follow your rice cooker's lines). Let it rest, covered, for 10 minutes before fluffing.

Short-grain rice is **not** a substitute for Italian risotto rice, which is bred to release starch into a sauce while staying firm.
