Udon are thick, chewy Japanese wheat noodles made from flour, water and salt. They're served hot in dashi broth (*kake udon*), cold with a dipping sauce, stir-fried (*yaki udon*), or in curry.

## Fresh, frozen or dried

- **Frozen udon** is the cook's favorite: the texture is closest to fresh, and it cooks from frozen in a minute or two.
- **Shelf-stable vacuum packs** are soft and convenient, but less chewy.
- **Dried udon** keeps for months and is thinner and firmer once cooked.

The prized chewiness, *koshi*, comes from a well-kneaded, firm dough. Sanuki udon from Kagawa Prefecture is the best-known regional style.

Udon's mild flavor makes it a good carrier for Italian sauces too: a thick bolognese clings to it the way it clings to tagliatelle.
