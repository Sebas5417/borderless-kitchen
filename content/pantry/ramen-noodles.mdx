Ramen noodles are wheat noodles made with *kansui*, an alkaline mineral water that gives them their springy bite, yellow tint and slightly eggy aroma. The alkalinity also lets them hold their texture in hot broth.

## Fresh, frozen or dried

- **Fresh or frozen ramen noodles** (from Japanese and Asian grocers, or the freezer case) are the closest to a ramen shop. Cook them briefly in plenty of boiling water.
- **Dried ramen noodles** sold without a flavor packet are a good pantry backup.
- **Instant ramen** is pre-fried or air-dried and seasoned; it's its own category.

**No kansui? Baking soda trick.** Boiling spaghetti in water with a spoonful of baking soda makes the water alkaline and gives the pasta a ramen-like chew. It's a well-known shortcut, though not the same as real ramen noodles.
