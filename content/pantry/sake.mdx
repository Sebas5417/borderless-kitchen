Fermented rice wine. The equivalent of white wine in Japanese cooking — used for deglazing, as a marinade base, and for softening the raw edge of fish or meat stocks. Where white wine adds tartaric acid sharpness, sake brings a softer sweetness from rice starch and a more neutral fermented depth.

For cooking, use *junmai* (pure rice sake) — flavor carries better than premium drinking grades, which lose their nuance in heat. Dry sherry is the best substitute when sake isn't available.
