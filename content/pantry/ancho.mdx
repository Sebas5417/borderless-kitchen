The ancho is the dried form of the poblano pepper. It's wide, wrinkled and deep reddish-brown, with mild heat and a sweet, fruity flavor often compared to raisins or dried plums. It's one of the most-used dried chiles in Mexican cooking.

## Where it goes

Ancho gives body and sweetness to red chile sauces, moles, adobos and chili. It's often paired with guajillo, which adds tang and brighter red color.

## Using it

Stem and seed the chiles, toast them briefly in a dry pan until fragrant, then soak in hot water for about 15 to 20 minutes and blend with some of the soaking liquid. Good anchos are soft and pliable, not brittle.
