Pecorino Romano is a hard, salty sheep's-milk cheese and the cheese of Rome's classic pastas: cacio e pepe, carbonara, amatriciana and gricia. It's a protected (DOP) cheese, made in Lazio, Sardinia and the province of Grosseto in Tuscany.

## Pecorino vs Parmigiano

Pecorino is sharper, saltier and tangier than Parmigiano Reggiano, which is made from cow's milk and tastes nuttier and sweeter. They aren't interchangeable in the Roman dishes: cacio e pepe made with Parmigiano is a different, milder dish.

## Using it

Grate it finely just before using, and because it's salty, salt the pasta water a little less than usual. For cacio e pepe, melt it into starchy pasta water off the heat so it turns creamy instead of clumping.
