The guajillo is a dried Mexican chile, the dried form of the mirasol pepper. It's long, smooth and deep red, with mild to medium heat and a bright, tangy, slightly berry-like flavor. It's one of the most-used dried chiles in Mexican cooking.

## Where it goes

Guajillo is the backbone of red chile sauces and adobos: birria, pozole rojo, red enchilada sauce and many salsas. It's often paired with ancho, which adds sweetness and depth.

## Using it

Wipe the chiles, remove the stems and seeds, and toast them briefly in a dry pan until fragrant (a few seconds a side; burnt chiles turn bitter). Soak in hot water for about 15 minutes, then blend with some of the soaking liquid. Strain the sauce: guajillo skins are tough.

Store dried chiles airtight in a cool, dark place. They should be pliable, not brittle.
