Panko are Japanese breadcrumbs made from crustless bread, ground into large, airy flakes rather than fine crumbs. That open structure absorbs less oil and stays crisp longer, which is why panko is the coating on tonkatsu, korokke and ebi fry.

## Using it

- **Frying:** flour, then beaten egg, then panko, pressed on gently.
- **Baking:** toss panko with a little oil or melted butter first so it browns in the oven.
- **Toppings:** toasted in olive oil with a little garlic, it's a crunchy topping for pasta, the way Italian cooks use *pangrattato*.

Plain panko is mostly wheat flour, yeast, salt and a little sugar, often with some vegetable oil. Check the label if you need it gluten-free; rice-based versions exist.
