Tamari is a Japanese soy sauce made with mostly or entirely soybeans and little or no wheat, originally the liquid that pooled in vats of soybean miso in central Japan; today it is brewed in its own right. It's thicker, darker and rounder in flavor than everyday soy sauce, with less of the sharp, alcoholic edge that wheat brings.

## Is it gluten-free?

Often, but not always. Some tamari contains a small amount of wheat. If gluten matters, buy a bottle that says **gluten-free** on the label rather than relying on the name.

## Using it

Use tamari anywhere you'd use soy sauce. It's especially good as a dipping sauce for sashimi, in glazes where its body helps, and for anyone avoiding wheat.
