## Carousel 1: 5 Ingredients That Make Everything Taste Better

**Hook (Slide 1):**
5 ingredients that make every dish taste
like it took twice as long.
(Most people have never heard of #3.)

**Slide 2:**
**#1 — Miso**
Fermented soybean paste.
One spoonful adds months of depth.

**Slide 3:**
Stir it into butter for steak.
Whisk it into salad dressing.
Add it to the base of any braise.

**Slide 4:**
**#2 — Kombu**
Dried kelp, loaded with glutamate.
Drop a strip into any broth while it heats.

**Slide 5:**
Glutamate is the compound that makes
Parmesan taste like Parmesan.
Kombu delivers the same thing, silently.

**Slide 6:**
**#3 — Fish Sauce**
Yes, it smells. Use it anyway.
A teaspoon disappears — but the depth stays.

**Slide 7:**
**#4 — Dried Mushrooms**
Shiitake or porcini. Grind them to powder.
Add a pinch anywhere you'd add salt.

**Slide 8:**
**#5 — Anchovy Paste**
Melts into olive oil in 30 seconds.
Transforms a simple pasta into something serious.

**Slide 9:**
These five ingredients share one thing:
umami — the fifth taste.
Stack them. The effect multiplies.

## Carousel 2: Why Your Pasta Tastes Flat (And How to Fix It in 2 Minutes)

**Hook (Slide 1):**
Your pasta tastes flat.
It's not the recipe. It's two things
you're doing at the end.

**Slide 2:**
Most home cooks drain the pasta
and pour the sauce on top.
That's where the flavor gets lost.

**Slide 3:**
**Fix #1 — Reserve the pasta water.**
One cup. Before you drain.
This is liquid gold.

**Slide 4:**
Pasta water is starchy, salty, hot.
Add it to your sauce a splash at a time.
It emulsifies. The sauce clings.

**Slide 5:**
**Fix #2 — Finish in the pan.**
Add drained pasta directly to the sauce.
Toss over heat for 60 seconds.

**Slide 6:**
The pasta absorbs the sauce.
The sauce absorbs the pasta starch.
They become one thing.

**Slide 7:**
**The bonus fix — add miso.**
Half a teaspoon into the sauce,
before you add the pasta.

**Slide 8:**
You won't taste miso.
You'll taste something richer,
deeper, more complete.

**Slide 9:**
Flat pasta = sauce sitting on top.
Good pasta = sauce cooked in.
Two minutes, total difference.

## Carousel 3: The Japanese Technique That Makes Any Sauce Richer

**Hook (Slide 1):**
Japanese cooks have a technique
that makes any sauce taste richer.
No cream. No extra butter. No extra time.

**Slide 2:**
It's called **umami layering.**
You build depth by stacking
ingredients that amplify each other.

**Slide 3:**
Here's the science that matters:
Glutamate + inosinate together
= 6-8x more perceived umami.

**Slide 4:**
That's not a typo.
Six to eight times more flavor.
From the same ingredients, stacked correctly.

**Slide 5:**
**The combination:**
Kombu (glutamate) + katsuobushi (inosinate).
This is dashi — Japanese stock.

**Slide 6:**
You can apply this anywhere.
Tomato sauce: add a parmesan rind (glutamate)
+ anchovy paste (inosinate). Same science.

**Slide 7:**
Mushroom gravy: dried porcini (glutamate)
+ a small piece of kombu (more glutamate)
+ a splash of soy sauce (both). Layered.

**Slide 8:**
The rule is simple:
find two umami sources from different families
and use them together.

**Slide 9:**
The sauce doesn't taste Japanese.
It doesn't taste different.
It just tastes more like itself.

## Carousel 4: 3 Pantry Swaps That Professional Cooks Actually Make

**Hook (Slide 1):**
3 swaps professional cooks make
that home cooks don't know about.
None of them are complicated.

**Slide 2:**
**Swap #1**
White wine → sake
(when deglazing or braising)

**Slide 3:**
Sake is lower in acidity.
It adds sweetness and amino acids.
The sauce becomes rounder, softer.

**Slide 4:**
You don't need cooking sake.
Cheap drinking sake works.
Same bottle, straight from the fridge.

**Slide 5:**
**Swap #2**
Table salt → soy sauce
(for finishing, not seasoning throughout)

**Slide 6:**
A teaspoon of soy at the end
adds salt AND umami AND color.
One ingredient doing three jobs.

**Slide 7:**
**Swap #3**
Chicken stock → dashi
(for light, delicate dishes)

**Slide 8:**
Chicken stock adds weight and fat.
Dashi adds depth without heaviness.
For fish, vegetables, light sauces: dashi wins.

**Slide 9:**
These aren't fusion tricks.
They're flavor principles
applied with different ingredients.

## Carousel 5: The Miso Trick — Add It to Anything for Instant Depth

**Hook (Slide 1):**
One ingredient. One spoonful.
It makes everything taste like
it cooked for twice as long.

**Slide 2:**
Miso is fermented soybean paste.
It's been fermenting for weeks or months.
You're borrowing that time.

**Slide 3:**
**Where to add it:**
Butter for steak.
Salad dressing.
Any braise or broth.

**Slide 4:**
**How much:**
Start with half a teaspoon.
Taste. Add more if needed.
You want depth, not miso flavor.

**Slide 5:**
**The rule:**
If you can taste the miso, you used too much.
If the dish tastes mysteriously better,
you got it right.

**Slide 6:**
Miso + butter = the best thing
you will ever put on roasted vegetables.
Takes 30 seconds to make.

**Slide 7:**
Miso + soy + rice vinegar + sesame oil
= a marinade that works on everything.
Chicken, fish, tofu, mushrooms.

**Slide 8:**
White miso = mild, sweet, versatile.
Start here.
Red miso = stronger, saltier, for braises.

**Slide 9:**
The fermentation does the heavy lifting.
You just have to add the spoonful.
That's the whole trick.
