Yakitori is one of Japan's most complete pub foods — chicken pieces threaded on bamboo skewers, grilled over high heat, available at izakayas, specialist yakitori-ya shops, and street stalls. The technique is minimal; the results, when done correctly, are extraordinary.

## Ingredients

**The chicken:**
- 600g boneless chicken thighs, skin-on
- 6-8 Japanese long onions (naganegi) or thick scallions, cut into 3cm pieces

**Tare sauce (sweet soy glaze — for half the skewers):**
- 4 tbsp soy sauce
- 3 tbsp mirin
- 2 tbsp sake
- 1 tbsp sugar

**Shio seasoning (salt — for the other half):**
- 1 tsp flaky salt or fine sea salt
- 1/4 tsp white pepper
- Lemon or yuzu juice at finish

**Equipment:**
- 12-16 bamboo skewers (soak in water 30 min before use)
- Grill pan, cast iron grill, or outdoor grill

## Method

**1. Make the tare:**
Combine soy sauce, mirin, sake, and sugar in a small saucepan. Bring to a simmer over medium heat, stirring until sugar dissolves. Simmer 5-7 minutes until slightly thickened. Remove from heat. The tare should coat a spoon.

**2. Prepare the chicken:**
Cut chicken thighs into 3-4cm pieces — irregular is fine. Thread onto soaked bamboo skewers, alternating chicken and green onion pieces (3 pieces chicken + 2 pieces scallion per skewer). Don't pack too tightly — leave slight space for heat.

**3. Season shio skewers:**
For half the skewers, season lightly with salt and white pepper just before grilling.

**4. Grill:**
Heat a grill pan or cast iron over medium-high heat until very hot. Brush with a small amount of neutral oil.

Place skewers on the grill. Don't move for 2-3 minutes until the first side caramelizes and releases easily.

Flip. Cook 2 minutes.

**For tare skewers:** Brush with tare sauce. Grill 1 more minute. Brush again. Grill 30 seconds. The tare should be sticky and slightly caramelized with a few darker spots.

**For shio skewers:** Cook without adding sauce until cooked through and slightly charred. Squeeze lemon or yuzu over immediately after removing from grill.

**5. Rest briefly:**
Let skewers rest 1-2 minutes before serving — the interior continues cooking and the juices redistribute.

## Serving

Serve hot, immediately. Eat directly from the skewer. Accompany with cold Sapporo or Asahi beer. The tare and shio versions should both appear — the contrast is part of the experience.

## Notes

- **Skin-on thigh** is essential for proper yakitori. The fat in the skin bastes the meat and creates the char. Breast or skinless thigh produces dry, less flavorful skewers.
- The **charred spots** on tare skewers are not burnt — the sugar in the tare caramelizes at high heat. This char is the flavor. Do not remove it.
- **Specialty cuts:** True yakitori-ya restaurants offer liver (rebā), heart (hāto), skin (kawa), cartilage (nankotsu), and gizzard (sunagimo). Each is a distinct experience. Kawa (skin only) is the most underrated.
