Tamagoyaki is Japan's rolled egg. Each slice reveals a spiral of golden layers — proof of the layering-and-rolling process that makes it. It's softer than a Western omelette, mildly seasoned with dashi, and eaten cold or at room temperature in bentos, or warm straight from the pan.

You need a rectangular tamagoyaki pan (*tamagoyaki-ki*) for clean edges, though a round pan can work with more awkward rolling.

## Ingredients

**Savory dashimaki style (Kansai):**
- 3 large eggs
- 3 tbsp dashi (ichiban dashi or 1/4 tsp Hondashi dissolved in 3 tbsp water)
- 1/2 tsp soy sauce
- 1/2 tsp mirin
- Pinch of salt
- Neutral oil or sesame oil for cooking

**Sweet bento style (Kanto):**
- 3 large eggs
- 1 tbsp mirin
- 1 tsp sugar
- 1/2 tsp soy sauce
- 2 tbsp water

**To serve:** Grated daikon, soy sauce

## Method

**Mix:** Beat eggs with your chosen seasonings until well combined. For the smoothest result, strain through a fine mesh to remove the chalazae.

**Heat pan:** Over medium heat, oil the tamagoyaki pan with a very thin layer. The pan is ready when a drop of egg sizzles on contact.

**First layer:** Pour 1/3 of the egg mixture into the pan. Tilt to spread evenly. Cook until the top is barely set — still slightly moist.

**First roll:** Roll from the far end toward you, as tightly as possible. Push the roll to the far end of the pan.

**Oil again:** Lightly re-oil the empty portion of the pan.

**Second layer:** Pour another 1/3 of mixture into the empty area. Lift the existing roll at the far end and let the new egg flow slightly underneath it. Let this layer partially set, then roll again.

**Third layer:** Repeat with remaining egg. You should have a plump log.

**Shape:** Remove from heat. While still warm, place on a bamboo sushi mat (*makisu*), wrap, and press gently into a rectangular shape. Hold 1-2 minutes.

**Slice:** Cut into 2-3cm rounds. The cross-section should show spiral layers.

## Serve

With grated daikon and a few drops of soy sauce. The daikon cuts the richness.

## Notes

- **Temperature is critical.** Too hot = egg sets before you can roll; too low = egg doesn't set and it slides around. Medium heat throughout.
- The layers separate when sliced? The egg was too dry when you rolled. The top of each layer should still have a slight sheen before rolling.
- The dashimaki style (with more dashi) is softer and more delicate — better eaten warm. The sweet bento style is firmer and packs better cold.
