Korean fried chicken and Mexican tacos share a structural logic: a crunchy, lacquered protein with acidic contrast and a soft wrapper. This recipe does not require you to double-fry anything. The chicken thigh pieces are dredged and fried once, then tossed in gochujang glaze while still hot. The glaze sets as it cools slightly. The tortilla holds everything.

The kimchi slaw is the key — it provides the same function that a traditional *salsa* or pickled jalapeños would, but with fermented Korean funk underneath the crunch.

---

## Ingredients

**For the chicken:**
- 4 boneless, skinless chicken thighs (about 600g / 1.3 lbs), cut into 3cm pieces
- 1 cup (130g) all-purpose flour
- 2 tablespoons cornstarch
- 1 teaspoon garlic powder
- 1 teaspoon kosher salt
- ½ teaspoon black pepper
- 2 eggs, beaten
- Neutral oil for frying (about 2 cups in a 10-inch pan)

**For the gochujang glaze:**
- 3 tablespoons gochujang
- 2 tablespoons honey or rice syrup
- 1 tablespoon soy sauce
- 1 tablespoon rice wine vinegar
- 1 teaspoon sesame oil
- 2 cloves garlic, minced

**For the kimchi slaw:**
- 1 cup kimchi, squeezed dry and roughly chopped
- 1 cup purple cabbage, thinly shredded
- 2 scallions, thinly sliced
- 1 tablespoon rice wine vinegar
- 1 teaspoon sesame oil

**For the sesame-lime crema:**
- ½ cup sour cream or Mexican crema
- 1 tablespoon toasted sesame oil
- Zest and juice of 1 lime
- Salt to taste

**For serving:**
- 8 small corn tortillas, warmed
- Sesame seeds, for garnish
- Extra lime wedges

---

## Instructions

**1. Make the glaze and crema first**

Combine all gochujang glaze ingredients in a small saucepan. Simmer over medium-low for 3–4 minutes until slightly thickened. Set aside.

Combine all crema ingredients, mix well. Refrigerate until needed.

**2. Make the kimchi slaw**

Combine kimchi, purple cabbage, scallions, rice wine vinegar, and sesame oil. Toss to combine. Taste and adjust. Set aside — it can sit for up to an hour.

**3. Fry the chicken**

Mix flour, cornstarch, garlic powder, salt, and pepper in a shallow bowl. Beat eggs in a second bowl.

Heat 2 cm of oil in a heavy skillet over medium-high heat until 175°C (350°F) — a pinch of flour should sizzle immediately on contact.

Dip each piece of chicken: egg first, then flour mixture. Press the flour to coat. Shake off excess.

Fry in batches (do not crowd) 4–5 minutes per batch until golden and cooked through. Internal temperature should reach 74°C (165°F). Transfer to a wire rack.

**4. Glaze**

While the chicken is still hot, toss it in the gochujang glaze. Work quickly — the glaze sets as the chicken cools.

**5. Assemble**

Warm the tortillas (directly on a gas flame for 30 seconds per side, or wrapped in a damp towel in the microwave for 30 seconds).

Spread a tablespoon of sesame-lime crema on each tortilla. Top with kimchi slaw, then 3–4 pieces of glazed chicken. Garnish with sesame seeds. Serve with lime wedges.

## The Flavor Logic

Korean fried chicken (*yangnyeom chikin*) is characterized by a thin, ultra-crunchy coating (from the cornstarch in the batter) and a sauce that clings to the surface without making it soggy. The Mexican taco structure — soft wrapper, crunchy filling, acidic contrast — is functionally identical to the Korean tradition of wrapping *ssam* (fried items) in perilla leaves or lettuce with fermented condiments.

The gochujang glaze does triple duty: heat, sweetness (from honey), and fermented depth. The kimchi slaw provides acid and crunch from the same fermentation tradition that produced the gochujang.
