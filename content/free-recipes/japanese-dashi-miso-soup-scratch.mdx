The single most impactful upgrade in Japanese home cooking: make miso soup with real dashi instead of instant granules. Real ichiban dashi takes 15 minutes and the difference in depth, clarity, and flavor is not subtle.

## Ingredients

**Dashi (makes 400ml — double for 4 servings):**
- 5g kombu (a palm-sized piece)
- 10g katsuobushi (dried bonito flakes, loosely packed)
- 400ml cold water

**Miso soup:**
- 400ml ichiban dashi (made above)
- 1.5 tbsp white miso (*shiro*) or red miso (*aka*) or mixed
- 100g soft tofu, cut into 1.5cm cubes
- 1 tbsp wakame seaweed (dried — rehydrates in the hot soup)
- 1 scallion, thinly sliced

## Making the Dashi

1. Place cold water and kombu in a small pot
2. Heat over medium — watch carefully. Remove kombu when the water shows small bubbles at the bottom (approximately 60-70°C). Do not boil the kombu.
3. Bring water to just below a boil (90-95°C — steady wisps of steam, no rolling boil)
4. Add katsuobushi loosely — turn off heat immediately
5. Let steep undisturbed 2-3 minutes
6. Strain through a fine-mesh strainer. Do not press or squeeze the katsuobushi.

The dashi should be clear and golden, with a clean, savory, lightly smoky aroma.

## Making the Miso Soup

1. Return dashi to pot. Add tofu and dried wakame.
2. Heat over medium until just below a simmer (not boiling).
3. **Dissolve miso separately:** Place miso in a small ladle or strainer. Lower it into the hot soup. Use chopsticks to dissolve the miso through the ladle — this prevents lumps and ensures the miso doesn't hit the pot bottom and scorch.
4. **Do not boil after adding miso.** Boiling destroys the delicate volatile aromatics and active enzymes that make miso flavorful. Heat until steaming, then serve immediately.
5. Garnish with scallion.

## Notes

- **Miso amount:** Taste before serving. 1.5 tbsp per 400ml is a starting point — some misos are saltier, some have different intensities. Adjust to your preference.
- **Shiro (white) miso:** Sweeter, lighter, less salty. Good for daily soup.
- **Aka (red) miso:** Deeper, more complex, saltier, slight bitterness. More assertive.
- **Awase (mixed):** Most common — a blend of white and red. Balanced.
- **Tofu choice:** Silken tofu is the classic — it sets gently in the hot soup. Firm tofu holds shape better if you're cooking ahead.
