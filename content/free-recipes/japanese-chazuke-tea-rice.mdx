Ochazuke (お茶漬け) is one of Japan's most honest comfort foods — simple, fast, and made from whatever is on hand. There is almost nothing to it, which is the point.

## What You Need

**Rice:** Freshly cooked or leftover rice, warmed. Short-grain Japanese rice is traditional but any cooked rice works.

**Tea:** Hot green tea (*sencha* or *hojicha*). Some versions use *dashi* broth instead of tea, or a mix of both. The tea should be hot but not boiling — use 80°C water for green tea.

**Toppings (choose 2-3):**
- Pickled plum (*umeboshi*): the classic. Sour, salty, cuts through the richness of the rice.
- *Nori* (dried seaweed), cut into strips
- Pickled *takuan* (yellow daikon) or other *tsukemono*
- Salmon flakes (grilled or smoked salmon, broken into pieces)
- Mentaiko (pollock roe) or tarako
- Sesame seeds
- *Wasabi*: small amount on the side
- *Arare* (small Japanese rice crackers) — adds crunch, traditional ochazuke garnish

## Method

Place warm rice in a bowl. Arrange toppings over the rice. Pour hot green tea over everything — about 150-200ml, enough to partially submerge the rice. Eat immediately.

## The Instant Version

Nagatanien and other Japanese brands sell instant ochazuke packets — small foil pouches containing arare, dried nori, seasoning powder, and sometimes dried wasabi. You tear open the packet over rice and pour hot water or tea. These are available at Japanese grocery stores and are extremely practical for late-night meals.

## Notes

- **Dashi ochazuke:** Replace the tea with warm dashi (or half dashi, half tea). This is richer and more savory.
- **The "end of the feast" tradition:** In Japanese restaurant culture, ochazuke is sometimes offered at the end of a long meal to clear the palate and settle the stomach. The gentle, clean flavors after rich dishes serve a similar function to cheese in a Western meal finale.
- **Leftover rice version:** The next day's rice works perfectly — the slightly drier texture absorbs the tea more readily than fresh rice.
