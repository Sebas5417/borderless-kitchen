Real teriyaki (照り焼き) takes its name from the glaze: *teri* (照り) means the sheen or gloss produced by a sugar-reduced sauce; *yaki* (焼き) means grilled or broiled. The glaze is the dish.

## The Sauce

**The correct ratio is 1:1:1:**
- 3 tbsp soy sauce
- 3 tbsp mirin
- 3 tbsp sake

That's it. Nothing else.

The reduction — simmering these three together — concentrates the sugars in the mirin and sake, thickens the liquid, and deepens the color. No cornstarch, no thickener. The natural sugars provide the gloss.

## Method — Teriyaki Chicken Thighs

**Ingredients (serves 2):**
- 2 boneless chicken thighs, skin on
- 3 tbsp soy sauce
- 3 tbsp mirin
- 3 tbsp sake
- 1 tsp neutral oil
- Sesame seeds, scallion to garnish

**Prep:**
Pat chicken completely dry. Score the skin 3-4 times with a knife (prevents shrinking, allows sauce to penetrate). Season with a pinch of salt.

**Sear:**
Heat oil in a skillet over medium-high. Place chicken skin-side down. Press gently with a spatula. Cook 6-7 minutes without moving until the skin is golden and releases cleanly. Flip. Cook flesh side 4-5 minutes.

**Glaze:**
Pour the soy-mirin-sake mixture into the pan over the chicken. It will sizzle immediately. Reduce heat to medium. The sauce will foam and then begin to reduce — about 2-3 minutes. Swirl the pan and spoon sauce over the chicken repeatedly as it reduces. The sauce is ready when it coats a spoon and has the characteristic glossy, slightly sticky consistency.

**Rest 2 minutes.** Slice. Spoon remaining pan sauce over the top.

## Salmon and Tofu Variations

Same sauce ratio. For salmon: sear skin-side down first, add sauce in the final 2 minutes. For firm tofu: pat very dry, brown on both sides first, then add sauce and reduce — tofu absorbs the sauce into the surface as it reduces.

## Notes

- Don't use low-sodium soy sauce for teriyaki — the sodium is part of the flavor balance with the mirin sweetness.
- Sake can be replaced with dry sherry or extra mirin in a pinch, but the alcohol evaporation step is important — sake burns off during reduction, leaving only its flavor compounds.
- Leftover teriyaki sauce (reduced, cooled) keeps refrigerated for 2 weeks. It thickens further as it cools — thin with a little water if needed.
