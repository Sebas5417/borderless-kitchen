Tonkatsu is Japan's answer to schnitzel — same concept, different breadcrumb, completely different result. Panko (Japanese large-flake breadcrumbs) creates a crust that shatters into individual crispy flakes rather than forming a dense coating. The double fry drives off the moisture that makes the crust go soft.

## Ingredients

- 2 pork loin cutlets (1.5-2cm thick, about 150g each)
- Salt and white pepper
- 2 tbsp all-purpose flour
- 1 egg, beaten
- 1 cup panko breadcrumbs (must be panko — not regular breadcrumbs)
- Neutral oil for deep frying

**To serve:** Tonkatsu sauce (Bulldog brand), shredded raw cabbage, rice, miso soup

## Method

**Prep the pork:** Pat dry. Make 3-4 shallow cuts through the fat cap on each cutlet (prevents curling). Season both sides with salt and white pepper.

**Bread:** Dredge in flour (shake off excess) → dip in beaten egg → coat thoroughly in panko, pressing gently to adhere. The coating should be complete with no bare spots.

**First fry (165°C):** Deep fry in enough oil to submerge, at 165°C. Fry 3-4 minutes until just cooked through (internal 63°C). Remove and rest on a wire rack 2 minutes.

**Second fry (190°C):** Increase oil to 190°C. Return cutlets for 45-60 seconds until the exterior turns deep golden and the crust shatters when tapped. Remove immediately.

**Rest and slice:** Rest 2 minutes. Slice into 3-4cm strips with a downward pressing motion (not sawing — preserves the crust).

## Serve

With tonkatsu sauce, shredded cabbage, and rice.

## Notes

- **Panko is non-negotiable.** Standard breadcrumbs produce a dense, uniform crust that doesn't shatter. Panko creates the textural experience.
- Score the fat cap — without scoring, the fat shrinks faster than the meat and curls the cutlet.
- The double fry: the rest between fries allows moisture to redistribute; the second fry drives it off. A single longer fry at medium-high heat won't achieve the same crispness.
