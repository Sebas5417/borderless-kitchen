Arrabbiata — chili, garlic, tomato, olive oil — is Italian food stripped to its structure. Tteokbokki is Korean street food defined by the same structure: chili paste, savory base, starchy chewy substrate. Replacing pasta with tteok (cylinder-shaped Korean rice cakes) is less a fusion experiment than a recognition that both dishes are solving the same problem with different ingredients.

The result is less surprising than you'd expect. It tastes like both things at once, which means it tastes like neither — and that's the point.

---

## Ingredients

**Serves:** 2  
**Time:** 25 minutes

- 300g cylindrical tteok (Korean rice cakes) — fresh, refrigerated, or soaked from dried
- 2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
- 4 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
- 1–2 teaspoons Calabrian chili (or ½ teaspoon red chili flakes)
- 1 tablespoon gochujang
- 1 can (400g) whole peeled tomatoes, crushed by hand
- 1 tablespoon fish sauce (or 1 tablespoon soy sauce for vegetarian)
- 1 teaspoon sugar
- Salt to taste

**To finish:**
- Fresh parsley or Korean perilla leaves, torn
- Pecorino Romano or Parmigiano, finely grated (optional)
- Extra olive oil for drizzling

---

## Instructions

**1. Prep the tteok**

If using refrigerated tteok: soak in warm water for 10 minutes to loosen them (they tend to stick together). Drain.

If using fresh tteok: no prep needed.

If using dried tteok: follow package instructions (typically 30 minutes in cold water).

**2. Make the arrabbiata base**

Heat the olive oil in a wide skillet over medium heat. Add the sliced garlic. Cook gently — the garlic should sizzle but not brown — 2 minutes.

Add the Calabrian chili (or chili flakes) and the gochujang. Stir and cook 1 minute until fragrant. The gochujang will darken slightly.

Add the crushed tomatoes and fish sauce. Bring to a simmer. Season with sugar and a pinch of salt. Simmer 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce has thickened slightly.

**3. Cook the tteok in the sauce**

Add the drained tteok to the sauce. Stir to coat. Cook over medium heat, stirring regularly, 5–7 minutes. The tteok will absorb sauce and become tender — they should be soft throughout but still slightly chewy in the center.

If the sauce becomes too thick, add 2–3 tablespoons of water. The final consistency should coat the tteok but still be saucy.

**4. Finish and serve**

Taste. Adjust salt, chili, and sweetness. The final dish should be spicy, savory, and bright from the tomato.

Divide into bowls. Tear fresh parsley or perilla over the top. Add a drizzle of good olive oil. Serve with Pecorino if using.

## The Tteok

Tteok (떡) are Korean rice cakes made from glutinous or non-glutinous rice flour, shaped into cylinders for tteokbokki. They have a dense, chewy texture that holds up to simmering better than pasta — they don't overcook as quickly and their starch thickens the sauce naturally.

Find them at Korean grocery stores (H Mart, Hana, any Korean supermarket) refrigerated, frozen, or dried. The refrigerated version has the best texture. They keep for 1–2 weeks in the fridge or months in the freezer.

## The Flavor Logic

Arrabbiata's chili-garlic-tomato structure maps directly onto *tteokbokki* sauce's gochujang-garlic-tomato-sugar structure. Both are built around a chili paste (Calabrian vs gochujang), alliums, and an acidic tomato base.

The key shifts in this recipe:
- Gochujang provides the Korean fermented chili character alongside (or instead of) Calabrian chili
- Fish sauce provides the same invisible savory depth that a good Parmigiano rind would add to an Italian tomato sauce
- Tteok replaces pasta — same function (starchy, absorptive substrate), completely different texture
