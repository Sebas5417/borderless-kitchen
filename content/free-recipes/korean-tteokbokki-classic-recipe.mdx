## Ingredients

- 300g cylindrical rice cakes (garae-tteok, fresh or refrigerated — if dried, soak 1 hour)
- 200g fishcake (eomuk), cut into 3-4cm pieces
- 2 hard-boiled eggs, peeled
- 2 scallions, cut into 3cm pieces

**Sauce:**
- 3 tbsp gochujang
- 1 tbsp gochugaru
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 2 tbsp sugar
- 1 tbsp corn syrup or honey (optional — adds gloss)
- 300ml anchovy broth (or water)

**Anchovy broth (quick):** Simmer 5 dried anchovies (heads removed) and 1 small piece of kelp in 400ml water for 10 minutes. Strain.

## Method

**Soak the rice cakes:** If using refrigerated or dried rice cakes, soak in cold water 30-60 minutes until pliable. Drain.

**Make the sauce:** In a wide pan or shallow pot, combine anchovy broth, gochujang, gochugaru, soy sauce, and sugar. Stir to combine. Bring to a boil.

**Add rice cakes and fishcake:** Add soaked rice cakes and fishcake pieces to the simmering sauce. Stir.

**Reduce:** Cook over medium heat, stirring frequently, 8-10 minutes until the sauce thickens and the rice cakes are coated and tender all the way through. The sauce should reduce to a thick, glossy consistency — not watery.

**Add eggs** in the final 3 minutes — they will absorb the sauce color.

**Add scallions** in the last minute. Serve immediately.

## Notes

- **Fresh rice cakes** don't need soaking and cook faster. Refrigerated rice cakes need soaking or they stay firm in the center.
- The sauce ratio: gochujang provides the fermented base flavor; gochugaru adds heat and fresh pepper flavor; sugar balances the heat. Adjust the sugar to your taste — some recipes use twice as much sugar.
- **Corn syrup** (mulyeot) adds glossiness and is standard in Korean street food tteokbokki. Honey works as a substitute.
- Leftovers: the rice cakes continue to absorb the sauce and will be even more flavorful the next day, though they lose some chewiness.
