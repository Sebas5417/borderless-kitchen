The rice must be warm when you shape. Cold rice breaks. Hot rice burns your hands and gets sticky. The window is about 10-15 minutes after cooking.

## What You Need

- 3 cups short-grain Japanese rice, cooked and kept warm
- 1 tsp salt (for your hands)
- 8 strips nori (each roughly 3cm × 15cm)
- A small bowl of water

**Choose your fillings (about 1 tbsp per onigiri):**
- **Sake (salmon):** cooked salmon, skin removed, flaked and seasoned
- **Tuna mayo:** canned tuna + Japanese mayonnaise (Kewpie) + pinch of soy sauce
- **Umeboshi:** 1 umeboshi plum, pit removed, per onigiri
- **Tarako:** salted pollock roe, small spoonful
- **Shio kombu:** salt-pickled kelp strips — no preparation needed
- **Okaka:** katsuobushi (bonito flakes) + soy sauce, mixed

## How to Make It

**Wet and salt your hands:** Dip hands in water, then rub with a little salt. This prevents sticking and adds seasoning.

**Take a portion:** Scoop about ½ cup warm rice into one palm. Flatten gently into a rough round.

**Add filling:** Place about 1 tbsp filling in the center. Fold rice over it.

**Shape the triangle:** Cup both hands in a V-shape around the rice. Apply gentle, even pressure while rotating the ball. Press with the palm of one hand to create a flat face, two other faces form naturally. Repeat until you have a triangle with three flat faces and defined edges. It should hold its shape without falling apart — but NOT be compressed into a hard ball.

**Wrap with nori:** Wrap a nori strip around the base, or wrap the full bottom and press to adhere. For konbini-style onigiri, wrap entirely in nori.

**Serve immediately** or wrap in plastic wrap for up to 4 hours. The nori will soften over time — which some people prefer.
