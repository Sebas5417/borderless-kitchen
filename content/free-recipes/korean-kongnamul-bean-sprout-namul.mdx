Kongnamul (콩나물) is Korea's most elemental side dish — three ingredients, ten minutes, and a banchan that belongs on every Korean table. The technique is exactly the same every time; the only variable is how crunchy you want the sprouts.

## Ingredients

- 300g soybean sprouts (*kongnamul*, not mung bean sprouts — the larger, yellow-headed variety)
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- 1 tsp soy sauce
- 1 clove garlic, minced (optional for milder version)
- 1 scallion, thinly sliced
- 1 tsp sesame seeds
- Salt

**Note on sprouts:** Soybean sprouts have a larger yellow head and a longer tail than mung bean sprouts. The head has a distinct nutty flavor. Mung bean sprouts are thinner, more delicate, shorter, and produce a slightly different banchan (*sukju namul*). Both work; they are different dishes.

## Method

**1. Blanch:** Bring a pot of salted water to a boil. Add the soybean sprouts. Return to boil. Cook:
- **2-3 minutes** for slightly crunchy (recommended — the sprout retains bite)
- **4-5 minutes** for tender

**2. Drain and rinse** under cold water immediately to stop cooking. Squeeze out as much water as possible.

**3. Season:** In a bowl, combine blanched sprouts with sesame oil, soy sauce, garlic (if using), and a pinch of salt. Toss well to coat evenly.

**4. Finish:** Add scallion and sesame seeds. Toss once more.

Serve at room temperature or cold. Keeps refrigerated 3-4 days.

## Notes

- The **sesame oil is the flavor** — don't reduce it. It should coat the sprouts.
- For bibimbap: this is one of the five standard toppings. Make a larger batch and use it all week.
- Spicy version: add ½ tsp gochugaru (chili flakes) with the seasoning.
- Do not skip the cold rinse — warm sprouts continue cooking in their own heat and will turn mushy.
