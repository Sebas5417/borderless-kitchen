## Ingredients

**Beef:**
- 500g rib-eye or sirloin, partially frozen (makes slicing thin easier)

**Marinade:**
- 4 tbsp soy sauce
- 2 tbsp sesame oil
- 2 tbsp sugar (or honey)
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tsp grated ginger
- 100g Asian pear (or Korean pear *bae*), grated — this is the tenderizer
- 1 tbsp mirin (optional)
- 1 tsp sesame seeds
- 2 scallions, finely sliced

**To serve:** Scallion, sesame seeds, lettuce wraps (optional)

## Method

**Slice the beef:** While partially frozen, slice across the grain into 2-3mm slices. If the butcher offers pre-sliced bulgogi beef, use that.

**Marinate:** Combine all marinade ingredients. Add beef slices. Toss well to coat every slice. Marinate 30 minutes at room temperature or up to 4 hours refrigerated. Do not marinate longer — the pear enzymes will start breaking down the texture too much.

**Cook:** Heat a cast iron pan or heavy skillet over high heat until very hot. Add a small amount of neutral oil. Work in batches — don't crowd (crowding steams instead of sears). Add beef in a single layer. Cook 1-2 minutes without stirring until the bottom is caramelized. Flip once. Cook 30-60 seconds more.

**The caramelized edges** are where the soy sugar has reduced and charred slightly — this is the flavor goal. If the pan isn't hot enough, the beef releases moisture and steams.

**Repeat** with remaining beef.

Garnish with scallion and sesame seeds. Serve with rice.

## Lettuce Wrap Version

Serve with red leaf lettuce, sesame leaves (perilla), ssamjang, sliced garlic, and sliced green chili. Wrap a few pieces of bulgogi in a lettuce leaf with ssamjang.

## Notes

- **Asian pear substitutes:** Kiwi (very potent — use 1/4 kiwi and reduce marinade time to 15-20 minutes), or regular apple (milder, less effective).
- Day-old rice is best here — the dry rice absorbs the bulgogi juices better.
- The marinade works equally well on chicken thighs (slice thinly), pork shoulder (slice thinly), or firm tofu.
