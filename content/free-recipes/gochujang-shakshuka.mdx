Shakshuka and gochujang are structurally compatible. Shakshuka needs: heat (gochujang has it), fermented depth (gochujang has it), tomato as the base (no change), aromatics like garlic and onion (no change). The swap is precise rather than creative — gochujang does what harissa does with its own flavor profile, and the result is clearly shakshuka but clearly Korean.

Serve with warm pita, crusty bread, or — better yet — a bowl of short-grain rice.

## Ingredients

- 4-6 eggs
- 1 can (400g) crushed tomatoes
- 1 medium white onion, diced
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 red bell pepper, diced
- 2-3 tablespoons gochujang (start with 2, adjust for heat preference)
- 1 tablespoon soy sauce
- 1 tablespoon sesame oil
- 1 teaspoon gochugaru (optional — adds more heat and color)
- 1/2 teaspoon cumin
- Salt to taste
- 2 tablespoons neutral oil

**To serve:** Sliced green onion, sesame seeds, fresh cilantro (optional), feta cheese crumbled over (works surprisingly well with the gochujang — the brininess plays well), warm bread or rice

## Instructions

**1. Build the base.** Heat neutral oil in a large skillet or cast iron pan over medium heat. Add the diced onion and cook until soft and beginning to brown at the edges, about 8-10 minutes. Add the red bell pepper and cook 3 minutes more.

**2. Add aromatics and gochujang.** Add the garlic and cook 1 minute until fragrant. Add the gochujang and stir into the vegetables — cook for 1-2 minutes, letting it caramelize slightly against the pan. Add gochugaru if using. Add cumin.

**3. Add tomatoes.** Pour in the crushed tomatoes. Add soy sauce. Stir to combine. Season with salt. Bring to a simmer, then reduce heat and cook 8-10 minutes until the sauce thickens slightly and the flavors meld. Taste and adjust gochujang, salt, and soy.

**4. Poach the eggs.** Make small wells in the sauce using the back of a spoon. Crack an egg into each well. Cover the pan with a lid. Cook 4-6 minutes until the whites are set but the yolks are still runny (or longer if you prefer fully set yolks).

**5. Finish.** Drizzle sesame oil over. Scatter green onion and sesame seeds. Add crumbled feta if using. Bring the pan directly to the table.

## Variations

**Tofu shakshuka:** Replace eggs with firm tofu cut into 3cm cubes. Nestle the tofu into the sauce instead of cracking eggs; cook 5 minutes covered until the tofu absorbs the sauce. Finish identically.

**Doenjang version:** Replace 1 tablespoon of gochujang with 1 tablespoon doenjang for a deeper, more fermented, less spicy base. The flavor shifts toward the savory-umami register rather than the spicy one. Excellent for people who want the Korean flavor without significant heat.

**Over rice:** Standard in a Korean fusion context. The sauce and egg over a bowl of short-grain rice, topped with sesame and green onion, makes a complete meal in 30 minutes.

## Notes

The key technique is the gochujang caramelization step — cooking the paste in the oil with the vegetables for 1-2 minutes before adding the tomatoes. This blooms the gochujang and develops a deeper flavor than simply adding it to the tomatoes. Don't skip it.

Gochujang brands vary significantly in heat level. Bibigo and Haechandle are mild-to-medium; O'Food is spicier. Taste before adding the second tablespoon.
