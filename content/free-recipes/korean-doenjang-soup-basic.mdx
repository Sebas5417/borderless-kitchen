Doenjang guk (된장국) is the everyday Korean soup — lighter in body than doenjang jjigae (which is a stew), but built on the same fermented paste that defines Korean cooking. This is the soup at the breakfast table, the lunch table, the dinner table.

## Ingredients

**The broth:**
- 600ml water
- 5-6 dried anchovies (myeolchi), heads and guts removed
- 1 piece dried kelp (dashima), about 5x5cm

**The seasoning:**
- 2 tbsp doenjang (Korean fermented soybean paste — not Japanese miso)
- 1 tsp gochugaru (optional — for heat)
- 1 tsp minced garlic

**The vegetables:**
- 150g soft or silken tofu, cubed
- 1/2 medium zucchini (hobak), sliced into half-moons
- 2 shiitake mushrooms, sliced (or 4-5 dried mushrooms, rehydrated)
- 2 scallions, cut into 3cm pieces

**Garnish:**
- Additional scallion, sesame oil (1/2 tsp)

## Method

**1. Make the anchovy broth:**
Combine water, dried anchovies, and kelp in a pot. Bring to a simmer over medium heat. Simmer 10 minutes. Remove anchovies and kelp with tongs or a strainer.

*Shortcut:* Use 600ml dashi instead of making anchovy broth from scratch.

**2. Add the doenjang:**
Dissolve doenjang in a small amount of broth in a small bowl before adding to the pot — this prevents lumps. Add the dissolved paste to the pot. Add minced garlic and gochugaru if using.

**3. Add the vegetables:**
Add zucchini and mushrooms to the simmering broth. Cook 4-5 minutes until the zucchini is just tender.

**4. Add the tofu:**
Add tofu cubes gently. Simmer 2-3 minutes. Tofu doesn't need long — just enough to heat through and absorb a little broth.

**5. Add scallions:**
Add scallions in the final 1 minute of cooking.

**6. Taste and serve:**
Taste the broth — doenjang varies in saltiness. Adjust with a small amount of salt if needed (not more doenjang). Drizzle with sesame oil before serving.

## Tips

**Use Korean doenjang, not Japanese miso:** Doenjang is stronger, earthier, and less sweet than Japanese miso. It has a different flavor profile — more fermented, more pungent, more complex. The dishes are distinct.

**Don't boil aggressively after adding doenjang:** Simmer gently. Strong boiling drives off the nuanced fermented flavors.

**The broth base matters:** Anchovy broth gives the soup its proper Korean character. Dashi is a decent substitute; plain water results in a flatter soup.

**Other additions:** Potato (cubed, added with zucchini), clams (add in the last 5 minutes), dried shrimp, or radish (daikon) cubed and added early.

## Serving

Serve hot in individual bowls alongside rice. This soup is eaten throughout the meal — a few spoonfuls of soup, some rice, some banchan, back to soup. It's not consumed all at once before the meal.
