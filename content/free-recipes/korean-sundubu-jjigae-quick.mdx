Sundubu jjigae (순두부찌개) is one of the most comforting things in the Korean repertoire: silken tofu in a vivid red broth that gets its heat from gochugaru and its depth from anchovy and clam. The version below uses readily available ingredients and cooks in under 20 minutes.

## Ingredients

- 1 tube (350g) soft silken tofu (*sundubu*) or 1 package
- 150g small clams, scrubbed (or 100g baby clams canned in brine, drained)
- 100g ground pork or sliced pork belly (or omit for seafood-only)
- 2 tbsp gochugaru (Korean red pepper flakes)
- 1 tbsp sesame oil
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 300ml anchovy broth (1 tsp dashi powder + 300ml water, or proper anchovy stock)
- 1 tbsp fish sauce
- 1 tsp soy sauce
- Salt to taste
- 2 eggs
- 2 scallions, sliced

## Method

**Step 1 — Bloom the gochugaru:** Heat sesame oil in a stone pot (*dolsot*) or small heavy pot over medium heat. Add pork, cook 1-2 minutes. Add garlic, cook 30 seconds. Add gochugaru. Stir and cook for **1 full minute** — the gochugaru fries in the oil and the color deepens from dull red to a vivid brick red. This step is not optional. Raw gochugaru tastes harsh; bloomed gochugaru tastes rich and complex.

**Step 2 — Add broth:** Pour in the anchovy broth. Add fish sauce and soy sauce. Bring to a boil.

**Step 3 — Add clams:** Add clams. Cook until they open, 3-4 minutes.

**Step 4 — Add tofu:** Squeeze or spoon the silken tofu directly into the pot in large sections. **Do not stir.** The tofu should remain in large, soft, undisturbed pieces. Once you add the tofu, do not stir the stew. Simmer 3-4 minutes.

**Step 5 — Egg at the table:** Remove from heat. Crack a raw egg on top of each serving. The residual heat of the boiling stew will cook the egg to a soft-set. Serve immediately with rice.

## Dolsot Note

A stone pot (*dolsot*) retains heat extremely well — it keeps the stew boiling for several minutes after leaving the heat, which is how the egg sets perfectly without you having to do anything. If using a regular pot, let the stew come to a vigorous boil right before cracking the egg.

## Notes

- Pork is optional — you can make this entirely with seafood (clams + shrimp).
- The three rules: (1) bloom gochugaru in oil, (2) don't stir tofu, (3) egg at the table.
- Serve with rice and one or two banchan — the stew is the centerpiece.
