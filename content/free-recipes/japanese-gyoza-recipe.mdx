## Dough

- 200g all-purpose flour
- 100ml just-boiled water
- Pinch of salt

Mix flour and salt. Pour in hot water gradually while mixing. Knead 5 minutes until smooth. Rest covered 30 minutes.

**Or:** Use pre-made gyoza wrappers (round, 8-9cm diameter). Available at Japanese/Korean grocery stores.

## Filling

- 200g ground pork (not lean — 20%+ fat)
- 200g napa cabbage, very finely shredded
- 1 tsp salt (for cabbage)
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tsp grated ginger
- 2 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tbsp sesame oil
- 1 tsp sake or mirin
- 2 scallions, finely sliced
- White pepper

**Salt the cabbage:** Toss shredded cabbage with 1 tsp salt. Let sit 10 minutes. Squeeze very firmly to remove as much water as possible — wet filling makes the gyoza burst during cooking.

Mix all filling ingredients. Squeeze the cabbage again before adding. The filling should hold together when squeezed.

## Folding (Easy Method)

Place 1 tsp filling in the center of a wrapper. Wet the edge halfway around with water. Fold the dry half over the wet half and press to seal in the center. Make 3-4 small pleats on one side, pressing to seal each pleat against the flat back. The bottom should be flat.

## Cooking (Yaki-Mushi Technique)

1. **Fry:** Heat 1 tbsp neutral oil in a non-stick pan over medium-high. Place gyoza bottom-down in a tight row. Fry 2-3 minutes until the bottom is golden.

2. **Steam:** Pour 100ml water into the pan (it will spit). Cover immediately with a lid. Steam 3-4 minutes until the water is absorbed.

3. **Crisp:** Remove lid. If there's still water, let it evaporate. Add a few drops of sesame oil around the edges. Cook 30-60 seconds uncovered until the bottom re-crisps.

4. **Flip onto plate:** The golden bottom becomes the presentation side.

## Dipping Sauce

- 2 tbsp rice vinegar
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- Chili oil to taste

## Notes

- The crispy bottom is the goal — if the gyoza are uniform color all over, you skipped the right frying step.
- Freeze any uncooked gyoza on a tray (single layer, not touching), then bag. Cook from frozen — add 2 minutes to the steaming step.
- The pleating takes practice — the easy press method is fine and produces good gyoza.
