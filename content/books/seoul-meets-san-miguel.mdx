Two pantries built on the same principle: that time is an ingredient.

Seoul cooks with what fermentation gives you — kimchi, gochujang, doenjang, a long quiet wait for depth. San Miguel cooks with what slow heat gives you — mole, adobo, charred chiles, sauces that take a day and taste like a year. Different climates. Different methods. The same patience.

Thirty recipes at the meeting point of those two kitchens. Not fusion in the costume sense — two traditions arriving at the same answer from opposite ends of the world.
