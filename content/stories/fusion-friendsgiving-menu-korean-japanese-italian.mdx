A Friendsgiving table already breaks the rules: everyone brings something, and nobody expects the menu their grandmother made. That makes it the perfect place for a fusion menu.

The structure of a holiday meal translates easily: something to snack on while people arrive, a centrepiece, starchy sides, something green, something sweet, and leftovers worth looking forward to. Korean, Japanese and Italian cooking have a strong answer for every slot. All thirteen recipes below are free on this site.

---

## While People Arrive

**[Gochugaru elote](/recipes/gochugaru-elote-korean-mexican-corn).** Mexican street corn with gochugaru heat and a gochujang mayo. Corn is already a harvest-table food, so this is the bridge dish: familiar shape, new flavour. 20 minutes on a grill pan or under the broiler.

**[Korean-Italian arancini with kimchi](/recipes/korean-italian-arancini-kimchi-risotto-balls).** Sicilian rice balls with kimchi in the filling and gochujang aioli for dipping. They take time, but the risotto can be made the day before, and cold risotto rolls better anyway.

**[Pajeon (scallion pancake)](/recipes/korean-pajeon-scallion-pancake-recipe).** Cut into wedges for passing around. Twenty minutes, and it's the one to make if you're short on time.

## The Centrepiece

**Keeping the turkey?** Glaze it in the last stretch of roasting with [teriyaki sauce from scratch](/recipes/japanese-teriyaki-sauce-recipe): soy sauce, mirin and sake reduced until glossy. Brush it on near the end only, because the sugar in the mirin burns if it goes on too early.

**Skipping it?** [Gochujang braised short rib tacos](/recipes/gochujang-braised-short-rib-taco) feed a crowd from one pot, and the braise is better made a day ahead. For a vegetarian centrepiece, make [miso mushroom tagliatelle](/recipes/miso-mushroom-tagliatelle): mushrooms and miso stack umami the way a roast does.

## The Sides

**[Maitake butter rice](/recipes/japanese-maitake-butter-rice), in place of stuffing.** Rice cooked with torn maitake mushrooms, soy sauce and butter. It does stuffing's job, savoury, starchy and buttery, with less fuss, and it's made in a rice cooker or a pot.

**[Gamja jorim (Korean braised potatoes)](/recipes/korean-gamja-jorim-braised-potato), in place of mash.** Small potatoes braised in a sweet-salty soy glaze until sticky. They keep for a week, so make them two days ahead.

**[Nasu dengaku (miso-glazed eggplant)](/recipes/japanese-miso-eggplant-nasu-dengaku).** Broiled eggplant with a caramelised sweet miso glaze. It's the side that disappears first.

**[Sigeumchi namul (sesame spinach)](/recipes/korean-sigeumchi-namul-spinach).** The green on the plate: eight minutes, and it can be made the morning of.

---

## Dessert

**[Hojicha panna cotta](/recipes/hojicha-panna-cotta).** Roasted green tea steeped into cream: caramel and coffee notes, lighter than pie after a big meal. It has to set, so it's made the day before by definition.

**[Matcha tiramisu](/recipes/matcha-tiramisu).** For a bigger crowd, and also a make-ahead: tiramisu improves overnight.

---

## The Leftovers

**[Kimchi fried rice](/recipes/kimchi-fried-rice-korean-bokkeumbap)** with leftover turkey or short rib, and the rest of the maitake rice. High heat, one pan, and the best breakfast of the long weekend.

## Make-Ahead Plan

- **Two days before:** shop for the pantry items, make the gamja jorim.
- **The day before:** braise the short ribs (if making them), cook the risotto for the arancini, make the panna cotta or tiramisu, make the teriyaki glaze.
- **The morning of:** sesame spinach, shape the arancini, prep the eggplant and corn.
- **The last hour:** fry the arancini, broil the eggplant and corn, cook the maitake rice, glaze the turkey.
