**Short answer:** for a first donabe, buy a **general-purpose donabe about 9 to 10 inches across**, made of **Iga clay** if your budget allows. Use it for hot pot, braises and soup. If rice is what you're after, a **double-lidded rice donabe** (such as Nagatani-en's Kamado-san) makes fluffier rice with less watching. Season either one before its first use.

---

## What a Donabe Does

Donabe clay heats slowly and holds heat for a long time. The heat rises gradually and stays even, and food keeps cooking after the flame goes off. That's why donabe rice develops a good crust (*okoge*) and why a hot pot stays bubbling at the table.

**Iga clay**, from Iga in Mie Prefecture, is the most prized donabe clay. Most everyday donabe are Banko-yaki, also from Mie. It is porous and handles heat changes well. Nagatani-en, an Iga workshop founded in 1832, is one of the best-known makers outside Japan.

## What Size?

Rice donabe are sold by the **gō**, the traditional Japanese rice measure (about 180 ml, the same as a rice-cooker cup).

- **2 to 3 gō:** two to four people.
- **5 gō:** a family, or rice for leftovers and [onigiri](/journal/onigiri-types-fillings-guide-japanese-rice-balls).

For a general-purpose pot, 9 to 10 inches across serves two to four from a hot pot.

---

## First Use: Seasoning (Medome)

New porous clay should be sealed before its first use. The usual method is to fill the pot about 70 to 80% with water, add a bowl of cooked rice (or a handful of raw rice), and simmer it into a thin porridge for about an hour on low heat. Let the pot cool completely, discard the porridge, rinse and dry it fully. The starch fills the tiny pores and helps prevent cracks and leaks. Always follow your maker's instructions if they differ.

Want to eat the porridge instead? See [okayu, Japanese rice porridge](/journal/okayu-japanese-rice-porridge-guide).

## Care Rules That Prevent Cracks

- **Never heat it empty.** Always have liquid or food in it.
- **The outside bottom must be dry** before it goes on the flame.
- **Start on low to medium heat**, then raise it.
- **Let it cool before washing**, and don't put a hot donabe on a cold or wet surface.
- **Dry it thoroughly before storing** so the clay doesn't grow mold. Don't soak it.
- **Check your cooktop.** Most traditional donabe are made for a gas flame. Use one on electric or induction only if the maker says it's made for that cooktop.
