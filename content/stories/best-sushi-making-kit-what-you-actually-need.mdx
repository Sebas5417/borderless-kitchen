Boxed sushi kits mostly sell the idea of sushi: a bamboo mat, a plastic paddle, some chopsticks and a printed guide. The things that decide whether homemade sushi is good aren't in the box. It's the rice and how it's seasoned, the nori, a knife that cuts a roll cleanly, and fish bought with raw eating in mind.

Here's what to buy, in the order it matters. Everything works for onigiri and rice bowls too, so none of it sits unused between sushi nights.

## The Rice (Most Important)

### Short-grain Japanese-style rice

→ [Shop short-grain sushi rice on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=short+grain+sushi+rice+calrose+premium&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Sushi rice is short-grain japonica rice, which turns sticky and glossy when cooked so it holds together under a slice of fish. Long-grain rice like jasmine or basmati falls apart. Rinse it until the water runs mostly clear, then let it soak before cooking. Brands and grades are compared in our [short-grain rice guide](/journal/best-short-grain-rice-for-japanese-and-korean-cooking).

### Rice vinegar

→ [Shop Japanese rice vinegar on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=mizkan+rice+vinegar&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Sushi rice is seasoned with sushi-zu: rice vinegar mixed with sugar and salt, folded into the rice while it's hot. Buy plain rice vinegar and season it yourself, or buy "seasoned" rice vinegar, which already has the sugar and salt in it. Don't use both, or the rice gets sweet and salty. Why rice vinegar isn't mirin is in [mirin vs rice vinegar](/journal/mirin-vs-rice-vinegar-difference).

### A rice cooker (optional, but it helps)

→ [Shop Japanese rice cookers on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=zojirushi+rice+cooker+3+cup&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Sushi rice can be made in a pot, but a good rice cooker makes it consistent. If they'll make sushi or rice bowls every week, this is the upgrade. Models are compared in our [rice cooker guide](/journal/best-japanese-rice-cooker-for-home-cooks).

## The Wrap and the Tools

### Nori

→ [Shop sushi nori sheets on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=sushi+nori+roasted+seaweed+sheets+gold+grade&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Buy roasted (yaki) nori made for sushi: dark green to almost black, even and crisp, without holes. Cheaper nori is thinner and brownish and tears when rolled. Keep it sealed, because humidity makes it chewy fast. What nori is and isn't is in [kombu vs wakame vs nori](/journal/kombu-vs-wakame-vs-nori-difference).

### Bamboo rolling mat (makisu)

→ [Shop bamboo sushi rolling mats on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=bamboo+sushi+rolling+mat+makisu&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

The one tool every roll needs. Wrap it in cling film for inside-out rolls so the rice doesn't stick in the slats. It's cheap, and buying two lets two people roll at once.

### Rice paddle (shamoji) and a wide bowl

→ [Shop rice paddles on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=shamoji+rice+paddle&tag=borderlesskitchen-20) · [Shop wooden sushi tubs (hangiri) on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=hangiri+wooden+sushi+rice+bowl&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

The vinegar is folded into hot rice with a slicing motion, not stirred, so the grains stay whole. A traditional hangiri is a wide, shallow wooden tub that absorbs extra moisture and lets the rice cool quickly. It's a lovely gift for an enthusiast. For most people a wide glass or ceramic bowl works.

### A sharp knife

→ [Shop Japanese gyuto chef's knives on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=japanese+gyuto+chef+knife+210mm&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

A dull knife squashes a roll and tears fish. A sharp chef's knife is enough. Wipe the blade with a damp cloth between cuts and slice in one long pull instead of pressing down. A long single-bevel yanagiba is the traditional sushi knife, but it's a specialist tool, not a starting point. Which knife to buy first is in our [Japanese knife guide](/journal/japanese-knife-buying-guide-beginners).

## The Table

### Wasabi and pickled ginger

→ [Shop real wasabi (hon wasabi) on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=real+wasabi+japonica+hon+wasabi&tag=borderlesskitchen-20) · [Shop pickled sushi ginger (gari) on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=pickled+sushi+ginger+gari&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Most "wasabi" in tubes and powder is mainly horseradish and mustard coloured green. Real wasabi (*Eutrema japonicum*, formerly *Wasabia japonica*) is milder, sweeter and much more expensive; check the ingredient list if it matters to them. Gari, the thin pickled ginger, is for cleansing the palate between pieces, not for piling on top.

## A Word on the Fish

"Sushi-grade" isn't a regulated term in the US. It's a seller's claim, not an inspection. Fish eaten raw should come from a fishmonger you trust who sells it for raw eating. In particular, wild fish should have been frozen to kill parasites; FDA guidance is −4°F (−20°C) or below for 7 days total; or frozen at −31°F (−35°C) until solid, then stored at −31°F for 15 hours (or at −4°F for 24 hours). Home freezers often don't reach the colder temperature, so buy fish that was frozen by the supplier. Farmed salmon is lower-risk and tuna is exempt from the FDA freezing guidance, but ask anyway.

A beginner's sushi night doesn't need raw fish at all. Cucumber, avocado, tamagoyaki, cooked shrimp, smoked salmon and crab sticks all roll beautifully.

## What to Skip

- **All-in-one "sushi bazooka" kits.** Plastic tubes that push out a log of rice. They make rice cylinders, not sushi, and a bamboo mat is cheaper.
- **Kits sold on their number of pieces.** Twenty plastic gadgets don't add up to a good roll; the rice and nori do.
- **Bulk "sushi" rice of unknown variety.** Buy a named short-grain or medium-grain Japanese-style rice.
