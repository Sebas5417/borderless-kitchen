Galbijjim (갈비찜) — *galbi* (갈비 = short ribs) + *jjim* (찜 = steamed/braised) — is the Korean braised short rib dish served at celebrations: the new year, ancestral rites, weddings, birthdays. It is time-consuming by design — the 3-hour braise is part of the respect shown through cooking for an important occasion.

The result is deeply different from Western braised short ribs: sweeter, with the specific sweetness of Korean soy sauce (ganjang), chestnuts, and jujube (Korean dates); and the addition of Asian pear in the marinade produces an unusually tender texture even at the braising stage.

## The Preparation Steps

### Step 1 — Blood Soak (2-4 hours)

Place raw short ribs in cold water. Soak 2-4 hours, changing the water 2-3 times. The water draws out residual blood and myoglobin from the meat — this produces a cleaner-tasting braise without the gray-brown discoloration that untreated ribs produce.

### Step 2 — Score and Blanch

Score the meat between the bones with a knife (this allows the marinade to penetrate deeper). Blanch in boiling water for 3-5 minutes. Drain and rinse. This removes additional impurities.

### Step 3 — The Marinade

The galbijjim marinade is what distinguishes it from other braised short ribs:
- 100ml soy sauce (regular ganjang or jin ganjang)
- 1 small Asian pear, grated (about 100g) — provides enzymes that tenderize the meat
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tbsp ginger, grated
- 2 tbsp sugar (or brown sugar or dark corn syrup *mulyeot*)
- 2 tbsp sesame oil
- 1 tsp black pepper
- 2 scallions

Toss the blanched ribs in the marinade. Let sit 30 minutes to 4 hours refrigerated.

### Step 4 — The Braise

**Aromatics:** In a heavy-bottomed pot, place the marinated ribs. Add enough water to come halfway up the ribs (about 500ml). Bring to a boil.

**The additions:**
- 1 medium carrot, cut into large pieces
- 1 medium potato (optional — absorbs sauce)
- 8-10 dried jujubes (*daechu*): a uniquely Korean addition that adds a subtle sweetness and herbal note to the braising liquid
- 10-12 chestnuts (fresh or pre-cooked): add richness and texture
- Optional: ginkgo nuts, shiitake mushrooms

**Braise:** Reduce heat to a low simmer. Cover and cook 1.5-2 hours until the meat is very tender. Check periodically — the liquid should maintain a gentle simmer.

**Glaze:** Remove the lid for the final 30 minutes of braising. The liquid will reduce and concentrate, glazing the ribs and vegetables. The final sauce should be thick, sticky, and deeply flavored.

### Adjust Seasoning

After reducing, taste and adjust: add soy if it needs salt, sugar if it needs sweetness, sesame oil at the end.

## The Color

Authentic galbijjim has a specific dark amber-brown color from:
1. The soy sauce caramelizing during the long braise
2. The *mulyeot* (corn syrup) or dark sugar used in the marinade
3. The Maillard reaction on the meat surface during the initial browning (optional but recommended)

---

Galbijjim's position as a celebration dish is embedded in the ingredient list: chestnuts, jujubes, and the ginkgo nuts (in the most formal versions) are not everyday ingredients in Korean cooking. Their presence signals that effort and expense have been made. The dish is culturally coded as an expression of care — which is why it appears at significant occasions rather than Tuesday dinners.
