Two years of testing gochujang against mole, kimchi against salsa, doenjang against adobo — and *Seoul Meets Mexico City* is finally out.

This is Volume II of the Borderless Kitchen series, and it works the same way Volume I did. *Tokyo Meets Tuscany* found the structural overlap between Japanese and Italian cooking — both obsessed with restraint, both built around one good ingredient handled correctly. *Seoul Meets Mexico City* does the same thing for Korea and Mexico, except the overlap here isn't restraint. It's fermentation and fire.

## Why Korean and Mexican Food Actually Belong Together

Korean cooking and Mexican cooking arrived at the same conclusion from opposite sides of the planet: heat needs depth underneath it, or it's just noise. Korea builds that depth with gochujang, doenjang, and years of patient fermentation. Mexico builds it with dried chilies, masa, and slow-built mole pastes. Line the two pantries up side by side and they're solving the same problem with different vocabulary.

That's the premise of the book — not "fusion" in the sense of throwing two cuisines in a blender, but convergence. Finding where a Korean technique and a Mexican technique are already doing the same job, then building a recipe that uses both without diluting either.

- **Master Sauces** — Gochujang Adobo, Sesame Mole, Kimchi Salsa Roja, and three more

Every recipe follows the same Flavor Pairing Matrix logic from Volume I: find the structural equivalent between the two pantries, then swap at the ingredient level instead of stacking flavors on top of each other.
