**Short answer:** start with **Lao Gan Ma Spicy Chili Crisp**. It's the original mass-market jar, crunchy, savory and inexpensive. If you want the tingle of Sichuan pepper, try **Fly By Jing Sichuan Chili Crisp**. If you like it rounder, a little sweeter and garlicky, try **Momofuku Chili Crunch**.

---

## What to Compare

- **Heat:** from mild and savory to genuinely hot.
- **Numbing (*má*):** from Sichuan peppercorn. Some jars have a lot, some none.
- **Crunch:** fried shallot, garlic, soybeans or sesame.
- **Sweetness and savoriness:** sugar, MSG, mushroom powder or fermented black beans.

---

## The Jars

**Lao Gan Ma Spicy Chili Crisp.** From Guizhou, China, and the jar that made chili crisp famous worldwide. Crunchy fried onion, savory, moderate heat, little numbing. Very good value.

**Fly By Jing Sichuan Chili Crisp.** A Los Angeles–based brand whose chili crisp is made in Chengdu with Sichuan ingredients. More numbing Sichuan pepper and a deeper, fermented savoriness. It costs several times more per ounce than Lao Gan Ma.

**Momofuku Chili Crunch.** From David Chang's Momofuku. Garlic- and shallot-forward, with sesame. Its heat comes from three Mexican chiles, and many tasters find it rounder and less fiery than the Chinese jars.

Taste varies from person to person. If you're unsure, buy Lao Gan Ma first: it's cheap enough that you'll keep it even if you find a favorite later.

## What to Put It On

- **Eggs:** fried, scrambled, or spooned over [soy-marinated eggs](/journal/mayak-gyeran-korean-soy-sauce-marinated-eggs).
- **Noodles and dumplings:** stir it into soy sauce and black vinegar for a dipping sauce.
- **Rice:** over plain rice, or [kimchi fried rice](/journal/kimchi-fried-rice-recipe).
- **Italian:** over burrata, pizza or a simple garlic-and-oil pasta.
- **Mexican:** on tacos or elote in place of a dried-chile salsa.

**Stir it before you spoon.** The crunchy bits sink, and the oil floats.
