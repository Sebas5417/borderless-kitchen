Italian home cooking is regional, seasonal and short on ingredients. The best Italian cookbooks teach you to cook that way: to trust a few good ingredients and get the technique right.

Five books are worth owning.

---

## The Foundation

### Essentials of Classic Italian Cooking — Marcella Hazan

→ [Shop on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=essentials+of+classic+italian+cooking+marcella+hazan&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Hazan's books taught Italian cooking to American home cooks, and this one is still the clearest. It combines her first two books. Precise, opinionated and reliable. Her tomato sauce with butter and onion is famous for good reason: three ingredients, one pot.

**Best for:** your first Italian cookbook.

### The Silver Spoon

→ [Shop on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=the+silver+spoon+cookbook&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

First published in Italy in 1950 (as *Il cucchiaio d'argento*), it's the country's best-known home-cooking reference, with over 2,000 recipes. Instructions are brief, so it suits cooks who already know the basics.

**Best for:** breadth and authenticity.

---

## Going Deeper

### Made in Italy — Giorgio Locatelli

→ [Shop on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=made+in+italy+giorgio+locatelli&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

A book by the chef behind London's long-Michelin-starred Locanda Locatelli that reads like a long conversation about food, full of stories about Italy's regions. The recipes are detailed, and the essays explain why Italians cook the way they do.

**Best for:** the cook who wants the stories behind the food.

### Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat — Samin Nosrat

→ [Shop on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=salt+fat+acid+heat+samin+nosrat&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Not an Italian cookbook, but Nosrat trained at Chez Panisse and in Italy, and the book's way of thinking about seasoning and balance is deeply Italian. It makes every other cookbook on this list easier to use.

**Best for:** learning to cook without a recipe.

---

## Where Italy Meets Japan

### Tokyo Meets Tuscany

→ [Get Tokyo Meets Tuscany on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0GY8H2TCQ?tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Our own book. Italian technique and Japanese ingredients turn out to share a lot: a respect for umami, for a few good ingredients, and for doing simple things precisely. Try [miso carbonara](/recipes/miso-carbonara) or [udon bolognese](/recipes/udon-bolognese) for a taste of the book.

**Best for:** the cook who already owns the classics.

---

## Which One First?

- **Starting out:** Marcella Hazan.
- **Already cooking Italian, want range:** The Silver Spoon.
- **Want the why:** Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat, then Locatelli.
- **Want something new:** Tokyo Meets Tuscany.
