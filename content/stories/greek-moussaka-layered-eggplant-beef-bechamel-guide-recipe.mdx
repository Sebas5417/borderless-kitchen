Moussaka in its modern form was created by Nikolaos Tselementes, a Greek chef who trained in France and returned to Greece in the early 20th century to systematize Greek cooking. His 1910 cookbook codified the béchamel-topped version that became internationally standard. Before Tselementes, Greek moussaka was simpler — layers of eggplant and meat, sometimes tomato, without the French béchamel.

The dish exists across the Middle East and Balkans in variations — Lebanese moussaka is a tomato-based stew of eggplant and chickpeas; Turkish musakka is a pan-fried eggplant and meat preparation without béchamel. What the world knows as "moussaka" is the Greek, béchamel-topped version.

## The Three Elements

**Eggplant:** Must be pre-salted to draw out moisture, then roasted (or pan-fried) until soft and golden. Soggy eggplant makes watery moussaka. Dry, slightly collapsed eggplant absorbs the meat sauce and holds its structure when sliced.

**Meat sauce:** Minced beef (or lamb, which is more traditional) cooked with onion, garlic, tomato, and the spices that distinguish Greek moussaka: **cinnamon and allspice**. These spices, added in larger amounts than most Western cooks expect, give the meat its characteristic sweet-spiced depth.

**Béchamel:** Thick French white sauce (butter, flour, milk), enriched with eggs, flavored with nutmeg. The egg addition sets the sauce when baked, creating a solid, sliceable top layer rather than a liquid sauce.

## Pre-Salting the Eggplant

Slice eggplant 1cm thick; salt generously on both sides; let sit 30–60 minutes. The eggplant will release brown, slightly bitter liquid. Rinse and pat very dry before cooking. This step:
1. Removes excess moisture (prevents watery moussaka)
2. Draws out some of the bitter compounds
3. Allows the eggplant to absorb less oil when cooking

**Recipe: Greek Moussaka (Serves 8)**

**Eggplant:**
- 2 large eggplants (about 1 kg), sliced 1cm thick
- Olive oil for brushing
- Salt for pre-salting

**Meat sauce:**
- 500g minced beef or lamb
- 1 large onion, diced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 400g canned crushed tomatoes
- 2 tablespoons tomato paste
- 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
- 1/2 teaspoon ground allspice
- 1/4 teaspoon ground black pepper
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 100ml red wine (optional)
- Salt

**Béchamel:**
- 80g unsalted butter
- 80g all-purpose flour
- 700ml whole milk, warm
- 3 eggs, beaten
- 1/4 teaspoon ground nutmeg
- 100g Kefalotyri or Parmesan cheese, grated
- Salt and white pepper

**Method — Eggplant:**

1. Salt eggplant slices; let drain in a colander 30 minutes. Rinse; pat very dry.

2. Brush slices with olive oil. Roast at 200°C on baking sheets for 20–25 minutes, turning once, until golden and soft. (Alternatively, fry in batches in olive oil.)

**Method — Meat sauce:**

1. Heat olive oil. Cook onion until soft. Add garlic.

2. Add minced meat; cook until browned.

3. Add wine if using; let reduce.

4. Add tomatoes, tomato paste, cinnamon, allspice, black pepper, salt. Simmer uncovered 20–25 minutes until sauce is thick and almost dry. The sauce should hold its shape when spooned — not liquid.

**Method — Béchamel:**

1. Melt butter over medium heat. Add flour; whisk constantly for 2 minutes (making a roux).

2. Add warm milk gradually, whisking constantly to prevent lumps.

3. Cook over medium heat, whisking, until sauce is very thick (coats the back of a spoon heavily). About 5–7 minutes.

4. Remove from heat. Add nutmeg, salt, and white pepper. Let cool slightly.

5. Whisk beaten eggs into the sauce. Add half the grated cheese.

**Assembly:**

1. In a deep baking dish (30x22cm or similar), lay half the eggplant slices.

2. Spread the meat sauce in an even layer.

3. Add remaining eggplant layer.

4. Pour béchamel over everything; spread evenly. Sprinkle remaining cheese on top.

5. Bake at 180°C for 40–45 minutes until top is golden and a skewer through the center comes out hot.

6. **Rest 20–30 minutes before cutting** — moussaka needs time to set or it won't slice cleanly.
