Korean BBQ is an event as much as a meal. The protein cooks at the table, shared between everyone, eaten with lettuce wraps, ssamjang, kimchi, and rice. The cooking is part of the dining experience — the sizzle, the smoke, the act of wrapping and eating immediately.

You don't need a Korean grill table. A cast iron grill pan or skillet produces excellent results. Here is the complete home setup.

---

## The Three Essential Proteins

### 1. Galbi — Korean Short Ribs (갈비)

Galbi is the centerpiece of Korean BBQ — flanken-cut short ribs (cut across the bone into thin, flat strips of bone-on meat) marinated in a sweet-savory-sesame sauce and grilled at high heat.

**The cut:** Flanken-cut (or Korean-style) short ribs — the ribs are cut perpendicular to the bone into 5-7mm thick strips, with 3-4 small pieces of bone in each strip. This is distinct from English-cut short ribs. Ask a butcher or a Korean/Asian grocery store — they'll know exactly what you mean.

**The marinade:**
- 4 tablespoons soy sauce
- 2 tablespoons sesame oil
- 1 tablespoon honey or brown sugar
- 4 cloves garlic, grated
- 1 teaspoon ginger, grated
- ¼ Asian pear, grated (or ¼ kiwi) — the tenderizer
- 1 tablespoon sesame seeds
- Black pepper

Marinate 2-4 hours minimum; overnight is optimal. The pear enzyme tenderizes the beef while the soy and sesame season it throughout.

**The cook:** High heat, cast iron grill pan or actual grill. 2-3 minutes per side — galbi is thin and cooks fast. The sugars in the marinade caramelize quickly; you want those dark edges.

---

### 2. Bulgogi — Marinated Beef (불고기)

Bulgogi is thinly sliced beef ribeye in a similar sweet-soy-sesame marinade. Thinner cut than galbi, cooks faster, works equally well as the beef component of the spread.

**The cut:** Ribeye or sirloin, sliced 2-3mm thin. Freeze the beef for 30 minutes to firm it before slicing, cut against the grain. Or buy pre-sliced "shabu-shabu" beef from an Asian grocery.

**The marinade:** Same base as galbi, but the thinner slices require less time — 20-30 minutes is sufficient; don't exceed 2 hours (the pear enzyme will over-tenderize the thin slices).

**The cook:** Very high heat cast iron, working in batches (crowding produces steam, not sear). 1-2 minutes per side — faster than galbi because the slices are much thinner.

### 3. Samgyeopsal — Grilled Pork Belly (삼겹살)

Samgyeopsal (three-layer flesh = pork belly) is unseasoned pork belly, grilled directly and eaten with salt, sesame oil, and ssamjang. It's the boldest option — pure pork fat and meat without a marinade to moderate it.

**The cut:** Pork belly, sliced 5-7mm thick. If buying from a regular grocery, the thicker-cut pork belly sold for braising works; slice it yourself into strips.

**No marinade.** Samgyeopsal is cooked unseasoned and seasoned at the table — a small bowl of sesame oil with sea salt, and a spoonful of ssamjang for the wraps.

**The cook:** The fat renders and crisps on the grill. Cook 3-4 minutes per side over medium-high heat. The exterior should be golden-to-dark brown; the interior will remain tender from the fat content. Use scissors (kitchen scissors are used extensively in Korean cooking) to cut the grilled belly into bite-sized pieces at the table.

**The eating method:** Take a piece of grilled samgyeopsal, dip briefly in the sesame oil + salt, eat with a spoonful of ssamjang. The clean salt and sesame approach showcases the pork itself rather than a marinade.

## The Setup

### Ssamjang (쌈장) — The Essential Sauce

Ssamjang is the Korean BBQ dipping sauce / wrap sauce. Without it, ssam (lettuce wraps) don't work.

**Basic ssamjang formula:**
- 2 tablespoons doenjang (Korean fermented soybean paste)
- 1 tablespoon gochujang
- 1 teaspoon sesame oil
- 1 clove garlic, minced
- 1 teaspoon honey or sugar
- 1 teaspoon sesame seeds
- 1 green onion, minced

Mix together. Serve in a small bowl at the table. Keeps refrigerated 2 weeks.

The flavor: fermented, spicy, savory, with the earthy depth of doenjang. It bridges all the components in a ssam wrap — the richness of the meat, the crunch of the vegetable wrapper, the acid of the kimchi.

### Ssam — The Wraps

Korean BBQ is wrapped and eaten as ssam:

**Wrappers:** Perilla leaves (kkaennip) and/or butter lettuce or romaine leaves. Perilla has a distinct herbal flavor; lettuce is neutral. Use both.

**Assembly (in order):**
1. Take a leaf in your palm
2. Add a small amount of rice (optional but traditional)
3. Add a piece or two of grilled meat
4. Add a small amount of ssamjang (about ¼ teaspoon)
5. Add a piece of kimchi (optional)
6. Add a thin slice of raw garlic (traditional — makes the bite more assertive)
7. Fold the leaf around everything, eat in one bite

The constraint of fitting it in one bite is intentional. The ssam format is designed so all components arrive in the mouth at once — the fat of the meat, the fermented ssamjang, the crunch of the wrapper, the acid of the kimchi, the sharpness of the garlic.

### The Banchan

Korean BBQ is surrounded by banchan — small shared side dishes. For a home spread, three is sufficient:

- **Kimchi** (essential, always) — see [Kimchi Recipe](/journal/easy-kimchi-recipe-homemade-from-scratch)
- **Spinach namul** — blanched spinach + sesame oil + garlic + soy sauce + sesame seeds
- **Bean sprout namul** — blanched sprouts + same seasoning as spinach

All banchan can be made a day ahead. They improve overnight.

### The Rice

Short-grain white rice, plain. It's the structural neutral element — everything else is intensely flavored. The rice moderates it. Never skip the rice.

## Home Grill Setup

**Best option:** Cast iron grill pan — the ridges create grill marks and elevate the meat slightly above the fat pool. Heat over highest heat for 5 minutes before cooking.

**Next best:** Cast iron skillet — flat, no ridges, but achieves the same high-heat sear. The meat sits in its own rendered fat, which accelerates browning.

**For outdoor grills:** Charcoal (not gas) is closer to the Korean style — the smoke contributes. Use high heat direct heat. The fat will drip and flare; don't panic. A quick flare adds char flavor.

**Ventilation:** Korean BBQ produces significant smoke. Open a window or run a range hood. In an apartment: the cast iron skillet on the stove with maximum ventilation is more controllable than a tabletop grill.

---

## The Order of Cooking

**If cooking all three:**
1. Start with samgyeopsal (takes the longest, needs steady heat to render fat)
2. Cook galbi second while the belly rests (galbi is fast — 5 min total)
3. Finish with bulgogi (fastest — everything else is done, this is the last burst)

Rest 2 minutes between proteins to allow the pan to re-heat.

## The Fusion Angle

Korean BBQ and Brazilian churrasco share the same cultural logic: protein-centered grilling, communal eating, various cuts served in progression, dipping sauces for contrast. The Korean version adds the fermented element (ssamjang, kimchi) that churrasco doesn't have, and the wrap format that converts a protein dish into a contained bite. The Brazilian version extends the protein variety into offal (heart, linguiça) in ways Korean BBQ typically doesn't.

Both are the barbecue tradition formalized into a dining event — not just a cooking method but a social format. Every culture with fire has a version. The Korean expression of it is among the most refined.

**The Italian angle:** Florentine bistecca (T-bone steak, olive oil, lemon) is Italian fire cooking — same principle: excellent meat, minimal seasoning, high heat, lemon and oil instead of ssamjang. The contrast element (acid from lemon vs acid from kimchi) plays the same structural role in both traditions. Different acid; same function.
