If you bought white miso for a single recipe, breakfast is a good way to use the next spoonful. This soup serves two and takes about ten minutes when the dashi is ready. [Download the two-page kitchen card (PDF)](/downloads/kitchen-samples/japanese-pantry-breakfast.pdf) for a miso guide and the complete recipe.

## Tonight: get the broth ready

Set aside 400 ml of prepared kombu dashi. If you want to make stock using kombu and bonito flakes instead, our [miso soup from scratch](/recipes/japanese-miso-soup-from-scratch) walks through the separate stock step. Having the broth ready is what makes the morning version quick.

## In the morning: keep the tofu whole

Soak 2 tablespoons dried wakame in cold water for five minutes, then drain. Cut 150 g silken tofu into roughly 2 cm cubes and slice two scallions. Bring the dashi just to a simmer. Slide in the tofu and warm for two minutes. Add the wakame for another 30 seconds.

Take the pan off the heat. Mix 2 tablespoons white miso with a ladleful of the warm broth in a small bowl. When the paste is smooth, stir it back into the pot. Warm briefly without boiling. Divide the tofu and broth between two bowls, then add the scallions.

## Use the next spoonful for dinner

Mash a tablespoon of white miso into four tablespoons of softened butter. Try a little on grilled corn or roasted vegetables and taste before adding salt. The soup shows how white miso dissolves into broth. The butter lets you taste its sweetness more directly.

White miso is the mild option on the card. Red miso has a deeper flavor, and an awase blend sits between the two. If you already have a darker paste, start with less and taste the broth before adding more.
