Mexican cooking is regional cooking. The food of Oaxaca is not the food of Yucatán, and neither is the Tex-Mex most people grew up calling Mexican. The best cookbooks teach that difference instead of flattening it — and they teach the techniques (toasting chiles, grinding salsa, making masa) that every regional dish depends on.

Five books do that well.

---

## The Approachable Start

### Mexico: One Plate at a Time — Rick Bayless

→ [Shop on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=rick+bayless+mexico+one+plate+at+a+time&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Technique-first and patient. Bayless builds each chapter around one classic dish, explains it, then gives easier and more ambitious variations. The best first book for a cook who wants to understand why a red sauce tastes the way it does.

**Best for:** your first serious Mexican cookbook.

---

## The References

### The Essential Cuisines of Mexico — Diana Kennedy

→ [Shop on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=diana+kennedy+essential+cuisines+of+mexico&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

The book other Mexican cookbooks are measured against. Exacting, opinionated, occasionally severe — and correct. Not a weeknight book, but the one you open when you want to know how a dish is supposed to be done.

**Best for:** the cook who wants the authority.

### Mexico: The Cookbook — Margarita Carrillo Arronte

→ [Shop on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=mexico+the+cookbook+margarita+carrillo+arronte&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Encyclopedic: a huge collection of home recipes from across the country, organised so you can see how one dish changes from region to region. Less hand-holding, enormous range.

**Best for:** breadth — and as a gift that will not be outgrown.

## The Regional Deep Dive

### Oaxaca — Bricia Lopez with Javier Cabral

→ [Shop on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=oaxaca+cookbook+bricia+lopez&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

From the family behind Guelaguetza in Los Angeles. The book for moles, tlayudas and the Oaxacan pantry, written with real warmth. Pair it with our guide to [enchiladas and mole sauces](/journal/enchiladas-mexican-guide-recipe-red-green-mole-sauce).

**Best for:** mole.

---

## The Crossover

### Seoul Meets Mexico City — Borderless Kitchen Vol. II

→ [Get Seoul Meets Mexico City on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0H6VD21M2?tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Full disclosure: this is our book. Thirty recipes where the Mexican pantry meets the Korean one — guajillo and gochujang, mole and doenjang, [barbacoa](/journal/barbacoa-mexican-slow-cooked-beef-guide-recipe)-style slow cooking beside Korean braises. Not a first Mexican cookbook; a book for the cook who already knows the basics and wants a new angle. $19.99 paperback, $9.99 Kindle.

**Best for:** the cook who has Bayless and wants something nobody else on the block has.

## Which One to Buy

| You are… | Buy |
|---|---|
| Starting out | *Mexico: One Plate at a Time* |
| Want the definitive reference | *The Essential Cuisines of Mexico* |
| Want breadth | *Mexico: The Cookbook* |
| Want to make mole | *Oaxaca* |
| Want something new | *Seoul Meets Mexico City* |

Before you cook from any of them, get the tools right — the [Mexican cooking gift guide](/journal/best-mexican-cooking-gifts-for-home-cooks) covers the tortilla press, comal and molcajete.
