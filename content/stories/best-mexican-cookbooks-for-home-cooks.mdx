Mexican cooking includes many regional traditions. Before choosing a Mexican cookbook, decide whether you want a broad introduction, a regional focus or a fusion book that combines Mexican and Korean ingredients and techniques.

## For a broad collection: Mexico: The Cookbook

Margarita Carrillo Arronte's *Mexico: The Cookbook* is part of Phaidon's international cookbook collection. It is an option to explore when you want a broad Mexican home-cooking reference. Check the [publisher's description](https://www.phaidon.com/en-us/products/mexico-the-cookbook) and the exact edition before buying.

A wide reference can introduce many dishes, but choose by the recipes you would use. Read a sample method, make a pantry list and check whether the book gives the guidance you need for unfamiliar ingredients.

## For a regional focus

If you already know what you love cooking, narrow the choice further. Look for a book whose contents match that interest, whether you want to explore a particular region, work with masa or develop a wider salsa repertoire. A specialist book can be more useful than another general collection when it answers a specific cooking question.

Check how a recipe identifies its chiles, how it describes the preparation and whether you can obtain the ingredients locally. Those details affect how often you will use the book.

## For Korean-Mexican fusion: Seoul Meets Mexico City

**This is our book.** *Seoul Meets Mexico City* by Sebastian Dri explores Korean-Mexican cooking across thirty recipes and six master sauces. It is a choice for someone interested in both cuisines, rather than a replacement for a regional Mexican reference.

Start with the [book page](/seoul-meets-mexico-city), then browse the [Korean-Mexican recipes](/recipes/cuisine/korean-mexican). A free recipe preview lets you judge whether the flavors and preparation suit your kitchen. Compare the [Korean cookbook guide](/journal/best-korean-cookbooks-for-home-cooks) if you want to learn more about that side of the pairing.

## Before you choose

- **Experience:** does the recipe explain unfamiliar steps, or assume you know them?
- **Pantry:** which ingredients will you need, and can you use them across several dishes?
- **Equipment:** is your existing setup enough for the recipes you want to try?
- **Time:** do the preparation and cooking fit the days when you actually cook?
- **Purpose:** are you buying a working reference, exploring a region or trying a new pairing?
