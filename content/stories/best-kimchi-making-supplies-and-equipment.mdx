Kimchi is one of the easiest ferments to start with. Salt the cabbage, make a paste, mix, pack, wait. But two things decide whether the first batch works: the right pepper and the right salt. Everything else is convenience.

This is the shopping list for a first batch, in order of importance. For the method itself, follow our [step-by-step beginner's kimchi guide](/journal/how-to-make-kimchi-complete-beginners-guide).

---

## The Two Things That Actually Matter

### 1. Gochugaru (coarse)

→ [Shop coarse gochugaru on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=gochugaru+korean+red+pepper+flakes+coarse&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Non-negotiable. Kimchi's colour, heat and flavour come from coarse Korean red pepper flakes. Crushed red pepper from the spice aisle is not a substitute — it is hotter, full of seeds and turns the kimchi harsh. Buy the coarse grind for kimchi; the fine grind is for sauces. A one-pound bag makes many batches and keeps in the freezer. More in our [gochugaru guide](/journal/gochugaru-korean-red-pepper-flakes-guide).

### 2. Non-iodized salt

→ [Shop coarse sea salt for kimchi on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=korean+coarse+sea+salt+for+kimchi&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Use coarse sea salt or kosher salt — never iodized table salt, which can taste bitter and discolour the kimchi. Korean coarse sea salt is what most Korean cooks use to brine the cabbage, and its large crystals make it hard to over-salt.

## Equipment

### A fermentation container

→ [Shop kimchi containers on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=kimchi+fermentation+container+airtight&tag=borderlesskitchen-20) · [Shop wide-mouth glass jars on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=wide+mouth+glass+mason+jars+quart&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Two good options. **Wide-mouth glass jars** are cheap and let you watch the ferment, but leave room at the top — kimchi releases gas and brine and can overflow. **A dedicated kimchi container** with an inner press plate keeps the cabbage under its brine and the smell out of your fridge; worth it once you are making kimchi regularly. Avoid reactive metal containers.

### Food-safe gloves

→ [Shop reusable kitchen gloves on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=reusable+food+safe+kitchen+gloves&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

You mix kimchi by hand, rubbing the paste into every leaf. Gochugaru stains skin and garlic lingers; chili on bare hands will find your eyes later. Gloves are not optional.

### A very large mixing bowl

→ [Shop large stainless mixing bowls on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=extra+large+stainless+steel+mixing+bowl&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

A napa cabbage takes up far more room than you expect until it wilts in the salt. A large stainless steel bowl lets you salt and mix without spilling.

## Pantry Extras

### Fish sauce

→ [Shop fish sauce on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=fish+sauce&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Adds savoury depth and helps the ferment along. For vegan kimchi, use soy sauce instead.

### Salted shrimp (saeujeot) — optional

The traditional depth-builder, usually found in the refrigerated section of a Korean grocery rather than online. Skip it for the first batch if you cannot find it; fish sauce covers most of the job.

---

## The Two Shortcuts That Ruin a Batch

1. **Substituting the pepper.** Paprika plus cayenne, chili powder, or red pepper flakes do not make kimchi. Buy gochugaru.
2. **Using iodized salt.** Check the label. "Iodized" means the wrong salt.

## The Full First-Batch List

| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| Coarse gochugaru | Colour, heat, flavour — the defining ingredient |
| Coarse non-iodized salt | Brines the cabbage without harming fermentation |
| Container or glass jars | Holds the ferment; leave headspace |
| Gloves | Protects your hands from chili |
| Large bowl | Room to salt and mix |
| Fish sauce | Depth (or soy sauce for vegan) |
| Napa cabbage, garlic, ginger, green onion | From any grocery store |
