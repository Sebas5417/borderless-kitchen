Sundubu jjigae (순두부찌개) is defined by its tofu: uncurdled, undrained soft tofu (*sundubu*, 순두부) — barely set, in large irregular curds that are so soft they require a spoon, not chopsticks. This is not silken tofu from a box, though that works in a substitute. Traditional sundubu is freshly made uncurdled soybean custard, scooped directly from the container into the spicy broth.

The stew is ordered more than any other dish at Korean soft tofu restaurants, which are entire restaurant categories in Korea dedicated to this single dish.

## The Three Rules

**1. Bloom the gochugaru in sesame oil first.**
Before adding any liquid, the gochugaru (Korean red pepper flakes) must be cooked in sesame oil for 1-2 minutes over medium heat. This "blooms" the chili — extracting its fat-soluble color and flavor compounds into the oil. A stew where the gochugaru was added directly to liquid is visibly different: duller red, thinner flavor. The bloomed version is bright, vivid, deeper.

**2. Don't stir the tofu once it's in.**
Sundubu is so soft it breaks with any stirring. Spoon the tofu directly into the hot broth in large chunks. It will break naturally at the table as it's eaten — that's correct. Active stirring turns it into tofu mush.

**3. Add the egg at the table, not in the kitchen.**
The raw egg is cracked into the sizzling stone pot at the moment of service. In a hot dolsot, the residual heat cooks the white but leaves the yolk runny. This is the final textural element — the yolk runs into the broth when broken, enriching it.

## Ingredients (serves 2)

- 1 tbsp sesame oil
- 2 tsp gochugaru (more for spicier)
- 100g pork belly or neck, thinly sliced (or 6 shrimp + 4 clams for seafood version)
- 1 tsp soy sauce (ganjang)
- 1 tsp gochujang (optional for deeper heat)
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 300ml anchovy-kelp broth (or chicken broth)
- 400g sundubu (soft/silken tofu)
- 2 eggs (1 per person)
- Salt to taste
- Scallions for garnish

## Method

**Bloom the gochugaru:**
In a stone pot (dolsot) or heavy pot over medium heat, add sesame oil and gochugaru. Stir constantly for 1-2 minutes until the oil turns vivid orange-red and fragrant. Do not let it burn.

**Add protein:**
Add the pork belly (or seafood). Stir-fry with the bloomed gochugaru 2-3 minutes until pork is lightly cooked.

**Add aromatics and broth:**
Add garlic, soy sauce, and optional gochujang. Add broth. Bring to a simmer.

**Add tofu:**
Spoon the sundubu into the broth in large pieces. Do not stir. Simmer 2-3 minutes until the tofu is heated through.

**The egg:**
Crack one egg directly into each pot/bowl at the moment of service. Serve immediately while the pot is still sizzling. The egg finishes cooking in the residual heat.

## Variations

- **Haemul (해물):** Seafood version with shrimp, clams, squid. No pork.
- **Pork neck (돼지목살):** Richer, fattier than pork belly in the stew
- **Kimchi sundubu:** Add kimchi (chopped, aged kimchi preferred) with the pork for tangier, more complex broth
- **Mushroom (버섯):** Vegetarian version — use kombu-only dashi, add mushrooms instead of protein

## The Stone Pot Service

Sundubu jjigae is traditionally served in a *dolsot* (돌솥) — a thick stone pot that retains heat after removal from the flame. The pot arrives at the table still actively boiling. The egg is cracked into the boiling stew; the residual heat of the stone sets the white over the 2-3 minutes of the meal's beginning.

This is why sundubu jjigae is never served in regular bowls in dedicated restaurants — the sizzle and carry-heat are part of the dish.
