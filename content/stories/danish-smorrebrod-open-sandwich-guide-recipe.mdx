In Denmark, lunch is serious. The smørrebrød culture is specific about what toppings go on which breads, in what combinations, in what order if eating multiple, and how they should be garnished. The rules exist because Danish food culture developed them for good reason — they are matters of flavor compatibility and aesthetic coherence.

Smørrebrød appears at celebrations (birthdays, weddings, Christmas lunches), at dedicated smørrebrød restaurants (the oldest, Ida Davidsen in Copenhagen, founded in 1888, has over 250 different smørrebrød on its menu), and at the simple packed lunch (madpakke) that Danish schoolchildren and office workers have carried for generations.

The foundation is specific: **rugbrød** — dense, dark, sour rye bread, sliced thin. Not toast. Not sourdough. Not baguette. Rugbrød has a specific flavor and density that supports toppings without collapsing. A smørrebrød made on ordinary white bread is not smørrebrød.

## The Bread

Danish rugbrød is a dense, moist, dark rye bread made with whole rye kernels, rye flour, a sourdough starter, and often seeds (sunflower, pumpkin). It is sliced thinly — 6–8mm — and each slice is a small rectangle. The bread is tangy and earthy, the necessary counterpart to the rich toppings.

Rugbrød is available at Scandinavian bakeries and some specialty grocers. In its absence, pumpernickel (German dark rye) is the best substitute — it lacks the moist texture and seeds but provides similar flavor. Rye crackers (like Wasa crispbread) are a different product and not a substitute.

## The Butter

Full-fat Danish butter, applied generously and consistently across the entire surface. Salted is traditional. The butter serves as: moisture barrier (prevents the bread from going soggy), flavor layer, and adhesive for toppings.

## Classic Toppings

**Marineret sild (pickled herring):** The most traditional smørrebrød. Herring pickled in a sweet-sour brine, topped with raw red onion rings, fresh dill, and sour cream. The herring and onion combination is fundamental.

**Røget laks (smoked salmon):** Cold smoked salmon, crème fraîche, capers, red onion, dill, lemon. Always elegant.

**Rejer (shrimp):** Baby shrimp (Nordic cold-water shrimp, smaller and sweeter than tropical varieties) on a lettuce leaf with mayonnaise and lemon. Garnished with fresh dill or cucumber.

**Rullepølse (rolled spiced pork):** Cold rolled pork, aspic jelly, remoulade, raw onion, cress. The most Danish of fillings.

**Leverpøste (liver pâté):** Homemade or commercial liver pâté on the bread, topped with pickled beets, cucumber, bacon.

**Roastbeef:** Thinly sliced cold roast beef, remoulade, fried onions, grated horseradish, watercress.

**Æg og rejer (egg and shrimp):** Sliced hard-boiled egg, mayonnaise, shrimp, caviar or roe garnish, dill.

## The Rules

Conventional Danish smørrebrød follows traditions:
- Fish toppings come first if eating multiple smørrebrød (before meat)
- Garnishes must extend beyond the edge of the bread
- Toppings are piled generously, not minimally
- You eat smørrebrød with a knife and fork, never pick it up

**Recipe: Classic Smørrebrød (Makes 4 pieces)**

**Base:**
- 4 slices Danish rugbrød or pumpernickel, cut into rectangles
- Softened salted butter, for spreading

**Pickled herring smørrebrød:**
- 2 pieces pickled herring (from a jar), cut into strips
- 1/4 small red onion, cut into thin rings
- 2 tablespoons sour cream
- Fresh dill
- Capers

**Smoked salmon smørrebrød:**
- 4 thin slices smoked salmon
- 2 tablespoons crème fraîche
- 1 tablespoon capers
- 2 thin slices lemon
- Fresh dill

**Shrimp smørrebrød:**
- Butter lettuce leaf
- 100g cold-water shrimp (small, cooked and peeled)
- 2 tablespoons good mayonnaise
- Lemon
- Fresh dill
- Optional: fish roe for garnish

**Liver pâté smørrebrød:**
- 2 slices Danish liver pâté (or chicken liver pâté)
- 4 slices pickled beets
- 2 strips bacon, cooked crispy
- Fresh cucumber slices

**Method:**

1. Spread butter generously and evenly on each slice of rugbrød, right to the edges.

2. **Herring:** Layer herring strips over the bread. Add sour cream in small dollops. Top with red onion rings, capers, and a generous amount of fresh dill.

3. **Salmon:** Drape salmon over bread in overlapping layers. Add small dollops of crème fraîche. Garnish with capers, a rolled lemon slice, and dill.

4. **Shrimp:** Place a lettuce leaf on buttered bread. Pile shrimp in a mound. Add a dollop of mayonnaise. Garnish with lemon and dill.

5. **Liver pâté:** Spread a thick layer of liver pâté. Arrange beet slices overlapping. Add bacon strips. Finish with cucumber.

Serve immediately on individual plates with a knife and fork.
