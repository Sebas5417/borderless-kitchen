**Short answer:** start with **ancho and guajillo**. Together they make most red sauces, adobos and enchilada sauces. Add **pasilla** for moles and darker sauces, **chipotle** for smoke, and **árbol** when you want real heat.

---

## Side by Side

| Chile | Fresh form | Heat | Flavor | Classic uses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ancho | Poblano | Mild | Sweet, raisin-like, rich | Red sauces, enchilada sauce, mole, adobo |
| Guajillo | Mirasol | Mild to medium | Bright, tangy, berry-like | Pozole, birria, salsas, adobo, marinades |
| Pasilla | Chilaca | Mild to medium | Dark, earthy, herbal, a little bitter | Mole, dark salsas, soups |
| Chipotle | Jalapeño (smoked) | Medium | Smoky | Adobo, salsas, braises |
| Árbol | Árbol | Hot | Clean, sharp heat | Hot salsas, chili oil |

---

## Ancho

The dried poblano: wide, wrinkled and dark red-brown. The sweetest of the group, with a dried-fruit depth that gives red sauces their body. If you buy only one dried chile, buy ancho.

→ [Shop dried ancho chiles on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=dried+ancho+chiles&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

## Guajillo

Long, smooth and shiny brick-red. Tangier and brighter than ancho, and the backbone of birria, red pozole and many [barbacoa](/journal/barbacoa-mexican-slow-cooked-beef-guide-recipe) recipes. Ancho and guajillo blended together is the most common pairing in Mexican red sauces.

→ [Shop dried guajillo chiles on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=dried+guajillo+chiles&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

## Pasilla

Long, thin and nearly black. Earthy and herbal rather than sweet; one of the chiles in many moles.

**A labelling trap:** in some US stores, especially in California, fresh poblanos and dried anchos are sold as "pasilla". A true dried pasilla is long and thin, not wide and heart-shaped.

→ [Shop dried pasilla chiles on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=dried+pasilla+negro+chiles&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

## Chipotle

Smoke-dried jalapeño. Sold dried, or canned "in adobo" sauce. The canned version is the easiest way to add smoke to a sauce.

→ [Shop chipotles in adobo on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=chipotle+peppers+in+adobo+sauce&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

## Árbol

Small, slim and bright red. Much hotter than the others. Use a few for heat alongside the milder chiles that carry the flavor.

→ [Shop chiles de árbol on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=chile+de+arbol+dried&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

## How to Buy and Use Them

- **Buy pliable chiles.** Good dried chiles bend like leather. Brittle, dusty chiles are old and have lost their flavor.
- **Store them** airtight, away from light. They keep for months, longer in the freezer.
- **Toast, then soak.** Wipe them clean, stem and seed them, and toast a few seconds per side on a dry [comal or skillet](/journal/comal-vs-cast-iron-skillet) until fragrant. Don't let them blacken, or the sauce turns bitter. Then soak in hot water for about 15–20 minutes and blend.
- **Grind** the soaked chiles with garlic and spices in a blender, or a [molcajete](/journal/molcajete-vs-mortar-and-pestle) for a coarser salsa.
