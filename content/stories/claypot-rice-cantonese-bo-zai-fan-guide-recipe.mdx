Bo zai fan (煲仔飯, also romanized as "pot rice" or "claypot rice") is a Cantonese preparation that makes an everyday ingredient — raw rice — into something that requires specialized equipment, careful timing, and produces an entirely different textural experience from steamed rice.

The claypot is essential, not interchangeable. The porous clay absorbs heat and distributes it slowly and evenly around the sides and bottom, creating the conditions for the crust (*fan jiao*) without scorching the top layer of rice.

---

## The Crust (Fan Jiao, 飯焦)

The crust is not a byproduct of inattention. In Cantonese claypot rice culture, the *fan jiao* is the most prized element of the bowl — the golden-brown layer of rice that forms directly against the claypot wall, slightly chewy, with a toasted, nutty character from the Maillard reaction against the hot clay.

At dai pai dongs (open-air food stalls) in Hong Kong, claypot rice is served without fully mixing the contents — the top is the pristine topping layer, and the crust at the bottom is revealed when the rice is scooped. Diners eat from the top down, then scrape the crust off the bottom with a spoon.

---

## The Claypot

A small individual clay cooking pot (*sand pot* or *sha guo* — sand pot — in Mandarin) with a lid. Diameter roughly 15–18cm for one serving. Clay pots are available at Chinese grocery stores and restaurant supply shops.

**New claypot seasoning:** Before first use, soak the claypot in water overnight. Then rub the outside with neutral oil; let dry. This prevents cracking on first use.

**Heat rule:** Claypots cannot withstand sudden temperature changes. Start over medium-low heat; increase gradually. Never place a hot claypot on a cold surface.

---

## The Classic Toppings

The most traditional topping combination for bo zai fan:

**Lap cheong (臘腸, Chinese dried sausage):** Sweet, slightly smoky, firm pork sausage in deep red casings. Sliced thin diagonally; placed over the rice before the final cooking phase. The fat from the sausage renders and drips into the rice below.

**Lap yuk (臘肉, Chinese preserved pork belly):** Air-dried cured pork belly with a soy-sweet cure. Sliced thin; layered alongside the sausage.

**Preserved duck (臘鴨腿, laap aap):** Preserved duck leg — a richly flavored, funky, dried duck preparation. Less common but deeply traditional.

Other common toppings: steamed chicken (bone-in pieces), eel with black bean sauce, minced pork with salted fish, beef.

---

## The Soy Sauce Drizzle

Immediately after the claypot is taken off the heat (and before serving), the lid is lifted and a mixture of dark soy sauce and light soy sauce (sometimes with a small amount of sesame oil and sugar) is drizzled directly over the cooked rice. The hot claypot and rice absorb and lightly cook the soy sauce.

**Standard drizzle ratio:** 1 tablespoon dark soy sauce + 1 tablespoon light soy sauce + a few drops of sesame oil + ½ teaspoon sugar. Warm together briefly if desired.

The soy sauce adds a caramelized, savory finish to the top layer of rice.

---

## The Complete Recipe

**Serves:** 1–2 (one claypot per person in restaurants; two-person claypots exist)
**Time:** 40 minutes

### Per claypot (1 serving):

- 150g (¾ cup) jasmine rice, washed and rinsed until water runs clear
- 200ml water (1:1.3 ratio, or slightly less water than usual as the claypot retains heat more than a rice cooker)
- 2–3 pieces lap cheong (Chinese sausage), sliced thin diagonally
- 2–3 slices lap yuk (preserved pork belly), if available
- 1 teaspoon neutral oil (for the inside of the claypot — prevents sticking)
- Soy sauce drizzle (above)
- Optional: 1 egg (added in the final 5 minutes for a soft-cooked center)
- Green onion, sliced, for garnish

### Method

1. **Prep:** Rub the inside of the claypot with neutral oil. Add washed rice and water. Let soak 15–20 minutes.

2. **Initial cook:** Place claypot over low-medium heat. As the water begins to simmer and the rice absorbs most of it, the surface of the rice will show steam holes forming. This takes 12–15 minutes. Do not lift the lid during this time.

3. **Add toppings:** When the water has reduced to the level of the rice surface, open the lid quickly and add the sliced sausage and preserved meat over the rice. Replace lid. Increase heat to medium-high for 3–5 minutes — this is when the crust forms at the bottom. You will hear a crackling, sizzling sound from the bottom; this is correct.

4. **Optional egg:** One minute before done, create a small well in the center of the rice and break in one egg. Replace lid; let the egg set from the residual heat.

5. **Drizzle and serve:** Turn off heat. Remove lid; drizzle the soy sauce mixture over the rice and toppings. Replace lid; let rest 2–3 minutes. Serve in the claypot.

6. **Eat the crust:** After the top layer is finished, use a spoon to scrape the golden crust from the bottom of the claypot — this is eaten separately, with or without the sauce.
