**Short answer:** gochujang is sweet, spicy, salty and deeply savory all at once, because it's a fermented paste of chili, glutinous rice and soybeans. No single bottle in a Western pantry does all four. The closest substitute is a quick homemade paste of red pepper flakes, miso and sugar. The others below each get part of the way.

---

## Ranked: Closest to Furthest

| Substitute | How close | Use per 1 tbsp gochujang | What's missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quick homemade paste (below) | Closest | 1 tbsp | Some fermented depth |
| Red miso + chili flakes + sugar | Close | 1 tbsp miso + ½–1 tsp flakes + ½ tsp sugar | Thinner chili flavor |
| Doubanjiang (Chinese chili bean paste) | Fair | 2 tsp, plus a pinch of sugar | Saltier, less sweet, contains broad beans |
| Sriracha + miso | Fair | 2 tsp sriracha + 1 tsp miso | Garlicky, vinegary, thinner |
| Harissa | Rough | 2 tsp + pinch of sugar | Often caraway- or cumin-scented; fine in marinades |
| Sriracha or chili sauce alone | Last resort | 2 tsp + ½ tsp sugar | No body, no fermented savoriness |

## The 5-Minute Homemade Version

Stir together:

- 1 tbsp **gochugaru** (Korean red pepper flakes), or ½ tbsp crushed red pepper flakes (they're hotter) plus ½ tsp sweet paprika for colour
- 1 tbsp **miso** (red or white) or doenjang
- 1–2 tsp **sugar, honey or rice syrup**
- 1 tsp **soy sauce**
- ½ tsp **rice vinegar**
- a splash of water to loosen

It isn't fermented like the real thing, but it has the same sweet-hot-savory balance, and it works in stews, marinades, bibimbap sauce and [gochujang pasta](/journal/korean-pasta-recipes-gochujang-kimchi-and-doenjang). Taste and adjust: more sugar for tteokbokki, more chili for stews.

## When a Substitute Isn't Good Enough

For dishes where gochujang is the main flavor, like [tteokbokki](/journal/korean-tteokbokki-recipe) or a gochujang glaze, the fermented depth matters and substitutes taste noticeably flat. A tub of gochujang keeps for many months in the fridge once opened, so it's worth keeping one.

Check the heat level printed on the tub (Korean brands usually label it from mild to very hot) and start in the middle if you're not sure.

## Don't Confuse These

- **Gochugaru** is the dried chili flakes, not the paste. See [gochujang vs gochugaru](/journal/gochujang-vs-gochugaru-difference).
- **Ssamjang** already contains gochujang, mixed with doenjang. It's a dipping sauce, not a substitute you'd have when you're out of gochujang. See the [ssamjang guide](/journal/ssamjang-korean-bbq-dipping-sauce-guide).
- **Doenjang** is the unspicy fermented soybean paste. See [miso vs doenjang](/journal/miso-vs-doenjang-difference).
