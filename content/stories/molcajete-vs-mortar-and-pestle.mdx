**Short answer:** a molcajete is a three-legged mortar carved from porous volcanic basalt. Its rough surface tears chiles, garlic and tomatoes into a salsa with body that a blender can't match, but it has to be seasoned before first use. A granite mortar and pestle is smoother and needs no seasoning, and it handles spices, pastes and pesto. If you make salsa and guacamole often, get the molcajete. If you want one all-purpose mortar, get granite.

## Side by Side

| | Molcajete | Granite mortar | Suribachi |
|---|---|---|---|
| Material | Volcanic basalt | Polished or semi-polished granite | Ceramic bowl with ridged inside, wooden pestle |
| Surface | Rough, porous | Fairly smooth, non-porous | Combed ridges |
| Seasoning | Required before first use | None | None |

| Serves at the table | Yes, traditionally | Sometimes | Sometimes |
| Care | Water and a brush, no soap | Soap and water | Brush out the ridges |

## Molcajete

The rough basalt is the point: it grinds, crushes and tears at the same time. Salsa made in a molcajete has texture and a fresher, brighter taste than blended salsa, and the bowl goes straight to the table. The pestle is called a *tejolote*.

**Seasoning:** a new molcajete sheds grit. Grind a handful of dry white rice into powder, rinse, and repeat until the rice powder comes out clean and white. That can take several rounds. Many cooks finish by grinding garlic and salt.

**Real vs fake:** real basalt is heavy and has small natural pores. If it is smooth, light and uniformly grey, it is likely concrete or resin. Avoid those, because they never stop shedding grit.

## Granite Mortar and Pestle

Heavy, stable and non-porous, so it doesn't hold onto flavors or need seasoning. A large one (2 cups or more) works for nearly everything: toasting and grinding whole spices, curry and chili pastes, pesto, garlic and ginger. It makes good guacamole too, but salsa comes out slightly less textured than in a molcajete.

## Suribachi

The Japanese mortar: a ceramic bowl with fine ridges on the inside and a wooden pestle (*surikogi*). The ridges are made for grinding toasted sesame seeds into a fragrant paste for goma-ae and sesame dressings, and they do it better than any smooth mortar.

## Which Should You Buy?

- **Salsa and guacamole most weeks:** a basalt molcajete, 8 inches or wider.
- **One mortar for everything:** a large granite mortar.
- **Japanese sesame dishes:** a suribachi. It's small and cheap.
