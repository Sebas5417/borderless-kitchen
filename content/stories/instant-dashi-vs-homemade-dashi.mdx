**Short answer:** instant dashi granules, Hondashi being the best-known brand, are fine for weeknight miso soup, stir-fries and anything heavily seasoned. Homemade dashi from kombu and bonito flakes tastes cleaner and is worth it where the broth is the dish: clear soups, chawanmushi, agedashi tofu. Dashi packs, which work like tea bags, get you most of the way to homemade in about five minutes.

---

## Side by Side

| | Instant granules | Dashi packs | Homemade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time | Seconds | About 5 minutes | About 20 minutes |
| Made from | Bonito powder, salt, sugar and flavor enhancers | Ground bonito, kombu and sometimes other fish in a bag | Kombu and bonito flakes |
| Flavor | Strong, salty, one-note | Close to homemade | Clean, layered, subtle |
| Salt | Already salted | Some are salted; check the label | You control it |
| Best for | Miso soup, stir-fries, takoyaki batter | Everyday soups and simmered dishes | Clear soups, chawanmushi, agedashi tofu |

## Instant Dashi Granules

Dissolve in hot water and you have stock. That is why it is in most Japanese kitchens. The drawbacks: it is salty, so cut the salt or soy sauce in the recipe, and the flavor is louder and flatter than real dashi. Start with less than the jar suggests and taste. Most contain MSG; check the label if that matters to you.

→ [Shop instant dashi granules on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=hondashi+bonito+soup+stock&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

## Dashi Packs

Ground bonito and kombu in a tea-bag-style pouch. Simmer one in water for a few minutes, squeeze, and remove. The flavor is far closer to homemade than granules are, with no straining. It is the best upgrade for anyone who cooks Japanese food weekly.

→ [Shop dashi packs on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=japanese+dashi+packs+bonito+kombu&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

## Homemade Dashi

Soak kombu in cold water, heat gently, remove it before the boil, add bonito flakes, steep and strain. Full method in the [complete dashi guide](/journal/japanese-dashi-complete-guide). Where the broth is the whole dish, like [agedashi tofu](/journal/japanese-agedashi-tofu-recipe) or [dashimaki tamago](/journal/dashimaki-tamago-recipe-japanese-rolled-omelette), the difference is obvious.

## Which Should You Buy?

- **Cook Japanese food occasionally:** a jar of granules.
- **Cook it every week:** dashi packs.
- **Making a clear soup or a dish where the broth is the point:** make it from scratch.
