**Short answer:** buy **Carnaroli** if you can find it. It holds its shape best and is the hardest to overcook. **Arborio** is the easiest to find and makes a good, creamier risotto, but it goes soft faster. **Vialone Nano** is the Veneto's choice for looser, wavy (*all'onda*) risottos, especially with seafood.

## Why Risotto Rice Is Different

Risotto rice is stirred while hot stock is added a ladle at a time. The stirring rubs starch off the surface of the grains into the liquid, which thickens into the creamy sauce. Meanwhile, the center of each grain should stay slightly firm (*al dente*). A good risotto rice has to release surface starch without falling apart, which is why these varieties are large-grained and starchy. [The science of starch in risotto](/journal/risotto-technique-and-the-science-of-starch) explains it in detail.

## Carnaroli

Often called the "king" of risotto rices by Italian cooks. Its grains are high in amylose, the starch that keeps a grain firm, so they stay distinct even if you cook a minute or two too long. If you only buy one, buy this. **Acquerello** is a well-known aged Carnaroli. It costs more and is especially forgiving.

## Arborio

The one most supermarkets stock. It makes a very creamy risotto, but the grain softens sooner, so watch it closely toward the end. It's also a good choice for [arancini](/recipes/korean-italian-arancini-kimchi-risotto-balls), where some stickiness helps the balls hold together.

→ [Shop Arborio rice on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=arborio+rice+italian&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

## Vialone Nano

A smaller-grained rice from the Veneto, sold with a protected geographic indication (IGP) as Riso Nano Vialone Veronese. It absorbs a lot of liquid and gives the loose, flowing risotto Venetian cooks prefer.

## Can You Use Sushi Rice?

Not as a straight swap. Japanese short-grain rice is bred to cook up sticky and cohesive, not to release starch into a sauce while staying firm. Stirred like risotto, it turns gluey. For a risotto that borrows from Japan, try [dashi risotto](/recipes/dashi-risotto) made with Italian rice. For Japanese rice itself, see [the best short-grain rice for Japanese and Korean cooking](/journal/best-short-grain-rice-for-japanese-and-korean-cooking).
