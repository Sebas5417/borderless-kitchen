**Short answer:** a comal is a flat griddle with no walls, or a very low lip, so you can flip tortillas by hand and slide food on and off easily. A cast-iron skillet heats the same way but its sides get in the way. If you already own a cast-iron skillet, start there. If you make tortillas often, a comal is cheap and noticeably easier.

---

## Side by Side

| | Comal | Cast-iron skillet |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | Flat, rimless or a very low lip | Walls 2 inches or so high |
| Materials | Carbon steel, cast iron, traditionally clay | Cast iron |
| Weight | Carbon steel is light; cast iron is heavy | Heavy |
| Best at | Tortillas, quesadillas, toasting chiles and spices, charring tomatoes and onions | Searing, frying, braising, baking, plus all of the above |
| Flipping by hand | Easy | Awkward |
| Seasoning | Needed for steel and iron | Needed |

## Comal

The tool for anything that goes straight onto dry, hot metal: [tortillas](/journal/masa-harina-vs-cornmeal-difference) from the press, chiles toasted for [enchilada sauce](/journal/enchiladas-mexican-guide-recipe-red-green-mole-sauce), tomatoes and onions charred for salsa. Without walls, you can work fast with your fingertips or a spatula.

- **Carbon steel:** light, heats quickly, inexpensive. The everyday choice.
- **Cast iron:** heavier, holds heat longer, takes longer to heat up. Good on a weak stove.
- **Clay (comal de barro):** traditional, gives a gentle even heat, but it is fragile and suited to gas or open flame. Not for electric or induction tops.

A round comal fits one burner. A long rectangular one spans two burners for cooking several tortillas at once.

## Cast-Iron Skillet

It handles everything a comal does, plus searing, shallow frying and baking. The only real drawback for tortillas is the sides: flipping by hand is harder, and you can fit fewer tortillas at once.

## Can You Use a Nonstick Pan?

For a few tortillas, yes. For toasting chiles and charring vegetables, no: those need dry, high heat, and most nonstick coatings shouldn't be heated empty at high temperatures. That's the real reason to own a comal or cast iron.

## What to Buy

- **Making tortillas weekly:** a carbon steel or cast-iron comal, plus a [cast-iron tortilla press](/journal/best-mexican-cooking-gifts-for-home-cooks).
- **Occasional taco night:** the cast-iron skillet you already own.
