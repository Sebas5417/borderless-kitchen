Burek arrived in Bosnia with the Ottoman Empire and stayed for 500 years. It is now so thoroughly Bosnian that the dish's identity has been reclaimed as distinctly Bosnian rather than Turkish — and with it, the very specific definition: burek is meat, full stop. The Ottomans brought the technique; the Bosnians refined the form.

The Bosnian style differs from Turkish börek in crucial ways. Turkish börek uses thin sheets stacked flat or folded; Bosnian burek is a coil — the filled dough rolled into a rope and arranged in a spiral in a round baking tray. This coil form creates more surface area for the dough to crisp and brown, and the spiral unwinds into individual servings in a way that stacked börek doesn't.

The traditional meat is a 50/50 mix of ground beef and ground lamb — the beef for leanness and mild flavor, the lamb for fat and character. Modern burek often uses pure ground beef (lamb's price and availability have changed); pure lamb is also authentic. Ground pork is never used in traditional Bosnian burek (Bosnia is predominantly Muslim).

## The Dough

Authentic burek dough is handmade — a wheat dough rolled so thin it becomes nearly translucent, similar to strudel dough or handmade phyllo. This requires practice and skill; the dough is stretched over a floured cloth or table until large enough to fill the whole tray.

**At home shortcut:** Commercial phyllo dough (filo), thawed completely, approximates the texture. It will not be identical — commercial phyllo is thinner and more uniform than hand-rolled burek dough — but produces an excellent result without the multi-hour dough rolling process.

## The Filling

Ground meat (beef and lamb, 50/50), diced onion, salt, and pepper — and nothing else in the traditional version. No garlic, no herbs, no spices beyond pepper. The restraint is deliberate; the meat and dough should be the flavor, not aromatics.

The filling should be slightly wet but not soupy. If using very lean ground beef, add a tablespoon of oil.

## The Yogurt

Bosnian burek is always served with cold yogurt — full-fat, slightly sour. The yogurt is not a sauce; it is poured directly over the hot burek or used for dipping. The contrast of hot, crispy pastry with cold yogurt is part of the dish.

**Recipe: Bosnian Burek (Serves 6)**

*Using commercial phyllo dough*

- 500g ground beef (or 250g beef + 250g lamb)
- 1 medium onion, very finely diced
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 1 egg (for filling, optional — adds cohesion)
- 400g commercial phyllo dough (thin sheets), thawed
- 100g unsalted butter, melted (or 100ml sunflower oil)

**For serving:**
- 400ml full-fat plain yogurt, cold
- Salt

**Method — filling:**
Combine raw ground meat, diced onion, salt, pepper, and egg (if using). Mix thoroughly. The filling is used raw; it cooks inside the pastry.

**Method — assembly:**
1. Preheat oven to 200°C (400°F). Oil a 30cm round baking dish.

2. Lay out one sheet of phyllo on a large cloth or clean table. Brush lightly with butter/oil.

3. Place a second sheet directly on top; brush again.

4. Spread a rope of meat filling (about 3cm thick) along the long edge of the double sheet, leaving 2cm margins.

5. Using the cloth, roll the phyllo tightly around the filling into a cylinder.

6. Carefully coil the filled cylinder in the baking dish, starting from the outside and spiraling inward.

7. Repeat with remaining phyllo sheets and filling until the tray is filled with a tight spiral.

8. Brush the top generously with remaining butter/oil.

9. Bake 35–40 minutes until deep golden-brown and crispy on top.

10. Brush immediately with a little cold water when removed from oven — this keeps the pastry from becoming brittle.

Serve with cold yogurt.
