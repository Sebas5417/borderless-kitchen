Bulgogi means "fire meat" — bul (fire) + gogi (meat). It's been eaten in Korea for at least 1,500 years, referenced in the Goguryeo kingdom era as *maekjeok*, grilled meat on skewers. The modern form with its characteristic sweet-savory soy marinade developed during the Joseon dynasty.

It's now one of the most recognized Korean dishes internationally — soft, sweet-savory, deeply aromatic beef served over rice or in lettuce wraps.

## Why the Pear Works

The most important ingredient in bulgogi marinade is one that's rarely explained: Asian pear (or Korean pear) juice. The pear contains proteolytic enzymes — specifically cysteine proteases — that partially break down the protein structure of the beef. This is not just tenderizing: the enzyme changes the texture of the surface fibers so the meat becomes silky and absorbs marinade more thoroughly.

It's the same principle as using papaya (papain) or pineapple (bromelain) as meat tenderizers — but pear is milder and doesn't break down the texture aggressively if you leave it longer.

**Substitutes if pear isn't available:** 1 tbsp pineapple juice (stronger enzymes — shorter marination time), or kiwi (very strong — 30 minutes maximum). Regular apple is a weak substitute.

## The Cuts

Beef that is too thick can't be bulgogi. The characteristic texture requires very thin cuts:

**Best cuts:**
- **Ribeye**, thinly sliced (most flavorful)
- **Sirloin**, thinly sliced (leaner, slightly firmer)
- **Chuck roll**, thinly sliced (more affordable, still good)

Butchers at Korean grocery stores sell pre-sliced bulgogi beef (*불고기용*, bulgogi-yong). If buying at a regular supermarket, freeze the beef partially (1 hour in the freezer) and slice as thin as possible — aim for 3mm.

## The Marinade

**For 500g beef:**
- 4 tbsp soy sauce
- 2 tbsp sesame oil
- 2 tbsp sugar (or 1 tbsp brown sugar + 1 tbsp honey)
- 1 tbsp sake or dry sherry
- 4 garlic cloves, minced or grated
- 1 tsp fresh ginger, grated
- 3 tbsp Asian pear or 2 tbsp pear juice (see substitutes above)
- 1 scallion, finely sliced
- ½ onion, grated (adds sweetness and enzyme action)
- ½ tsp black pepper

Mix. Add beef slices. Mix thoroughly — every surface should be coated. Marinate:
- Minimum: 30 minutes at room temperature
- Better: 4 hours refrigerated
- Best: overnight refrigerated

## Cooking

**Grill method (traditional):** High heat charcoal grill or gas grill. Cook in a single layer, not piled. The sugar in the marinade caramelizes quickly — watch carefully. 1-2 minutes per side.

**Pan method (most practical at home):** Cast iron pan or heavy stainless over high heat. Critically: don't crowd the pan. Overcrowding steams the meat instead of searing it. Cook in batches. The marinade should sizzle vigorously and caramelize at the edges. 1-2 minutes per side.

The goal: light char on the edges, caramelized surface, fully cooked through, still juicy. Not a grey steam-cooked interior.

## Serving

**Over rice:** The classic. A scoop of rice, bulgogi on top, sesame seeds, scallion.

**As ssam (wrap):** In romaine or perilla leaf with a smear of ssamjang and a grain of rice.

**Bulgogi dosirak (lunchbox):** Cold bulgogi over rice. Works cold because the marinade keeps the meat from tasting dry.

**With japchae:** Often served together as a combination dish, the sweet-savory note in bulgogi matching the glass noodle sauce.

---

Bulgogi's international success makes sense in retrospect: the sweet-savory profile is approachable for palates not used to Korean food, the technique is forgiving, and the results look and smell immediately appealing. But within Korea, bulgogi is also the dish that demonstrates why the marinade philosophy — enzyme tenderizing + flavor penetration + caramelization — is one of the most sophisticated approaches to beef in any food culture.
