The name means "what you like, grilled" — okonomi (what you like) + yaki (grilled). It's Japan's most flexible dish. The base is always batter and cabbage; everything else adapts to what you have and what you want.

There are two major regional styles:

**Osaka-style (Kansai/Osaka-yaki):** All ingredients mixed directly into the batter and cooked as a single pancake. The home cook's version and the most common internationally.

**Hiroshima-style:** Layers built on the griddle — batter poured first, then separately stacked with yakisoba noodles, cabbage, and protein. More complex; requires a specific technique and usually a teppan griddle.

This guide covers Osaka-style.

## The Batter

The batter is the foundation and needs one special ingredient: **nagaimo** (Japanese mountain yam). Grated nagaimo adds a unique stickiness and lightness that produces the characteristically fluffy interior texture of good okonomiyaki.

- 150g all-purpose flour
- 150ml dashi
- 2 eggs
- 100g nagaimo (grated) — substitute: 2 extra eggs + ½ tsp baking powder if unavailable
- ½ tsp salt

Mix together. Rest 10 minutes.

## The Cabbage

**3 cups napa cabbage, roughly shredded** — this is more cabbage than feels right. Okonomiyaki is mostly cabbage held together by batter. Use napa cabbage, not regular cabbage — it has more moisture and softer texture.

Mix the shredded cabbage into the batter.

## The Fillings

Choose one base and add to the batter:

**Buta tama (pork and egg):** 100g thinly sliced pork belly per pancake, laid over the top of the batter in the pan before flipping.

**Ebi tama (shrimp):** 6-8 medium shrimp, peeled and roughly chopped, mixed into the batter.

**Ika tama (squid):** Squid rings, mixed into batter.

**Yasai okonomiyaki (vegetarian):** Corn, scallions, mushrooms, and extra cheese in the batter.

## Cooking

**Per pancake:**
1. Heat a wide pan or griddle over medium heat. Brush with oil.
2. Pour batter (approximately ⅔ cup for one pancake) into the center. Shape into a round, 2-3cm thick. Don't spread too thin.
3. For buta tama: lay pork belly slices across the top.
4. Cook 4-5 minutes without moving until the bottom is set and golden.
5. **The flip:** Use two spatulas — one to support, one to guide. Flip decisively. Press down gently. Cook 4-5 more minutes.
6. The pancake should be cooked through — a skewer inserted in the center should come out clean.

## The Toppings (Essential)

This is not optional. The combination of these four toppings is what makes okonomiyaki okonomiyaki.

1. **Okonomiyaki sauce:** Similar to Worcestershire, but thicker and sweeter. Bull-Dog brand is the standard. Drizzle generously, spread with a brush.
2. **Kewpie mayonnaise:** In a zigzag pattern over the sauce.
3. **Katsuobushi (bonito flakes):** Scattered generously. They dance from the steam.
4. **Aonori (dried green seaweed powder):** Scattered.

Optional: beni shoga (pickled red ginger) on the side for contrast.

## Serving

Okonomiyaki is a communal dish in Japan — cooked at the table on a teppan griddle, eaten directly from the pan with special small spatulas. For home cooking, a cast iron skillet gets very close to the teppan result.

---

Okonomiyaki scales from a weeknight quick dinner (15 minutes) to a full teppanyaki evening with a tabletop griddle. The batter keeps refrigerated for 2 days; make extra.
