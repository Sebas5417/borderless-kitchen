Most people buy the wrong first Korean cookbook. They buy the most beautiful one, cook two recipes, and stall, because it assumed a pantry they did not have. The right first book is the one that teaches the pantry while it teaches the recipes.

Here are the six worth owning, sorted by what you already know.

---

## If You Are Starting From Zero

### Maangchi's Real Korean Cooking — Maangchi

→ [Shop on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=maangchi+real+korean+cooking&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

The default answer, and for good reason. Maangchi taught a generation of English-speaking cooks to make kimchi on YouTube, and the book has the same quality: every ingredient explained, every technique shown, nothing assumed. If you buy one Korean cookbook, buy this one.

**Best for:** the first 50 Korean dishes you will cook.

### Cook Korean! — Robin Ha

→ [Shop on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=cook+korean+robin+ha+comic+book&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

A cookbook drawn as a comic. That sounds like a gimmick until you try to follow it — step-by-step panels are clearer than prose for techniques like rolling kimbap or folding mandu. An excellent gift for a younger cook or anyone intimidated by dense recipe pages.

**Best for:** visual learners and gifts.

---

## If You Already Cook Korean Food

### Maangchi's Big Book of Korean Cooking — Maangchi

→ [Shop on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=maangchi+big+book+of+korean+cooking&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

The follow-up: broader, deeper, with more of the celebration and regional dishes that the first book leaves out. Buy it second, not first.

**Best for:** going from weeknight Korean to the full table.

### Korean Home Cooking — Sohui Kim

→ [Shop on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=korean+home+cooking+sohui+kim&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Strong on the things that make a Korean meal feel like home: banchan, jjigae, the everyday rhythm of rice, soup and side dishes. A good companion to our own guide to [easy banchan](/journal/korean-side-dishes-easy-banchan-recipes).

**Best for:** banchan and stews.

---

## If You Want Korean Food in an American Kitchen

### Koreatown — Deuki Hong and Matt Rodbard

→ [Shop on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=koreatown+cookbook+deuki+hong&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Korean-American cooking as it is actually eaten in LA, New York and Atlanta — barbecue joints, late-night spots, the drinking-food canon. Loud, fun and very usable.

**Best for:** barbecue night and [anju](/journal/korean-drinking-culture-anju-food-guide).

### Seoul Meets Mexico City — Borderless Kitchen Vol. II

→ [Get Seoul Meets Mexico City on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0H6VD21M2?tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Full disclosure: this is our book. Thirty recipes built on the overlap between the Korean and Mexican pantries — gochujang beside guajillo, doenjang beside mole, kimchi beside salsa. It is not a beginner's Korean cookbook; it is for the cook who already loves Korean food and wants to see what it does next to another cuisine built on fermentation and slow heat. $19.99 paperback, $9.99 Kindle.

**Best for:** the cook who has Maangchi and wants something new.

## Which One to Buy

| You are… | Buy |
|---|---|
| Starting from zero | *Maangchi's Real Korean Cooking* |
| Buying a gift for a beginner | *Cook Korean!* |
| Already cooking Korean weekly | *Maangchi's Big Book* or *Korean Home Cooking* |
| Hosting barbecue | *Koreatown* |
| Looking for something no one else has | *Seoul Meets Mexico City* |

Whichever you choose, stock the pantry first — the [Korean grocery store guide](/journal/korean-grocery-store-guide-what-to-buy) tells you what to buy, and the [Korean cooking gift guide](/journal/best-korean-cooking-gifts-for-home-cooks) covers the tools.
