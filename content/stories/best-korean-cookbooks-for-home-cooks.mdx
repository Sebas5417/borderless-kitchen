Choose a Korean cookbook by the way you learn and the meals you want to make. Illustrated steps, a wide recipe reference and a fusion collection can each be useful, but they meet different needs.

## For visual instruction: Cook Korean!

Robin Ha's *Cook Korean!* uses comics to explain dishes and cooking steps. Consider it if you prefer to see a sequence rather than work only from blocks of prose. The [publisher's description and sample](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/252746/cook-korean-by-robin-ha/) let you inspect that format before buying.

When choosing a first book, look for ingredient explanations as well as recipes. A method becomes easier to follow when you know which item to buy and how it is used again in another meal.

## For a broader collection: The Korean Cookbook

Junghyun Park and Jungyoon Choi's *The Korean Cookbook* is a wide-ranging collection of Korean dishes published by Phaidon. Consider it if you want to explore more of the repertoire over time. Check the [publisher's book page](https://www.phaidon.com/en-ca/products/the-korean-cookbook) and a sample to decide whether the level of detail fits your experience.

The authors' scope and the format matter more than a book's size alone. Pick a few recipes you would want to make soon and see what preparation, ingredients and equipment they require.

## For Korean-Mexican fusion: Seoul Meets Mexico City

**This is our book.** *Seoul Meets Mexico City* by Sebastian Dri combines Korean and Mexican cooking in thirty recipes and six master sauces. It is a fusion option for someone interested in both cuisines.

Read about the [book and its free previews](/seoul-meets-mexico-city), or browse the [Korean-Mexican recipe collection](/recipes/cuisine/korean-mexican). If you want a traditional Korean starting point first, choose a book around that goal and use the fusion recipes as a separate experiment.

## Make a pantry plan

Before buying a cookbook, read two or three sample recipes. List the ingredients you do not have, check local availability and look for ways to use each purchase in several meals. Our [Korean grocery guide](/journal/korean-grocery-store-guide-what-to-buy) and [Korean recipe collection](/recipes/cuisine/korean) can help you explore the ingredients and dishes.

Check package labels and the book's substitution guidance against your own dietary needs. Different recipes may ask for specific forms of an ingredient; a familiar name on a shopping list is only a starting point.
