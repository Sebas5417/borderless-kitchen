**Short answer:** **Mexican oregano** (*Lippia graveolens*) is a verbena-family plant with a citrusy, grassy, slightly licorice flavor. **Regular oregano** (*Origanum vulgare*, often called Mediterranean or Greek oregano) is a mint-family plant, sweeter and more minty. For pozole, chili, beans, and red-chile salsas, buy Mexican oregano. For pizza, tomato sauce and Greek salad, use Mediterranean oregano.

---

## Side by Side

| | Mexican oregano | Mediterranean oregano |
|---|---|---|
| Plant | *Lippia graveolens* (verbena family) | *Origanum vulgare* (mint family) |
| Flavor | Citrusy, grassy, earthy, a little licorice | Sweet, peppery, minty |
| Best with | Chiles, cumin, beans, pork, tomato in Mexican dishes | Tomato, olive oil, garlic, lamb, feta |
| Classic dishes | Pozole, menudo, chili, salsas, adobos | Pizza, marinara, Greek salad, souvlaki |

---

## Can You Swap Them?

In a pinch, yes. Both are savory dried herbs. But the difference shows in dishes where oregano is a main flavor. Mediterranean oregano in pozole tastes a little like pizza; Mexican oregano in marinara tastes slightly off. If you cook both cuisines, keep both: they're cheap and keep for months.

**Toast and crush.** Mexican oregano is often sold as whole dried leaves. Warm them briefly in a dry pan, then crumble them between your palms over the pot.
