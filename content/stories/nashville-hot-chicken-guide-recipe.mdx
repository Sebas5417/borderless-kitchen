Nashville hot chicken was created at Prince's Hot Chicken Shack, which Thornton Prince III opened in the 1930s. The story, long repeated in Nashville, is that his girlfriend added excessive cayenne to his fried chicken to punish him for staying out late. Instead of being deterred, Thornton loved it, developed the recipe, and turned it into a restaurant. His great-niece André Prince Jeffries still runs Prince's.

The dish remained a local Nashville secret for about seventy years. Then, in the 2010s, it became a national food trend — Hattie B's Hot Chicken (opened 2012) expanded to multiple cities, KFC launched a version, and "Nashville hot" appeared on menus from fast food to fine dining across the country.

## What Makes It Different

Nashville hot chicken is not:
- Spicy fried chicken (fried with heat in the breading)
- Buffalo chicken (tossed in vinegar-based hot sauce after frying)
- Chicken in a spicy sauce

It is fried chicken that is basted AFTER frying with a paste made from cayenne, brown sugar, and the hot oil the chicken was fried in. This is the invention that makes it distinct:

1. The chicken is fried in oil
2. A portion of that hot frying oil is combined with cayenne pepper, brown sugar, garlic powder, paprika, and salt
3. This paste is brushed over the just-fried chicken while it's still hot
4. The hot oil in the paste cooks the spices against the crust, creating a glistening, caramelized, spicy coating

The result has a dark, slightly oily, intensely spiced exterior that is at once crunchy (from the fried crust) and coated (from the paste). The sugar caramelizes slightly from the heat. The cayenne creates deep, building heat rather than bright pepper heat.

## The Heat Levels

Prince's and Hattie B's both offer tiers of heat. The naming varies but the concept is standard:

- **Plain/Mild:** No paste or minimal cayenne
- **Medium:** A moderate paste that most people can handle
- **Hot:** Significant heat, face-flushing
- **Extra Hot:** Serious spice tolerance required
- **"Shut the Cluck Up"** (Hattie B's highest): Not for casual heat consumption

The paste recipe scales by cayenne quantity; the sugar stays roughly constant.

## The Presentation

Authentic Nashville hot chicken is served:
- On two or three slices of white sandwich bread (not toast, not brioche — plain white bread, which absorbs the spiced oil as you eat)
- With dill pickle chips on top
- On a sheet of butcher paper or wax paper, not a plate

The bread and pickles are not decorative — they are functional. The bread absorbs the oil; the pickles cut the heat and richness. This is not presentation; it is balance.

**Recipe: Nashville Hot Chicken (Serves 4)**

**Chicken:**
- 1 whole chicken cut into 8 pieces (or 1 kg chicken thighs/drumsticks)
- 1 cup buttermilk
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1 teaspoon black pepper
- 1 teaspoon garlic powder
- Vegetable oil for frying (deep frying, 5–7cm of oil in a deep pan)

**Breading:**
- 200g all-purpose flour
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1 teaspoon black pepper
- 1 teaspoon garlic powder
- 1 teaspoon paprika

**Hot paste (medium-hot):**
- 4 tablespoons cayenne pepper (reduce to 2 tablespoons for medium; increase to 6 for extra hot)
- 2 tablespoons brown sugar
- 1 teaspoon garlic powder
- 1 teaspoon paprika
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- 4 tablespoons of the hot frying oil (removed just before frying the last batch)

**Method:**

1. **Brine:** Combine buttermilk, salt, pepper, and garlic powder. Submerge chicken; refrigerate 4 hours to overnight.

2. **Breading:** Mix flour, salt, pepper, garlic powder, paprika. Remove chicken from buttermilk (let excess drip off); dredge in flour mixture, pressing to adhere. Rest 10 minutes on a rack.

3. **Frying:** Heat oil to 175°C in a deep, heavy pot. Fry chicken in batches — don't crowd — 12–15 minutes, turning halfway, until deep golden brown and cooked through (internal temperature 74°C). Drain on a rack.

4. **Paste:** While the last batch fries, carefully remove 4 tablespoons of hot oil into a small heatproof bowl. Add cayenne, brown sugar, garlic powder, paprika, and salt. Stir to combine — it should form a thick, dark paste. The oil temperature helps bloom the spices.

5. **Apply paste:** Working quickly while chicken is hot, brush the paste generously over all surfaces of the fried chicken.

6. **Serve:** On white bread slices with dill pickle chips on top.
