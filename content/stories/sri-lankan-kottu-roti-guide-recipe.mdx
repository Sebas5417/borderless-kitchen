The kottu sound is unmistakable. The vendor uses two metal scrapers against the flat iron hotplate (tawa) to chop and mix the ingredients — a rapid, rhythmic clang-clang-clang that echoes down Sri Lankan streets and draws the crowd. The sound is part of the experience; kottu heard from a distance means hot food and a crowd.

Kottu roti's origin story sits in the resourcefulness of Sri Lankan street cooking. Godamba roti — thin, slightly flaky flatbread — accumulated as leftover at the end of a day of roti production. Chopped, stir-fried with eggs and whatever vegetables were available, seasoned with curry, it became a late-night staple. Today it is not a leftover dish — vendors make godamba roti specifically for kottu — but the principle of the dish remains: shredded flatbread as the base for a high-heat stir-fry.

## Godamba Roti vs Alternatives

**Godamba roti** is a thin, slightly stretchy Sri Lankan flatbread made from all-purpose flour, oil, and water — similar to Malaysian roti canai but slightly thinner. It is the specific bread used for kottu, and its texture (slightly chewy, flaky in layers, can be shredded) is what makes kottu work.

**At home:** Make a simplified version (recipe below), or substitute Malaysian roti canai (available frozen at South Asian/Southeast Asian grocery stores), or use plain store-bought paratha. The texture won't be identical but the dish works.

**What doesn't work well:** Naan (too thick, doesn't shred right), pita (too thick), or any bread with significant yeast structure.

## The Curry

Kottu is seasoned with a drizzle of Sri Lankan curry — a thin, dark, coconut-and-spice-based curry (chicken, beef, seafood, or vegetable). This is what gives kottu its characteristic red-brown color and deep spice flavor. If you have no Sri Lankan curry: a thin coconut milk curry seasoned with curry powder, chili, and garlic approximates it.

## The Protein Options

Traditional kottu options:
- **Chicken kottu:** Shredded cooked chicken
- **Egg kottu:** Egg only (common vegetarian option)
- **Cheese kottu:** With processed cheese (Sri Lankan innovation, widely popular)
- **Beef kottu:** Ground or shredded beef
- **Seafood kottu:** Shrimp, cuttlefish

**Recipe: Chicken Kottu Roti (Serves 2–3)**

**Simple godamba roti (make ahead):**
- 250g all-purpose flour
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- 150ml water
- 2 tablespoons oil

Combine flour, salt, water; knead 10 minutes until smooth. Cover; rest 30 minutes. Divide into 6 balls. Roll very thin. Cook on a dry hot pan 1 minute per side until set. Stack; refrigerate until needed. Before kottu: shred or roughly cut into 2–3cm strips.

**For the stir-fry:**
- 4 godamba rotis (or 4 paratha/roti canai), shredded
- 2 eggs, beaten
- 150g cooked chicken, shredded
- 1 medium onion, thinly sliced
- 2 green onions, chopped
- 1 small carrot, julienned
- 1 green chili, sliced
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 3–4 tablespoons Sri Lankan curry (or thin coconut milk curry)
- 1 teaspoon chili flakes
- Salt and pepper
- Oil for the pan

**Method:**

1. Heat a large flat-bottomed pan or tawa until very hot. Add 2 tablespoons oil.

2. Add onion, green chili, garlic; cook on high heat 2–3 minutes until lightly charred.

3. Add shredded roti; stir-fry constantly, chopping down with two spatulas (or chop roughly with a knife on the pan edge).

4. Push roti to the side; add eggs to the center. Scramble briefly, then mix into the roti.

5. Add chicken and carrot; continue stir-frying 2 minutes.

6. Add curry sauce; toss to coat everything. Season with salt, pepper, and chili flakes.

7. Add green onions in the final minute.

8. Serve hot. In Sri Lanka, served on a banana leaf with a light curry for dipping.

*The high heat and constant motion are essential — kottu should develop some char on the roti pieces and the egg.*
