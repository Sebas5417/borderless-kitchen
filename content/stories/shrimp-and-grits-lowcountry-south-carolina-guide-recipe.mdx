The Lowcountry is the coastal area of South Carolina and Georgia — tidal marshes, barrier islands, rice plantation history, and a distinctly African American foodway called Gullah Geechee. The Gullah people, descendants of enslaved Africans who worked the rice plantations, preserved and shaped the cooking of the region. Shrimp and grits is a Gullah staple: fresh shrimp from the tidal creeks, corn ground at local mills, cooked simply and eaten for breakfast or dinner.

Bill Neal put it on the menu at Crook's Corner in Chapel Hill in 1985 — adding cream, cheese, country ham, and mushrooms, making it a dinner-appropriate dish rather than working food. It was mentioned in the New York Times in 1987 as one of the things visitors must eat in the South. From there, shrimp and grits colonized American restaurant menus, appearing in a thousand variations from New Orleans to New York.

The Lowcountry version remains simpler than the restaurant derivatives. The debate about "correct" shrimp and grits misses the point — there is no single correct version. There is a correct technique for grits, and everything else varies.

## The Grits

This is where most versions fail. "Quick grits" (fine-ground, processed to cook in 5 minutes) produce a bland, thin porridge with no corn flavor. **Stone-ground grits** (coarse-ground corn, minimally processed) take 30–45 minutes and produce something entirely different: thick, flavorful, with the natural sweetness and aroma of corn.

Stone-ground grits are available from Anson Mills (South Carolina, mail order) and other Southern mills. Substitute coarse-ground polenta if stone-ground grits are unavailable — they are essentially the same product.

**Grits technique:**
- 4:1 or 5:1 water/stock to grits ratio
- Bring liquid to a boil; whisk in grits slowly
- Reduce heat; cook 30–45 minutes, stirring frequently to prevent sticking
- Finish with butter, cream or milk, salt, and optionally cheese

## The Shrimp

Gulf or Atlantic shrimp are traditional — the larger and fresher, the better. Peel and devein before cooking.

The shrimp sauce varies enormously by cook and region:
- **Simple Lowcountry style:** Bacon, garlic, butter, lemon
- **New Orleans style:** Tomatoes, andouille, Creole seasoning
- **Upscale restaurant style:** Country ham or prosciutto, mushrooms, cream reduction, sherry

All are legitimate. The shrimp should cook fast (2–3 minutes in a hot pan) and be served immediately — overcooked shrimp are rubbery.

**Recipe: Shrimp and Grits, Lowcountry Style (Serves 4)**

**Stone-ground grits:**
- 200g stone-ground grits (coarse)
- 900ml water or chicken stock (or half-half)
- 180ml whole milk or heavy cream
- 60g unsalted butter
- 80g sharp cheddar cheese, grated (optional — traditional Lowcountry versions don't always include it)
- Salt

**Shrimp and sauce:**
- 500g large shrimp, peeled and deveined
- 150g thick-cut bacon, cut into lardons
- 1 shallot or small onion, finely diced
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 120ml chicken stock
- 1 tablespoon lemon juice
- 1 teaspoon Worcestershire sauce
- 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
- Green onions and parsley for garnish
- Hot sauce to serve (Crystal, Tabasco, or Louisiana Hot Sauce)

**Method — Grits:**

1. Bring water/stock to a boil in a heavy saucepan. Season generously with salt.

2. Slowly whisk in the grits. Reduce heat to the lowest setting.

3. Cook, stirring every 5 minutes, for 30–45 minutes until the grits are thick, creamy, and no longer gritty. Add water if they get too thick.

4. Stir in butter, cream, and cheese (if using). Taste; adjust salt. Keep warm with a lid on, adding liquid as needed to maintain creamy consistency.

**Method — Shrimp:**

1. In a large skillet, cook bacon over medium heat until crispy. Remove bacon; leave fat in pan.

2. Increase heat to medium-high. Season shrimp with salt and pepper; cook in bacon fat 1–2 minutes per side until pink. Remove; set aside (they will finish in the sauce).

3. In the same pan, cook shallot in remaining fat until softened, 2 minutes. Add garlic; cook 30 seconds.

4. Add stock, lemon juice, and Worcestershire. Simmer 2 minutes, scraping up any brown bits.

5. Add butter; swirl to incorporate. Return shrimp to pan; heat through 1 minute. Add most of the crispy bacon.

6. To serve: spoon grits into shallow bowls. Top with shrimp and pan sauce. Garnish with remaining bacon, green onions, and parsley. Serve with hot sauce on the side.
