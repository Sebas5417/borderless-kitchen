**Short answer:** buy an **8-inch cast-iron press**. The weight does the pressing for you, the plates stay flat, and it lasts for decades. Aluminum presses are lighter and cheaper but can flex at the hinge. Wooden presses are beautiful but bulkier and need more care.

---

## Side by Side

| | Cast iron | Aluminum | Wood |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weight | Heavy (does the work) | Light | Medium to heavy |
| Even tortillas | Very good | Good when new; can flex over time | Good |
| Durability | Decades | Hinge and handle can bend | Long-lasting with care |
| Care | Keep dry; season if it's bare iron | Low maintenance | Keep dry; oil occasionally |
| Price | Moderate | Lowest | Highest |

## Cast Iron

The standard choice. A cast-iron press is heavy enough that a gentle pull on the handle flattens a ball of masa evenly. Victoria, a Colombian cast-iron maker, is a widely sold brand. Some presses come bare or pre-seasoned and need to be kept dry; others are enamel-coated. Check which kind you're buying.

## Aluminum

Light, cheap and fine for occasional taco nights. The weakness is flex: with heavy use, the handle or hinge can bend and the tortillas come out thicker on one side. Pick one with a thick plate and a solid hinge pin.

→ [Shop aluminum tortilla presses on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=aluminum+tortilla+press&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

## Wood

Traditional hardwood presses press evenly and look good on a counter, but they're larger and need to be kept dry so they don't warp or crack.

## What Size?

- **8 inch:** the most versatile. Makes standard taco-size and slightly larger corn tortillas. **Buy this one** if you're unsure.
- **6 to 6.5 inch:** compact, good for small street-taco tortillas.
- **10 inch:** for larger tortillas and quesadillas; heavier and needs more counter space.

A press is mainly for **corn** tortillas. Flour tortillas spring back after pressing and are usually rolled with a pin instead.

---

## How to Get Good Tortillas

1. **Line the press.** Cut a zip-top bag along two sides and put the masa between the two layers. Without a liner, the dough sticks.
2. **Use the right masa.** Mix [masa harina](/journal/masa-harina-vs-cornmeal-difference) with warm water to a dough that feels like soft play-dough: it shouldn't crack at the edges or stick to your hands. Rest it covered for 10–15 minutes.
3. **Press gently.** Golf-ball-size ball, one firm pull. If the edges crack, the dough is too dry.
4. **Cook hot and fast** on a dry [comal or cast-iron skillet](/journal/comal-vs-cast-iron-skillet): about 30 seconds on the first side, about a minute on the second, then flip back briefly. A good tortilla puffs.
5. **Keep them warm** in a cloth or an insulated tortilla warmer so they stay soft.
