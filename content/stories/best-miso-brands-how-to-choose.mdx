**Short answer:** if you buy one tub, buy a **mixed (awase) or white (shiro) miso without added dashi**, from a Japanese brand such as Marukome or Hikari Miso. It covers soup, dressings, glazes and marinades. Add a red miso later if you cook a lot of hearty stews and braises.

---

## The Three Styles

| | White (shiro) | Red (aka) | Mixed (awase) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fermentation | Short | Long | Blend of both |
| Taste | Sweet, mild | Salty, deep, robust | In between |
| Best for | Dressings, glazes, light soups, baking | Hearty soups, braises, meat marinades | Everyday miso soup and most recipes |

Colour and saltiness vary between makers, so treat these as tendencies rather than rules.

---

## Read the Label

- **Dashi-iri (だし入り) means dashi is already added.** It's convenient for quick miso soup but salty and fish-flavored, so avoid it for dressings, desserts or vegetarian cooking.
- **Low-sodium (gen-en) versions** exist if salt is a concern.
- **Organic and unpasteurized** misos are available. Unpasteurized miso is still alive, so keep it cold.
- **Storage:** keep opened miso in the fridge with the surface covered. It lasts for months.

## Widely Available Brands

### Marukome

One of Japan's best-known miso makers, based in Nagano. Its tubs are easy to find in Asian groceries, in white, red, mixed and dashi-iri versions. Check the label for dashi.

→ [Shop Marukome miso on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=marukome+miso&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

### Hikari Miso

Another large Nagano maker, with organic lines that are common in US supermarkets.

→ [Shop Hikari Miso on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=hikari+organic+miso&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

### Miso Master

Made in North Carolina by the American Miso Company, unpasteurized and organic, with unusual varieties such as chickpea miso. A good choice if you want a domestically made, live miso.

→ [Shop Miso Master on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=miso+master+organic+miso&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)
