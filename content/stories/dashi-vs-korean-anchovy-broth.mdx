Japan and Korea both build soups on a seafood stock rather than a meat one, and both start in the same place: a sheet of kombu, the dried kelp that releases glutamate into water. Then they split.

**Short answer:** Japanese *dashi* adds **katsuobushi** — smoked, dried bonito shaved into flakes — and is steeped briefly for a clean, smoky, delicate stock. Korean *myeolchi yuksu* adds **dried anchovies** and is simmered longer for a deeper, more savoury, slightly fishy stock that stands up to gochujang and doenjang.

---

## Side by Side

| | Japanese dashi | Korean anchovy broth |
|---|---|---|
| Base | Kombu | Kombu |
| Second ingredient | Katsuobushi (bonito flakes) | Dried anchovies (myeolchi) |
| Method | Short steep below a boil | Simmer 10–20 minutes |
| Flavour | Clean, smoky, delicate | Deep, savoury, robust |
| Classic uses | Miso soup, noodle broths, chawanmushi | Doenjang jjigae, tteokbokki, guksu |

---

## Why the Difference Matters

Dashi is built for clarity — it supports subtle flavours like miso, soy and egg without covering them. Our [complete dashi guide](/journal/japanese-dashi-complete-guide) covers the method and the umami chemistry. Anchovy broth is built to hold its own against chili and fermented paste; a delicate dashi would disappear under a spoonful of gochujang. Use dashi in a Korean stew and it tastes thin; use anchovy broth in miso soup and it tastes heavy.

## What to Buy

### Kombu (both stocks)

→ [Shop kombu on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=dried+kombu+kelp+for+dashi&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Wipe, do not wash — the white powder on the surface is flavour. Never boil it hard; it turns slimy and bitter.

### Katsuobushi (for dashi)

→ [Shop katsuobushi bonito flakes on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=katsuobushi+bonito+flakes+for+dashi&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Large, thin flakes for stock; the fine, small packets are for topping dishes.

### Dried anchovies for stock (for Korean broth)

→ [Shop dried anchovies for soup stock on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=korean+dried+anchovy+for+soup+stock+large&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Buy the **large** dried anchovies for stock — the small ones are for stir-fried side dishes. Remove the heads and dark guts before simmering, or the broth turns bitter. Keep them in the freezer.

### The shortcut

→ [Shop dashi packets on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=dashi+packets+tea+bag&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Tea-bag-style dashi packets are a reasonable weeknight shortcut and far better than instant granules. Korean stores sell similar anchovy-kelp broth packs.
