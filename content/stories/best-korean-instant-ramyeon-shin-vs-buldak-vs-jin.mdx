**Short answer:** start with **Nongshim Shin Ramyun**, the classic spicy beef-broth ramyeon. If you want a milder, rounder broth, try **Ottogi Jin Ramen** (it comes in mild and spicy). For thick noodles and a seafood broth, buy **Nongshim Neoguri**. **Samyang Buldak** is a stir-fried, saucy noodle with no soup, and it's much hotter than the others.

---

## Side by Side

| | Style | Heat | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shin Ramyun** (Nongshim) | Soup, beef-and-chili broth | Medium-hot | The benchmark; good with an egg |
| **Jin Ramen** (Ottogi) | Soup, savory broth | Mild or spicy versions | Rounder flavor |
| **Neoguri** (Nongshim) | Soup, seafood broth, thick udon-style noodles | Mild or spicy | Includes a piece of kelp |
| **Buldak** (Samyang) | Stir-fried, no soup, sweet-hot sauce | Very hot (the 2x is hotter still) | Many flavors, including carbonara |
| **Chapagetti** (Nongshim) | Black bean sauce, no soup | Not spicy | Mixed with Neoguri it's *jjapaguri*, the "ram-don" from *Parasite* |

## Five-Minute Upgrades

- **Egg:** crack one in during the last minute and don't stir, or add a soft-boiled egg.
- **Kimchi:** a handful of well-fermented kimchi makes the broth deeper and sourer.
- **Green onion and cheese:** a slice of American cheese melted on top is a classic Korean move.
- **Tofu:** soft tofu turns it into a quick [sundubu-style ramen](/recipes/sundubu-jjigae-ramen).
- **Less salt:** use the full noodle and three-quarters of the seasoning packet.
