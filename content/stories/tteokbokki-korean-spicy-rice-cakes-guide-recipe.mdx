Tteokbokki (pronounced ttok-boh-gee) is the food most associated with the pojangmacha — the covered food tent stalls that line Korean streets, particularly in Seoul. It has been street food since at least the 1950s, when the modern spicy version (using gochujang and gochugaru) emerged from an earlier royal court version made with soy sauce and beef.

The evolution from soy-braised royal court food to gochujang street food tracked Korea's postwar economy: chili was cheap, gochujang added deep flavor at low cost, and the chewy-saucy combination became an obsession that has persisted for seventy years.

## The Rice Cakes (Tteok)

Tteokbokki uses garaetteok — white rice cakes extruded into cylinders, about 1–1.5cm diameter and 5–6cm long. They are made from steamed glutinous rice flour and have a specific chewiness that is the textural point of the dish. When cooked in the sauce, they soften slightly on the outside while remaining springy in the center.

Fresh garaetteok (available at Korean grocery stores) are best — they cook faster and have a better texture. Refrigerated or frozen tteok needs to be soaked in cold water for 30 minutes before cooking to soften.

The rice cakes themselves are neutral in flavor — they're a vehicle for the sauce.

## The Sauce

The tteokbokki sauce is a specific balance of:

**Gochujang:** The fermented Korean red chili paste that provides deep, savory, complex heat — not the bright fresh heat of raw chili but a slower, rounder spiciness.

**Gochugaru:** Korean red pepper flakes, for additional heat and a more immediate chili flavor than gochujang alone provides.

**Soy sauce:** Umami and saltiness.

**Sugar:** The sweetness is not background — it is a primary flavor in tteokbokki and what makes the sauce addictive rather than simply hot. The sweet-spicy combination is the point.

**Dashi or anchovy stock:** Traditional tteokbokki uses a broth made from dried anchovies (myeolchi) and dried kelp (dashima) as the sauce base, which adds deep seafood umami. Water works as a substitute; the dish will be less layered.

The sauce reduces as the tteok cooks, thickening and clinging to each rice cake. The finishing consistency should be thick enough to coat but fluid enough that the rice cakes are swimming in sauce, not dry.

## Standard Additions

**Eomuk (fish cakes):** Flat Korean fish cakes, sliced into triangles or pieces. These are almost universal in tteokbokki — they absorb the sauce and add a savory contrast to the rice cake's neutral chewiness.

**Boiled eggs:** Hard or medium-boiled eggs, halved, added to the sauce to warm through. The eggs absorb the sauce color.

**Ramen noodles:** "Rabokki" — a combination of ramyeon (instant noodles) and tteokbokki. Extremely popular. The noodles are added to the sauce and cook together.

**Cheese:** A very modern variant popular among younger Koreans — a slice of processed cheese or mozzarella melted over the top.

**Fried foods (튀김/twigim):** Tteokbokki is commonly served with assorted fried foods — shrimp tempura, vegetable fritters, sweet potato — that are dipped into the sauce.

**Recipe: Classic Tteokbokki (Serves 3–4)**

- 500g garaetteok (cylinder rice cakes), soaked in cold water 30 minutes if refrigerated
- 200g eomuk (fish cake sheets), cut into triangles
- 2 hard-boiled eggs, peeled (optional)
- 3 stalks green onions, cut into 5cm pieces

**Sauce:**
- 500ml dashi (or water with 1/2 teaspoon anchovy powder)
- 3 tablespoons gochujang
- 1 tablespoon gochugaru (adjust to heat preference)
- 1 tablespoon soy sauce
- 1 tablespoon sugar (or 2 tablespoons if you prefer sweeter)
- 1 teaspoon sesame oil
- 1 teaspoon minced garlic

**Method:**

1. Combine dashi, gochujang, gochugaru, soy sauce, sugar, and garlic in a wide, shallow pan. Bring to a boil over medium-high heat, stirring to dissolve the gochujang.

2. Add drained tteok and fish cakes. Stir to coat everything in sauce. Reduce heat to medium.

3. Cook, stirring occasionally, 8–12 minutes until the sauce thickens and the tteok are coated and slightly swollen. The sauce should be thick enough to coat the rice cakes but not gluey. Add more water if it thickens too fast.

4. Add boiled eggs in the last 3 minutes to warm through. Add green onions in the last minute.

5. Drizzle sesame oil over before serving. Serve directly from the pan — tteokbokki cools and thickens quickly.

*For rabokki:* Add one block of instant ramen noodles (discard seasoning packet) in step 3 alongside the tteok.

**Making dashi:** Simmer 10 dried anchovies and a 10cm piece of dried kelp in 600ml water for 15 minutes. Discard solids. Makes approximately 500ml dashi.
