Sundubu (순두부) is not firm tofu made softer. It's a fundamentally different product — tofu that has been curdled but not pressed, so the curds remain surrounded by liquid, soft and cloud-like throughout. The texture is barely distinct from the broth when it's at its best.

Sundubu jjigae is served in the dolsot (stone pot) that holds heat through the entire meal, still bubbling at the table. An egg is cracked over the surface just before serving and stirred in, thickening the broth slightly as it cooks from the residual heat.

## The Broth Base

The color of sundubu jjigae is a vivid orange-red, coming entirely from gochugaru. The spice level varies — restaurants offer mild, medium, and spicy versions.

**Broth foundation:**
- 2 tbsp vegetable oil or sesame oil
- 2 tbsp gochugaru (adjust for heat preference)
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 300ml anchovy-kelp broth (or clam juice + water)

**Method:**
Heat oil over medium. Add gochugaru. Stir constantly — 30-45 seconds only. The gochugaru blooms and the oil turns deep red-orange. This is the most important step. Do not skip it and do not burn it.

Add garlic. Cook 30 seconds. Add broth. The broth should immediately turn red-orange from the bloomed gochugaru.

## The Variations

### Haemul Sundubu (해물 순두부) — Seafood Version

The most popular. Add any combination:
- 6-8 clams (cleaned)
- 6-8 shrimp, peeled
- Squid rings
- Mussels

Add seafood to the broth before the tofu. Clams need 3-4 minutes; shrimp need 2 minutes.

### Kimchi Sundubu (김치 순두부)

Add ½ cup well-fermented kimchi with the garlic, before adding broth. The kimchi's lactic acid enriches the spicy broth with sour complexity. One of the best combinations.

### Baechu Sundubu (배추 순두부)

With napa cabbage and pork belly. Add 50g thinly sliced pork belly with the gochugaru (it cooks in the spiced oil). Add napa cabbage cut into large pieces.

### Mushroom Sundubu (버섯 순두부)

Enoki, shiitake, king oyster mushrooms, or a combination. Vegetarian version with the same broth base using kombu-only dashi.

## Adding the Tofu

**Sundubu packaging:** Sold in soft tubes at Korean grocery stores. When adding to the pot, push the tube against the side of the pot and cut sections directly into the broth — don't scoop. This preserves the curd structure.

Add sundubu after the protein base is mostly cooked. Simmer **2-3 minutes only** — just enough to warm through. Longer cooking breaks down the curd structure.

## The Egg

Crack one egg per portion over the surface of the bubbling stew just before serving. It cooks from the residual heat in the dolsot — typically left slightly runny at the yolk, which enriches the broth when broken.

## The Dolsot

The stone pot (dolsot) is integral. Its ability to hold heat means the stew continues to cook slowly through the meal. The bottom layer of rice pressed against a hot dolsot develops a crispy crust (*nurungji*) — equally beloved.

If you don't have a dolsot, a small cast-iron pot or clay pot is the closest substitute.

## The Rice Ritual

Every sundubu set comes with a bowl of plain white rice. After finishing the stew, pour any remaining broth over leftover rice in the dolsot, press it together, and eat the rice soaked in the broth. This is not optional.

---

Sundubu jjigae at a Korean restaurant, still actively bubbling when it arrives at the table, with the raw egg gliding over the surface — it's one of the most satisfying sensory experiences in Korean food. The home version requires only a dolsot, gochugaru, sundubu, and anchovy broth.
