**Short answer:** buy kimchi from the **refrigerated case**, look for **napa cabbage, gochugaru, garlic and fish sauce or salted shrimp** on the label (or a vegan version if you need it), and check the date. Eat it fresh as a side dish; once it turns sour, cook with it. **Jongga** is a widely available Korean brand, and many grocery stores also carry good local makers.

---

## How to Read the Jar

- **Refrigerated, not shelf-stable.** Kimchi in the fridge case is usually live and fermenting. Shelf-stable jars are often pasteurized, which stops fermentation and changes the texture.
- **Ingredients.** Traditional napa cabbage kimchi uses gochugaru, garlic, ginger, scallion and a seafood seasoning (fish sauce or salted shrimp). Vegan kimchi replaces the seafood. Some brands add sugar or vinegar for a quicker sour taste; traditional kimchi gets its sourness from fermentation.
- **Bulging lid?** That's normal: fermentation makes gas. Open the jar over the sink.

→ [Shop Jongga kimchi on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=jongga+kimchi&tag=borderlesskitchen-20) · [Shop vegan kimchi on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=vegan+kimchi+refrigerated&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Refrigerated kimchi doesn't always ship well. If your local Korean or Asian grocery carries it, buying there is usually fresher.

## Fresh vs Sour: Use Both

| | Fresh kimchi | Sour (aged) kimchi |
|---|---|---|
| Taste | Crunchy, bright, spicy, a little sweet | Tangy, softer, deeper |
| Best for | Eating as a side dish (*banchan*) | Cooking |
| Classic dishes | With rice, grilled meat | [Kimchi stew](/journal/what-is-kimchi-jjigae-korean-kimchi-stew-guide), [kimchi fried rice](/journal/kimchi-fried-rice-recipe), kimchi pancakes |

The same jar does both: eat it fresh in the first week or two, then cook with it as it sours.

---

## Kinds to Know

- **Baechu kimchi:** the napa cabbage kimchi most people mean.
- **Kkakdugi:** cubed radish kimchi, crunchy and juicy. See [kkakdugi](/journal/kkakdugi-korean-radish-kimchi-guide).
- **Mul kimchi (water kimchi):** mild and refreshing, eaten with its brine.
