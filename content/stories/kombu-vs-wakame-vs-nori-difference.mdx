**Short answer:** kombu is thick dried kelp used to make stock. Wakame is a soft seaweed you eat, in miso soup and seaweed salad. Nori is a thin pressed sheet used to wrap sushi, onigiri and kimbap. They aren't interchangeable.

---

## Side by Side

| | Kombu | Wakame | Nori |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | Dried kelp | Dried leafy seaweed | Pressed, roasted sheets of laver |
| Looks like | Thick, stiff, dark green strips | Shrivelled dark flakes that swell in water | Thin, crisp, almost black sheets |
| Job | Flavor: the base of dashi | Eaten: soup, salad | Wrapper: sushi, onigiri, kimbap |
| Korean name | Dasima | Miyeok | Gim |
| Eaten as is? | Usually removed from the stock | Yes, after soaking | Yes |

## Kombu

Kombu is where dashi gets its savory depth. Soak it in cold water, then heat gently and lift it out before the water boils. Boiled kombu turns the stock slimy and bitter. The white powder on the surface is flavor, not mould: wipe the kombu with a dry cloth, don't wash it. The Korean anchovy broth starts with the same piece of kelp; see [dashi vs Korean anchovy broth](/journal/dashi-vs-korean-anchovy-broth).

## Wakame

Sold dried in small pieces. It expands to many times its size in water, so start with a small pinch. It's the seaweed in miso soup and in Korean birthday soup, [miyeok guk](/journal/miyeok-guk-korean-birthday-seaweed-soup), and the base of seaweed salad.

→ [Shop dried wakame on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=dried+cut+wakame+seaweed&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

## Nori (Gim)

Seaweed pressed into paper-thin sheets and roasted. Japanese nori is what wraps sushi and [onigiri](/journal/onigiri-types-fillings-guide-japanese-rice-balls). Korean gim is usually thinner, and the snack version is brushed with sesame oil and salt. For rolling [kimbap](/journal/korean-kimbap-guide) or sushi, buy plain, unseasoned sheets.

## What to Buy First

- **For soup and stock:** kombu. One pack lasts for months.
- **For miso soup or seaweed salad:** wakame. A small bag goes a long way.
- **For sushi, onigiri or kimbap:** a pack of plain roasted nori. Keep it sealed, because humidity makes it chewy.

All three keep for a long time, sealed and away from moisture. For more on stock, see the [complete dashi guide](/journal/japanese-dashi-complete-guide).
