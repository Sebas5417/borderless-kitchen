Flan's origins trace to ancient Rome — eggs and milk cooked together in a mold was a Roman preparation — and the dish traveled through medieval Europe, reaching Spain, and then to Latin America and the Philippines through colonization. The word "flan" is the Spanish and French term for custard tart.

The Latin American version is distinct from French crème caramel primarily in the use of sweetened condensed milk, which produces a richer, denser, more intensely flavored custard than the milk-and-sugar versions common in Europe. This is the version most people encounter in Mexican and Cuban restaurants.

## The Caramel

Caramelizing sugar for flan involves cooking sugar (usually with a small amount of water) to 170–180°C — beyond the point where it's golden and before it burns. The caramel needs to be:

**Dark enough:** Too light and it's sweet without complexity; the slight bitterness of a properly dark caramel balances the sweet custard.

**Not burned:** Burned sugar is acrid and ruins the dessert. The line between properly dark caramel and burned caramel is about 5 seconds and a few degrees — stay attentive.

**Poured immediately:** Caramel sets very quickly. As soon as it reaches the right color, pour it into the mold and tilt quickly to coat the bottom. Do not return it to heat; it will continue caramelizing from residual heat.

## The Custard Ratio

Mexican flan typically uses a combination of:
- Sweetened condensed milk (1 can/400g) — sweetener and dairy, very rich
- Evaporated milk (1 can/350ml) — thins the richness slightly
- Eggs (whole eggs + sometimes extra yolks) — set the custard
- Vanilla

The eggs are what makes the custard set. Too few eggs: custard won't set firmly and will weep. Too many eggs: rubbery, eggy-tasting custard. 3 whole eggs to 800ml of dairy is the range.

Blending the custard base (rather than whisking) produces a smooth, bubble-free mixture that bakes more evenly. Strain through a fine-mesh sieve after blending to remove any egg chalazae (white stringy parts) and foam.

## The Baking Method

Flan is baked in a bain-marie (water bath). The water distributes heat gently and prevents the edges from overcooking before the center sets.

**Temperature:** 160°C (320°F) is the target. Too hot and the edges will be rubbery and curdled; too cool and it will take too long to set, potentially weeping.

**Doneness:** The flan is done when the edges are set but the center still has a slight wobble — it should jiggle like set Jell-O, not slosh like liquid. A toothpick inserted 2cm from the edge should come out clean; the very center may still be slightly underdone (it will finish setting as it cools).

**Cooling:** Cool completely at room temperature, then refrigerate at least 4 hours (overnight is better) before unmolding. The custard continues to set and firm during cooling.

## Unmolding

Run a thin knife carefully around the edge of the flan. Place a serving plate (with a slight lip to catch the caramel) over the mold. Flip quickly and decisively. Hold for a moment — you will hear the caramel release. Lift the mold.

The caramel that was on the bottom (now on top) will pour down the sides of the custard.

**Recipe: Classic Mexican Flan (Serves 8, one 20cm round pan)**

**Caramel:**
- 200g sugar
- 60ml water

**Custard:**
- 1 can (400g) sweetened condensed milk
- 1 can (350ml) evaporated milk
- 3 large eggs
- 2 teaspoons vanilla extract

**Method:**

1. **Preheat oven to 160°C.** Have a 20cm round cake pan or individual ramekins ready.

2. **Caramel:** Combine sugar and water in a heavy saucepan over medium heat. Do not stir; swirl the pan if needed. Cook until the mixture turns a deep amber color. Remove from heat immediately; pour into the pan(s). Tilt to coat the bottom. Set aside while you make custard.

3. **Custard:** Combine condensed milk, evaporated milk, eggs, and vanilla in a blender. Blend 30 seconds until smooth. Strain through a fine-mesh sieve.

4. Pour custard carefully over the set caramel.

5. **Bain-marie:** Place the flan pan in a larger roasting pan. Add hot water to the roasting pan until it reaches halfway up the sides of the flan pan.

6. Bake 45–55 minutes until edges are set and center wobbles gently. Test with a toothpick near the edge — it should come out clean.

7. Remove from water bath; cool to room temperature. Refrigerate at least 4 hours, preferably overnight.

8. **Unmold:** Run a knife around the edge. Place a serving plate over the pan; flip quickly. Allow the caramel to run down the sides before lifting the pan.
