Mee rebus occupies a particular category of Malaysian hawker food: dishes that are recognizably neither purely Malay nor purely Chinese nor purely Indian but something produced by the interaction of all three. The yellow wheat noodles are Chinese (Hokkien); the spice paste (galangal, lemongrass, dried chilies) is Malay; the thickening technique with a starchy tuber and the combination of sweet and spicy may reflect Indian influence — the dish is associated particularly with the Johor state in the south and with Malay communities who adapted Chinese noodle form to a Malay-spiced sauce.

The sweetness of the gravy is real and deliberate. This is not a background sweetness — it is a foreground characteristic, balanced against the heat from dried chilies and the savory depth of the shrimp paste. New eaters sometimes register the sweetness as unexpected; within Malaysian cooking, sweet-savory combinations of this kind are standard.

The garnishes are not optional — they are part of the dish. The hard-boiled egg provides protein and richness; the fried tofu cubes absorb the gravy; the bean sprouts add crunch; the green chili and lime add freshness and acidity that the gravy (which is all warmth and depth) needs.

## The Gravy: Construction and the Sweet Potato Thickener

**The spice paste (*rempah*):** Blended together before frying:
- Shallots (or red onion)
- Garlic
- Galangal (fresh or dried)
- Dried red chilies (rehydrated)
- Lemongrass (white part only)
- Shrimp paste (*belacan*)

The rempah is fried in oil until fragrant and the raw smell is gone — this takes 10–15 minutes over medium heat, not 2–3. The patience here matters: insufficiently fried rempah produces a raw, sharp taste.

**The sweet potato:** Cooked (boiled or steamed) sweet potato, mashed until smooth, added to the gravy to thicken it. The sweet potato provides both the thickening effect and the characteristic sweetness. The quantity determines the thickness — more sweet potato = thicker and sweeter; less = thinner and more broth-like.

**The broth:** Beef broth or chicken broth, added to the fried rempah and sweet potato; simmered together to develop flavor.

**The final balance:** Soy sauce for salt, palm sugar or brown sugar to reinforce sweetness, more dried shrimp or shrimp paste for depth.

## The Noodles

**Yellow noodles (*mee*):** Fresh or dried Chinese-style wheat noodles, yellow from the addition of kansui (alkaline solution) or egg. Thicker than rice vermicelli, similar in character to lo mein noodles. Available at Asian grocery stores.

**Preparation:** Blanched in boiling water until tender; drained; placed in the serving bowl before the gravy is poured over.

## The Complete Recipe

**Serves:** 4 | **Time:** 1 hour

### Spice Paste (Rempah)
- 8 shallots (or 1 medium red onion), roughly chopped
- 4 garlic cloves
- 3cm fresh galangal (or 1 teaspoon dried galangal powder)
- 8–10 dried red chilies, soaked in hot water 15 minutes
- 2 stalks lemongrass, white part only
- 1 teaspoon belacan (shrimp paste), toasted

### Gravy
- 3 tablespoons neutral oil
- Spice paste (from above)
- 300g orange sweet potato, peeled, boiled until soft, mashed smooth
- 800ml chicken or beef broth
- 2 tablespoons soy sauce
- 1 tablespoon palm sugar or brown sugar
- Salt to taste

### Noodles and Garnish
- 400g fresh yellow noodles (or 200g dried, cooked per packet)
- 4 hard-boiled eggs, halved
- 200g bean sprouts, blanched 30 seconds
- 150g firm tofu, cubed and deep-fried or pan-fried until golden
- 4 tablespoons fried shallots
- 4 green chilies, sliced thin
- 2 limes, quartered

### Method

**1. Make rempah:** Blend all spice paste ingredients until smooth, adding a splash of water if needed.

**2. Fry rempah:** Heat oil in a heavy pot over medium heat. Add rempah; stir-fry 12–15 minutes, stirring frequently, until darkened, fragrant, and the oil separates slightly at the edges. Be patient — this is the foundation of the gravy's flavor.

**3. Build gravy:** Add mashed sweet potato to the fried rempah; stir to combine. Add broth; stir well to dissolve the sweet potato into the liquid. Bring to a simmer. Add soy sauce, palm sugar, and salt; taste and adjust. Simmer 15–20 minutes until the gravy is thick and all flavors are integrated.

**4. Cook noodles:** Blanch yellow noodles in boiling water 1–2 minutes until tender; drain.

**5. Serve:** Place noodles in bowls; ladle generous amounts of gravy over. Top with egg halves, bean sprouts, fried tofu cubes, fried shallots, and sliced green chili. Add a lime wedge.

**Eat immediately** — the noodles soak up the gravy quickly.
