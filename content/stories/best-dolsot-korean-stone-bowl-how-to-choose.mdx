**Short answer:** buy a **granite stone dolsot about 16 to 18 cm across** (one generous serving) that comes with a **wooden or trivet base**. Season it with sesame oil before first use, heat it slowly, and oil the inside before the rice goes in. That's how you get the crust (*nurungji*).

---

## What a Dolsot Does

A *dolsot* is a heavy stone bowl that goes straight onto the burner. It heats slowly and holds that heat, so the rice pressed against the oiled sides fries into a golden, crackly crust while the bowl keeps sizzling at the table. Most dolsot sold for home use are carved granite; some are made of other stone or heavy earthenware.

---

## What to Look For

| | Look for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Material | Natural stone (granite is common) | Holds heat for a long, even crust |
| Size | 16–18 cm (about 6.5–7 in) for one serving | Larger bowls take longer to heat |
| Base | Wooden or heat-proof trivet included | The bowl is far too hot for a table |
| Lid | Optional | Useful for stews like *sundubu jjigae* |

Earthenware bowls (*ttukbaegi*) look similar and are sold for stews. They're lighter and good for soups, but stone gives the better rice crust.

## Seasoning a New Dolsot

Natural stone is porous. Before first use:

1. Wash it with warm water only (no soap) and dry it completely.
2. Rub the inside with sesame oil (or a neutral oil).
3. Heat it slowly on low, then medium, for several minutes until the oil darkens slightly. Turn off the heat and let it cool on its base.
4. Wipe out the excess oil.

Some makers recommend repeating this two or three times. Always follow the instructions that come with your bowl.

---

## How to Get the Crust

1. Put the dolsot on the burner and **heat it gradually**: low for a couple of minutes, then medium.
2. **Brush the inside with sesame oil.**
3. Add cooked [short-grain rice](/journal/best-short-grain-rice-for-japanese-and-korean-cooking) and press it gently against the sides. Arrange the toppings, finishing with an egg yolk or fried egg.
4. Cook on medium until you hear a steady crackle, a few minutes. Set it on its base and serve.
5. At the table, mix in [gochujang](/journal/best-gochujang-brands-how-to-choose) and let the bottom keep crisping for a minute before digging down.

## Care Rules

- **Don't heat it empty on high.** Heat gradually and always oil it first.
- **No cold water on a hot bowl.** Thermal shock can crack stone.
- **Wash with hot water and a brush,** not soap, which the stone can absorb. Dry it fully.
- **Check your cooktop.** Stone bowls are usually made for gas. Don't use one on glass, ceramic or induction tops unless the maker says it's suitable.
