Gigantes plaki belongs to the tradition of Greek ladera — dishes cooked with olive oil as a primary ingredient, not just a cooking medium. The olive oil is not used sparingly; a proper gigantes plaki uses 80–100ml for a standard baking dish, and this is not excessive. The oil is what gives the dish its richness, its silky texture, and its satisfaction as a complete meal rather than a side dish.

The dish is also part of the Greek tradition of oven-braising — long, slow cooking in a ceramic or earthenware vessel in the oven (or historically in the village bakery oven, where Greek cooks would leave their dishes to cook while the oven cooled from bread baking). The slow heat from all sides produces the caramelized top, the collapsed beans, and the deeply concentrated sauce that cannot be replicated on the stovetop.

## The Beans

**Gigantes** — Greek for "giants" — refers to a specific large white runner bean (Phaseolus coccineus), grown in northern Greece particularly around Kastoria. The beans are enormous compared to standard white beans — 2–3 times larger — and their size is part of what makes the dish: they hold their shape through the long bake while softening inside to a creamy consistency.

**Available at:** Greek grocery stores, specialty Mediterranean markets, and increasingly mainstream importers. Look for "gigantes" or "giant butter beans."

**Substitute:** Large dried butter beans or corona beans. Lima beans work at a pinch but are significantly smaller and softer.

## The Preparation

Dried gigantes must be soaked overnight — at least 12 hours — then boiled separately until just tender (not fully cooked) before baking. This pre-cook is not optional; raw dried beans in the tomato sauce would take far longer to soften and would prevent the sauce from concentrating correctly.

**Recipe: Gigantes Plaki (Serves 6 as meze, 4 as main)**

- 500g dried gigantes (giant white beans)
- 150ml extra-virgin olive oil
- 2 medium onions, diced
- 5 cloves garlic, sliced
- 400g canned crushed tomatoes
- 200g fresh tomatoes, diced (or additional canned)
- 2 tablespoons tomato paste
- 1 stalk celery, sliced
- 1 medium carrot, diced
- Large handful fresh parsley, roughly chopped
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano
- 1 teaspoon sweet paprika
- Salt and pepper
- Sugar: a pinch to balance tomato acidity

**Day before — soak beans:**
Cover with cold water by at least 10cm; soak 12+ hours. Drain.

**Day of — parboil beans:**
Cover soaked beans with fresh water; bring to a boil; skim foam. Simmer 40–50 minutes until beans are just tender but still holding shape firmly (slightly underdone — they'll finish in the oven). Drain.

**The sauce:**
1. Heat 3 tablespoons olive oil in a pan. Sauté onion and celery until soft; add garlic.
2. Add carrot; cook 3 minutes.
3. Add tomato paste; cook 2 minutes stirring.
4. Add crushed and fresh tomatoes, oregano, paprika, salt, pepper, and a pinch of sugar. Simmer 10 minutes.
5. Add most of the parsley; stir.

**Assembly and baking:**
1. Preheat oven to 180°C (350°F).
2. In a large ceramic or earthenware baking dish, combine drained beans and tomato sauce. Mix gently.
3. Pour remaining olive oil (roughly 100ml total in the dish) over everything.
4. Add enough water to come almost to the top of the beans.
5. Bake uncovered 60–80 minutes until sauce has thickened, top is slightly caramelized, and beans are completely tender and creamy inside.
6. Check every 20 minutes; add a little water if the sauce reduces too quickly before the beans are done.
7. Rest 15 minutes before serving. Scatter remaining fresh parsley over.

Serve at room temperature or warm, with crusty bread to absorb the sauce.
