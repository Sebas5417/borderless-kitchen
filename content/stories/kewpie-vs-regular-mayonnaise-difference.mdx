**Short answer:** Kewpie is made with egg yolks only and its own vinegar blend, and the Japanese-made version adds MSG. The result is richer, tangier and more savory than American mayonnaise. In most sandwiches you can use either. In Japanese dishes built around mayo, Kewpie is the taste you're expecting.

---

## Side by Side

| | Kewpie (Japanese) | Regular (American) mayo |
|---|---|---|
| Eggs | Yolks only | Whole eggs |
| Vinegar | Proprietary blend (apple and malt in Japan; distilled, red wine and rice in the US version) | Usually distilled white vinegar |
| Savory boost | MSG in the Japanese-made version; yeast extract in the US-made one | None |
| Texture | Thick, custardy | Lighter, fluffier |
| Taste | Rich, tangy, a little sweet, savory | Mild, neutral |
| Packaging | Soft squeeze bottle with a fine star tip | Jar or bottle |

---

## Kewpie

The yolks make it rich and custard-thick. The vinegar makes it tangier. The MSG (or yeast extract) gives it the savory depth that makes people describe it as "more flavorful" without being able to say why. The soft bottle and star tip are made for drizzling thin lines over okonomiyaki and takoyaki.

Check the label: the bottle imported from Japan and the one made for the US market differ slightly. The Japanese one contains MSG; the US one uses yeast extract instead. Both are recognisably Kewpie.

## Regular Mayo

Whole eggs make it lighter and milder. That neutrality is the point: it carries other flavors without adding much of its own. For a BLT, a tuna sandwich or a dressing where mayo is only the binder, it is the right choice.

## Where Kewpie Makes the Difference

- **[Okonomiyaki](/journal/japanese-okonomiyaki-recipe) and [takoyaki](/journal/japanese-takoyaki-guide):** drizzled in thin lines with sauce. Regular mayo tastes flat here.
- **[Japanese potato salad](/journal/japanese-potato-salad-recipe):** creamy, tangy, a little sweet. The recipe depends on Kewpie.
- **Egg sando:** the soft Japanese egg sandwich. Yolk-rich mayo is the flavor.
- **Dipping sauce for [karaage and katsu](/journal/japanese-tempura-vs-karaage):** Kewpie with a squeeze of lemon or a dab of mustard.
- **Spicy mayo:** Kewpie plus sriracha or gochujang is the sushi-bar version.

## If You Only Have Regular Mayo

Stir in a pinch of sugar and a few drops of rice vinegar. A tiny pinch of MSG or dashi powder gets you most of the rest of the way. It won't be as thick, but it's close enough for potato salad.

## What to Buy

One squeeze bottle of Kewpie keeps for weeks in the fridge once opened. Buy the standard one, not the reduced-fat version: the fat is where the richness comes from.
