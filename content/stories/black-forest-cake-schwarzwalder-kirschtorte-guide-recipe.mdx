Schwarzwälder Kirschtorte gets its name from the Black Forest (Schwarzwald) region of Baden-Württemberg in southwestern Germany — both because the sour cherries (Schattenmorellen) that fill it are associated with the region and because Kirschwasser, the clear cherry schnapps that saturates the sponge, is a Black Forest specialty.

The first documented recipe appears in 1934 in a German cookbook, though earlier versions existed. Germany protects the name: to legally be called Schwarzwälder Kirschtorte in Germany, the cake must contain Kirschwasser. Alcohol-free versions must use another name. This is a statement about the importance of the schnapps to the dessert — it is not a flavoring; it is a primary component.

## What Makes It Distinctive

The tension in the best Black Forest cake is between sweet and not-sweet:

**The sponge:** A genoise or Rührteig (butter sponge) with cocoa powder — deeply chocolate-flavored, light in texture. It must be soaked thoroughly with Kirschwasser syrup to become moist enough.

**The cream:** Whipped cream, slightly sweetened or unsweetened. The cream is not German buttercream or pastry cream — it is fresh whipped cream, and its barely-sweet quality is what prevents the whole cake from being cloying. The cream must be freshly whipped; stabilized cream or non-dairy substitutes change the balance.

**The cherries:** Sour cherries (Sauerkirschen/Morello cherries), not sweet maraschino cherries. The tartness of sour cherries against the cream and chocolate is the flavor architecture. In Germany, jarred sour cherries (Schattenmorellen) are standard. Outside Germany, Morello cherries in syrup are the appropriate substitute.

**The Kirschwasser:** Kirschwasser is a clear, unaged cherry brandy distilled from Morello cherries. It is dry, almost medicinal in purity, with an intensely cherry-flavored character. It soaks into the sponge and also goes into the cherry filling. There is no replacement — other cherry liqueurs are too sweet.

## The Assembly Structure

A proper Black Forest cake has three sponge layers. Between each layer: a circle of cream and cherries. The outside is frosted with cream, the cherries ring the top edge, and the surface is covered with chocolate shavings (not sprinkles — shaved from a block of bittersweet chocolate with a knife or vegetable peeler).

The structure produces a cross-section when cut: three dark layers, two cream-and-cherry layers, visible in the slice.

**Recipe: Schwarzwälder Kirschtorte (Serves 10–12, 24cm cake)**

**Chocolate sponge (baked in 2 or 3 layers):**
- 5 large eggs, room temperature
- 150g sugar
- 75g all-purpose flour
- 50g cornstarch
- 30g unsweetened cocoa powder
- 1 teaspoon baking powder
- 60g butter, melted and cooled

**Kirschwasser syrup:**
- 100ml water
- 60g sugar
- 80ml Kirschwasser

**Cherry filling:**
- 1 jar (680g) Morello cherries in syrup, drained (reserve liquid)
- 2 tablespoons Kirschwasser
- 1 tablespoon cornstarch dissolved in 2 tablespoons reserved cherry syrup

**Whipped cream:**
- 700ml heavy cream (36%+ fat)
- 2 tablespoons sugar
- 1 packet whipped cream stabilizer (optional, helps hold shape)

**Decoration:**
- 100g dark chocolate block, for shavings
- Additional whole cherries

**Method:**

1. **Sponge:** Preheat oven to 175°C. Line two 24cm round pans. Beat eggs and sugar with electric mixer 8–10 minutes until pale, thick, and tripled in volume. Sift together flour, cornstarch, cocoa, and baking powder; fold gently into egg mixture in two additions. Drizzle in melted butter; fold briefly. Divide between pans. Bake 20–25 minutes until a toothpick comes out clean. Cool completely. Slice each layer horizontally if needed to make 3 total layers.

2. **Kirschwasser syrup:** Boil water and sugar until dissolved; cool; add Kirschwasser.

3. **Cherry filling:** Heat reserved cherry syrup in a saucepan. Add cornstarch mixture; stir until thickened. Remove from heat; add drained cherries and Kirschwasser. Cool.

4. **Cream:** Whip cream with sugar (and stabilizer if using) to firm peaks. Refrigerate until needed.

5. **Assembly:**
   - Place first sponge layer on cake board. Brush generously with Kirschwasser syrup.
   - Spread a layer of cream. Spoon half the cherry filling over.
   - Place second layer. Brush with syrup. Cream + remaining cherries.
   - Place third layer. Brush with remaining syrup.
   - Frost the outside completely with cream.
   - Pipe cream rosettes around the top edge; place a whole cherry on each.
   - Create chocolate shavings: pull a vegetable peeler across a room-temperature chocolate block. Cover the top and sides with shavings.
   - Refrigerate 2–4 hours before serving.
