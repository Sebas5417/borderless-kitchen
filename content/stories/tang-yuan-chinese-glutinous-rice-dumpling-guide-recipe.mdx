The round is not incidental. Every element of tang yuan — the shape, the occasion, the name — circles back to the concept of reunion and wholeness. The Lantern Festival meal that centers on tang yuan is the formal close of the lunar New Year celebrations; the Winter Solstice meal that also centers on tang yuan marks the turning of the year back toward light. Both occasions are about the family gathering that was initiated or restored; the round dumplings are the edible expression of that circle.

The northern Chinese term *yuanxiao* (元宵) refers to both the Lantern Festival and to a version of the same filled rice dumpling that is made by a rolling method (the filling is formed into balls and rolled in dry glutinous rice flour repeatedly, building up layers) rather than the southern *tang yuan* method (a dough is made and filled by wrapping, like a filled dumpling). The flavors are similar; the textures differ slightly; the cultural importance is identical.

The black sesame paste filling is the most widely preferred — intensely dark, nutty, slightly sweet, and oily (from the sesame), with a texture that is smooth and liquid-like when hot (the fat in the sesame melts when the dumpling is cooked). When a hot tang yuan is bitten into and the black sesame flows out, this is the desired experience: the contrast between the chewy exterior and the liquid, intensely flavored interior.

## The Dough: Glutinous Rice Flour

**Glutinous rice flour (*nuòmǐ fěn*, 糯米粉):** The only ingredient in the dough beyond water. Glutinous rice flour (from short-grain glutinous rice) becomes elastic, chewy, and translucent when hydrated and cooked — unlike wheat flour dough, it has no gluten but has starch that binds and stretches. The water temperature matters: hot water makes a more pliable dough; room-temperature water makes a firmer dough. A small amount of cooked dough (a pinch of the raw dough cooked briefly in boiling water) kneaded back into the batch — the *fan shú* technique — improves elasticity.

**The dough:** 200g glutinous rice flour + 120ml warm water = a smooth, soft, non-sticky dough. It should feel like soft playdough — pliable and smooth.

## The Black Sesame Filling

The black sesame paste filling requires fully toasted sesame seeds and enough fat to make the filling liquid when hot:

- 100g black sesame seeds, toasted until fragrant
- 50g sugar (or to taste)
- 30–40g lard (traditional) or softened butter or coconut oil
- Pinch of salt

Blend sesame seeds until a fine powder; combine with sugar, fat, and salt; mix until a dough-like paste forms that holds a shape when cold but liquefies when heated. Refrigerate to firm before using.

## The Assembly: Forming Perfect Spheres

**Portion filling:** Approximately 8–10g of filling per dumpling. Keep filling refrigerated and firm — it must hold its shape during wrapping.

**Wrap:** Take a portion of dough (approximately 18–20g); flatten into a disc with the thumb in the center to create a thin shell; place filling in center; gather the edges up and pinch closed; roll gently between both palms to form a perfect sphere. No seam should be visible.

**The sphere:** Must be perfectly round and smooth. Any irregularity means the dumpling may burst during cooking.

**Keep covered:** Formed dumplings dry out quickly; keep under a damp cloth.

## The Complete Recipe

**Makes:** 20 dumplings | **Time:** 1 hour

### Dough
- 200g glutinous rice flour
- 120ml warm water (50–60°C)

### Black Sesame Filling
- 100g black sesame seeds, toasted
- 50g white sugar
- 35g lard (or softened butter)
- Pinch of salt

### Sweet Broth
- 800ml water
- 60g rock sugar or white sugar
- 3 slices fresh ginger
- 2 tablespoons dried osmanthus flowers (optional)

### Method

**1. Make filling:** Process toasted sesame seeds in a food processor or blender until very fine powder. Add sugar, lard, and salt; process until combined into a sticky, uniform paste. Refrigerate 30 minutes until firm. Form into 20 small balls (approximately 8g each); refrigerate.

**2. Make dough:** Mix glutinous rice flour with warm water; knead until smooth and soft (2–3 minutes). If too dry, add water a teaspoon at a time. Cover.

**3. Form dumplings:** Divide dough into 20 pieces. Flatten each into a disc; fill with one sesame ball; pinch closed; roll into a smooth sphere. Keep under a damp cloth.

**4. Cook:** Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Add dumplings in batches (do not overcrowd). They sink first; when they float to the surface and stay floating (2–3 minutes), they are cooked. Remove with a slotted spoon; transfer to serving bowls.

**5. Make broth:** Simmer water with rock sugar, ginger, and osmanthus until sugar dissolves; strain. Pour hot broth over the dumplings in each bowl.

**Serve:** Immediately, while the filling is liquid and hot. The broth is delicate — the focus is on the dumpling.
