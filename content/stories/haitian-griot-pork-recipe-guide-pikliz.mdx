Haitian cuisine is one of the most underrepresented great Caribbean cooking traditions — complex, deeply flavored, with a history that runs through West African cooking, French colonial technique, and a cuisine developed in complete isolation after Haiti's 1804 revolution made it the first free Black republic.

Griot (pronounced "gree-oh," borrowing the West African word for storyteller/keeper of history) is this tradition's centerpiece dish. The pork preparation is a two-stage process that produces what fried pork aspires to be: a long braise that renders fat and deeply tenderizes the meat, followed by a hard fry that creates exterior crunch and mahogany color without drying the interior.

The pikliz is not a condiment — it is a requirement. The sour heat of pikliz is the counterweight to pork's fat; without it, the dish is incomplete. Pikliz is also Haiti's universal condiment, appearing alongside everything from griot to fritay (street food), but it was designed for griot.

## The Pikliz

Make pikliz at least 24 hours in advance; it improves over 3–5 days. Keeps refrigerated for months.

- 1/2 head cabbage, very thinly shredded
- 2 carrots, julienned
- 1 small bell pepper (any color), julienned
- 4–8 Scotch bonnet peppers, thinly sliced (adjust heat level)
- 4 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
- 1/2 small onion, thinly sliced
- 1 cup white or cider vinegar
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 10 peppercorns, cracked

Method: Combine all vegetables. Pack tightly into a jar. Pour vinegar over everything; it should cover or nearly cover the vegetables. Add salt and peppercorns. Seal; refrigerate 24 hours minimum before using.

## The Pork Marinade

Epis is the Haitian flavor base — a blended marinade of:
- Green onions
- Parsley
- Bell pepper
- Garlic
- Scotch bonnet (quantity to taste)
- Citrus (sour orange juice or lime)
- Thyme
- Cloves
- Salt and black pepper
- A little oil

Blend to a coarse paste. This is the marinade that goes on the pork.

**Recipe: Griot (Serves 6)**

**For the pork:**
- 1.5 kg pork shoulder, bone-in preferred, cut into 6–8 large chunks
- Juice of 2 sour oranges (or 1 orange + 2 limes)
- 4 green onions, sliced
- 1/2 green bell pepper
- 4 cloves garlic
- 1 Scotch bonnet, whole or sliced (adjust heat)
- Small bunch fresh parsley
- 3 sprigs fresh thyme
- 2 whole cloves
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1 teaspoon black pepper
- Oil for frying

**Method:**

1. Blend the marinade: combine citrus juice, green onions, bell pepper, garlic, Scotch bonnet, parsley, thyme, cloves, salt, and pepper in a blender; pulse to a coarse paste.

2. Rub paste thoroughly over pork pieces. Marinate refrigerated at least 4 hours; overnight is ideal.

3. Place pork with all its marinade in a deep pan or Dutch oven. Add 1/2 cup water. Cover; cook over medium-low heat 45–60 minutes, turning occasionally, until pork is very tender and most liquid has evaporated. The pork should be cooked through; some of its fat will have rendered.

4. Remove pork pieces. Allow to cool slightly if handling by hand.

5. Heat 2–3 inches of oil in a deep pan to 175°C (350°F). Fry pork in batches, 4–5 minutes per batch, turning once, until exterior is deep mahogany-brown and crispy.

6. Drain on paper towels. Season with salt immediately.

**To serve:** Griot with pikliz, rice and red beans or rice with sauce pois (black mushroom sauce), and fried plantains (banane pesée).
