Cullen is a small harbour village on the Moray Firth coast of northeast Scotland, an area historically known for its cold-smoking tradition — cold smoked haddock in particular, which became known as Finnan haddie (named after Findon, another Aberdeenshire fishing village). The cold-smoking tradition developed partly as preservation (smoked fish kept longer than fresh) and partly because the resulting flavor was extraordinary: the pale gold, delicate smoke of cold-smoked haddock is entirely different from aggressively smoked meats.

Cullen skink was originally a simple fisherman's dish — the smoking liquid from the haddock, the potatoes that grew readily in the north, the milk and butter that dairy farming provided. Today it is one of Scotland's signature dishes, appearing at formal dinners, country house hotels, and village pubs alike.

## The Smoked Haddock

**Cold-smoked haddock is the only appropriate fish.** Not salmon. Not hot-smoked anything. Cold-smoked haddock (Finnan haddie or un-dyed smoked haddock) has a pale yellow color from the smoke without artificial dye, and a delicate, clean smoke flavor.

**Avoid:** Artificially bright yellow "smoked haddock" dyed with food coloring (common in supermarkets) — this is cheaper but has a chemical quality. Natural un-dyed cold-smoked haddock is the correct ingredient and is widely available from fishmongers and better supermarkets.

The smoked haddock is poached first in milk and/or water; the resulting poaching liquid, now golden-hued and deeply flavored with smoke and fish, is the base of the soup.

## The Potato

Floury (starchy) potatoes work best — Maris Piper, Rooster, or King Edward in the UK; Russet in the US. The potatoes should partially break down to thicken the soup naturally; no additional thickener is needed if the right potato is used and some pieces are allowed to break.

**Recipe: Cullen Skink (Serves 4)**

- 400g cold-smoked haddock fillets (skin on)
- 600ml whole milk
- 400ml fish stock or water
- 500g floury potatoes, peeled and diced (1cm cubes)
- 1 medium onion, finely diced
- 50g unsalted butter
- 150ml double cream (heavy cream)
- Salt and white pepper
- Fresh parsley, chopped (for garnish)
- Crusty bread to serve

**Method:**

1. Place smoked haddock skin-side down in a wide pan. Pour over the milk. Bring slowly to a gentle simmer; poach 8–10 minutes until the fish is cooked through and flaking.

2. Remove fish with a slotted spoon; reserve the poaching milk. Remove and discard the skin; flake the fish into large pieces, checking for bones.

3. In a large pot, melt butter over medium heat. Add onion; cook gently 8–10 minutes until soft and translucent. Do not brown.

4. Add diced potato. Add the reserved poaching milk and fish stock (or water). Season lightly with salt (the smoked fish already provides significant salt) and white pepper.

5. Simmer 15–18 minutes until potatoes are tender. Using a potato masher, roughly mash about a quarter of the potatoes against the side of the pot — this thickens the soup while leaving most potatoes in chunks.

6. Add cream; stir gently. Add flaked smoked haddock. Heat through gently (do not boil after adding cream; it will split).

7. Taste; adjust salt and white pepper.

8. Serve with chopped parsley over the top and crusty bread or oatcakes.

*Cullen skink should be thick — more of a chowder than a thin broth. If too thin, continue to simmer gently until reduced to a satisfying consistency.*
