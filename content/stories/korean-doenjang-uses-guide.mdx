Most Western cooks who buy doenjang use it once for doenjang jjigae (fermented soybean paste stew) and then leave the container in the back of the refrigerator. This is an underutilization of one of the most versatile fermented ingredients in East Asian cooking.

Doenjang keeps indefinitely in the refrigerator — the fermentation that created it continues to preserve it. There is no reason not to use it daily.

## The 15 Applications

### Korean Applications

**1. Doenjang Jjigae (된장찌개):**
The classic. Anchovy-kelp broth, doenjang dissolved in broth, tofu, zucchini, mushrooms. See the jjigae article for full technique. This is the foundational use — but it shouldn't be the only one.

**2. Ssamjang (쌈장):**
The paste inside Korean BBQ ssam wraps. Mix 2 tbsp doenjang + 1 tbsp gochujang + 1 tbsp sesame oil + 1 tsp honey + 1 clove garlic + 1 scallion. This is the dipping component that makes the entire ssam work. Without doenjang, ssamjang is not ssamjang.

**3. Doenjang Marinade for Meat:**
Mix 1 tbsp doenjang + 1 tbsp soy sauce + 1 tbsp sesame oil + 1 tsp gochugaru + 2 cloves garlic. Use as a paste marinade on pork belly or beef short rib before grilling. The doenjang's enzymes tenderize the meat protein and the fermented depth adds complexity to the grill.

**4. Doenjang Dip for Raw Vegetables (쌈채소):**
Small amounts of doenjang mixed with sesame oil served alongside raw vegetables — perilla, lettuce, cucumber, carrot — for dipping. A snack or banchan component.

**5. Doenjang Juk (된장죽, rice porridge):**
Add 1-2 tbsp doenjang to plain congee-style rice porridge with vegetables. The fermented paste adds depth to what would otherwise be a very mild dish.

**6. Ganjang Doenjang Sauce for Tofu:**
Mix doenjang + ganjang (soy sauce) + sesame oil + garlic + scallion. Pour over silken cold tofu. A faster, bolder alternative to the Japanese hiyayakko.

### Cross-Cultural Applications

**7. Doenjang Butter (Korean Compound Butter):**
Mix 1 tbsp doenjang into 100g softened butter. Use on grilled steak, corn, roasted potatoes, or spread on bread. The fermented soybean depth transforms the butter in the same way miso butter does — but bolder. Works particularly well on grilled corn.

**8. Doenjang Vinaigrette:**
Whisk 1 tsp doenjang + 2 tbsp rice vinegar + 1 tbsp sesame oil + 1 tsp honey + 1 tsp soy sauce. Dress green salads or grain bowls. The doenjang replaces anchovy paste or Dijon as the umami-savory element that makes a dressing stick.

**9. Doenjang Pasta:**
Replace or supplement miso in miso-butter pasta recipes with doenjang. The result is deeper, more funky, and more complex — closer to an aged cheese depth than the cleaner miso version.

**10. Doenjang Shakshuka:**
Add 1 tbsp doenjang to the tomato base of shakshuka before adding eggs. The fermented paste melds with the tomato acidity to create a Korean-Middle Eastern hybrid that works extraordinarily well. The eggs cook in this deeper base.

**11. Doenjang Soup Base (Non-Korean):**
Dissolve 1-2 tbsp doenjang in any vegetable or chicken broth as an umami booster. Provides the same function as miso in a miso soup base but with a more complex, earthier character. Useful in ramen-style broths, French onion soup broth (replaces Worcestershire), or beef stew base.

**12. Doenjang Glaze for Fish:**
Mix 1 tbsp doenjang + 1 tbsp mirin + 1 tsp sesame oil + 1/2 tsp ginger. Brush on fish (salmon, cod, mackerel) before baking or grilling. The fermented paste caramelizes and creates a crust.

**13. Doenjang in Burger / Meat Patty:**
Work 1/2 tbsp doenjang into the beef mixture before forming burgers. The fermented protein deepens the meat flavor and adds umami without being identifiable as "Korean" in the finished burger.

**14. Doenjang Mayonnaise:**
Mix 1 tsp doenjang into 3 tbsp Japanese Kewpie mayo. Use as a dip for fried food, a sandwich spread, or a sauce for grain bowls. The combination creates a deeply savory aioli-like condiment.

**15. Doenjang in Braising Liquid:**
Add 1-2 tbsp doenjang to any braising liquid for pork shoulder, short ribs, or lamb. The paste dissolves into the braise and contributes fermented depth throughout the 2-3 hour cooking time. The result is a braise that has the background complexity of a long-aged fermented ingredient.

---

Doenjang's versatility comes from its core properties: it provides salt, umami (from the amino acids produced in fermentation), and the specific complex background note of Aspergillus mold fermentation — the same set of properties that makes miso, aged parmesan, and anchovy paste useful in cooking. The Korean version is bolder and more complex than most miso, making it valuable wherever deep, savory, fermented flavor needs to be added quickly.
