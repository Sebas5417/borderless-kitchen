Café Du Monde opened in the French Market in New Orleans in 1862 and has served beignets and café au lait continuously since — through the Civil War, Prohibition, and Hurricane Katrina. It operates 24 hours a day, seven days a week. It does not serve alcohol. It serves three things: beignets, café au lait (coffee with steamed chicory milk), and orange juice.

The beignet (pronounced "ben-YAY" in New Orleans — not the French "ben-YEH," which gets you marked as a tourist) arrived in Louisiana with French Creole settlers in the 18th century. The word simply means "fritter" in French. The New Orleans version — made from a yeast-and-flour dough fried in hot oil and covered in powdered sugar — is distinct from other regional American doughnuts and has been an inextricable part of New Orleans culture and tourism for over 150 years.

In 1986, Louisiana designated the beignet the official state doughnut.

## What Makes Them Different

New Orleans beignets are distinguished from most fried doughnuts by:

**The dough:** Beignet dough contains both yeast (for lift and flavor) and evaporated milk (for richness and browning). It is softer and more slack than a typical doughnut dough, producing a lighter interior after frying.

**The shape:** Squares, not rings. Cut into roughly 5cm squares from rolled dough, then dropped into hot oil.

**The finish:** Powdered sugar, applied with a shaker in quantities that would strike other pastry cultures as excessive. The powdered sugar sticks to the hot fried dough. You will inhale some of it. This is considered part of the experience.

**The service:** Always hot, always fresh from the fryer. Never pre-made. This is why the service at Café Du Monde is so fast — there is essentially no prep time for an individual order because the beignets are fried continuously.

## The Chicory Coffee

The traditional pairing with beignets is café au lait made with chicory coffee. Chicory (the root of the chicory plant, roasted and ground) was added to coffee in New Orleans during the Civil War when coffee was scarce. It became permanent because the resulting blend — slightly bitter, woodsy, less caffeinated — was found to pair particularly well with the sweetness of beignets and rich Creole food.

Community Coffee and Café Du Monde brand chicory coffee blends are the standard for authentic café au lait.

**Recipe: New Orleans Beignets (Makes about 24)**

**Dough:**
- 7g (1 packet) active dry yeast
- 120ml warm water (40–43°C — warm but not hot)
- 60ml evaporated milk, room temperature
- 1 egg, room temperature
- 50g granulated sugar
- 1/4 teaspoon salt
- 30g unsalted butter, melted and cooled
- 350g all-purpose flour, plus more for dusting
- Vegetable oil or shortening for deep frying

**To serve:**
- 200g powdered (icing) sugar
- Fine-mesh sieve for dusting

**Method:**

1. **Activate yeast:** In a large bowl, sprinkle yeast over warm water. Add 1 teaspoon of the sugar. Let sit 5–10 minutes until foamy. (If it doesn't foam, the yeast is dead — start over with fresh yeast.)

2. **Make dough:** Whisk evaporated milk, egg, remaining sugar, salt, and melted butter into the yeast mixture. Add flour in two additions, stirring until a soft, sticky dough forms. The dough will be shaggy and slightly sticky.

3. **Knead:** Turn out onto a floured surface; knead 3–4 minutes until smooth and elastic. The dough is softer than bread dough — flour your hands, not the dough excessively.

4. **First rise:** Place in a lightly oiled bowl, cover with plastic wrap, and refrigerate for at least 1 hour (or overnight up to 24 hours). Cold fermentation develops flavor and makes the dough easier to handle.

5. **Roll and cut:** On a floured surface, roll dough to about 5mm thickness. Cut into 5×5cm squares using a pastry cutter or sharp knife. Reroll scraps once.

6. **Fry:** Heat oil or shortening in a heavy-bottomed pot to 180°C (use a thermometer — temperature control matters). Fry beignets in small batches, 3–4 at a time, turning once. They cook 1–2 minutes per side until puffed and golden brown. They should puff and become hollow immediately upon hitting the oil.

7. **Drain:** Remove with a slotted spoon to a paper-towel lined tray briefly.

8. **Sugar:** Immediately (while hot) transfer to a serving plate and bury in powdered sugar sieved from above. Serve immediately.

*Beignets do not keep well — they must be eaten within minutes of frying. Make them to order, not in advance.*

**Café au lait to accompany:** Brew strong chicory coffee. Heat equal parts milk and coffee together (or steam milk). Combine in a mug — half coffee, half hot milk.
