**Short answer:** if you want tagliatelle, lasagne sheets and ravioli, buy a **hand-crank roller** (the Marcato Atlas 150 is the classic). If you already own a KitchenAid stand mixer, the **roller attachment** does the same job with both hands free. Buy an **electric extruder** only if you mainly want tubes and spaghetti-style shapes.

## Side by Side

| | Hand-crank roller | Stand mixer attachment | Electric extruder |
|---|---|---|---|
| Makes | Sheets, ribbons (tagliatelle, fettuccine), ravioli | Same as hand-crank (roller/cutter set) | Extruded shapes: spaghetti, penne, macaroni |

## Hand-Crank Roller

Short of a rolling pin, this is the tool most Italian home cooks reach for. You knead the dough by hand, then feed it through smooth rollers at progressively thinner settings. A cutter on the side turns the sheet into ribbons. The **Marcato Atlas 150**, made in Italy, is the standard: steel body, ten thickness settings, and a clamp for the counter edge.

Care matters. **Don't wash a roller in water.** Let leftover dough dry, then brush it off. Water gets into the rollers and can rust them.

## Stand Mixer Attachment

KitchenAid sells roller and cutter attachments that fit the mixer's power hub. They work like the hand-crank machine, but the motor turns the rollers, so you have one hand to feed and one to catch the sheet. That makes long sheets for lasagne much easier on your own.

→ [Shop KitchenAid pasta roller attachments on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=kitchenaid+pasta+roller+cutter+attachment&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

## Electric Extruder

An extruder mixes flour and liquid in its bowl, then pushes the dough through a shaped die. It's the easiest home option for tube shapes like penne or bucatini. (KitchenAid also sells a separate pasta-press extruder attachment if you already own the mixer.) The dough is drier and the pasta denser than rolled egg pasta. Good, but it's a different pasta. It won't make lasagne sheets or ravioli.

## The Dough That Works

For rolled pasta, the classic ratio is **100 g of "00" flour per large egg**. One egg's worth is roughly one generous serving.

1. Mound the flour, crack the eggs into a well, and mix with a fork, then by hand.
2. Knead 8 to 10 minutes until smooth. If it's sticky, add flour a spoonful at a time; if it cracks, wet your hands.
3. Wrap and rest 30 minutes at room temperature. This step matters more than the machine.
4. Roll from the widest setting down, folding and re-rolling a couple of times at the start for a smoother sheet.
