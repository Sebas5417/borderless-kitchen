Galbi (갈비) — Korean for "rib" — is one of Korean cuisine's most celebrated ingredients. Short ribs prepared Korean-style span a range of preparations: grilled over charcoal, braised in sweet soy sauce, served raw for the diner to grill at the table, prepared as soup. Each preparation produces a completely different eating experience from the same cut of meat.

Understanding the landscape of galbi is more useful than mastering any single recipe, because what you're eating (or ordering) depends on knowing the preparation.

---

## The Cuts

### LA Galbi (LA 갈비)

**What it is:** Beef short rib cut *across* the bone — a thin cross-section cut that includes 3-4 bone circles in each piece. The cut is 3-4mm thin, flanked by meat on each side of the bone sections.

**Origin of "LA":** This cut was developed in Los Angeles by Korean-American butchers for easy grilling — the thin cross-cut exposes more meat to direct heat. It's now widely used in Korea despite the American origin.

**Marinade:** The most commonly marinated galbi. Standard LA galbi marinade includes soy sauce, sugar or Asian pear (the enzymes in pear tenderize the meat and break down protein), garlic, sesame oil, green onion, and sesame seeds. Marinated 4-24 hours.

**Grilling:** Grilled over high direct heat, 2-3 minutes per side. The thin cut means rapid cooking; the sugars in the marinade caramelize to produce char. One of the most crowd-pleasing Korean BBQ items.

---

### Flanken-Style Galbi (갈비, 본 갈비)

**What it is:** The same beef short rib but cut *along* the bone rather than across it — each piece contains one whole rib bone with meat attached on either side, cut approximately 1.5cm thick.

**The traditional cut:** This is how galbi was originally prepared in Korea before the LA-cut innovation. The thicker cut requires longer cooking and can be grilled or braised.

**Saeng galbi (생갈비, raw/unmarinated galbi):** High-quality flanken-style galbi served raw without marinade, for the diner to grill directly. The quality of the beef is primary — premium Korean hanwoo (한우) or high-grade imported beef. Dipped in sesame oil and salt after grilling.

---

### Beef Short Rib Comparisons

| Type | Cut direction | Thickness | Typical prep | Cooking time |
|------|--------------|-----------|--------------|--------------|
| LA galbi | Cross-bone | 3-4mm | Marinated, grilled | 4-6 min |
| Flanken galbi | Along bone | 1-1.5cm | Marinated or saeng, grilled | 8-12 min |
| Galbi jjim (braising cut) | Thick chunks | 4-5cm | Braised | 40-60 min |

## Pork Galbi (돼지 갈비, Dwaeji Galbi)

Pork ribs prepared in the same tradition as beef galbi — grilled or braised, with a similar soy-based or gochujang-based marinade.

**Differences from beef galbi:**
- Pork ribs cook faster than beef (similar thin-cut methods)
- Often use a spicier gochujang-based marinade (the sauce stands up better to pork fat)
- Less expensive than beef, more casual context

**Dwaeji galbi gui (돼지갈비구이):** Grilled pork ribs — very common at Korean BBQ restaurants. Often served as a lower-cost alternative to beef galbi.

---

## Galbi Gui (갈비구이) — Grilled Galbi

The classic preparation. Marinated short ribs grilled over charcoal (*soot-bul*, 숯불) or gas grill, eaten with the standard Korean BBQ accompaniments:

- *Ssam* (쌈): Lettuce, sesame leaves (kkaennip) for wrapping
- *Ssamjang* (쌈장): Fermented soybean paste dipping sauce
- *Banchan*: Various side dishes
- *Kimchi*: Always present at the table
- Rice (white short-grain)

**The charcoal difference:** Charcoal-grilled galbi has a smoky depth that gas grilling cannot replicate. The high heat of soot-bul produces superior caramelization of the sugary marinade. Korean galbi restaurants that specify soot-bul are indicating this preparation.

**Eating galbi:** Use scissors to cut pieces from the bone if needed; eat in a ssam wrap or directly.

---

## Galbi Jjim (갈비찜) — Braised Galbi

A completely different preparation from galbi gui. Thick-cut beef short ribs braised in a sweet soy sauce with Asian pear, garlic, ginger, vegetables (daikon, carrot, potatoes), and chestnuts or jujube dates.

**Cultural significance:** Galbi jjim is celebratory food — Chuseok, Seollal, birthdays, significant family meals. The labor-intensive preparation (hours of braising) signals that someone has invested significant care in the meal.

**The Asian pear in jjim:** Korean and Chinese pears contain *actinidin* (an enzyme similar to papain) that tenderizes meat protein. Added to the marinade and used in the braising liquid, they allow a very deep tenderness that hours of slow braising enhances.

**Recipe characteristics:**
- Short ribs blanched first (removes blood and impurities)
- Marinated overnight in soy, pear, sugar, garlic
- Braised 40-60 minutes in marinade + water + vegetables
- Sauce reduced to a glaze
- Honey or sugar added at the end to finish the glaze

**For the complete galbi jjim recipe:** See the [Galbi Jjim braised short ribs guide](/journal/korean-galbi-jjim-braised-short-ribs-guide).

## Galbi Tang (갈비탕) — Short Rib Soup

Short ribs slow-simmered in clear broth — a milky, collagen-rich soup from long bone extraction. The ribs are removed from the bone after cooking; meat and broth are served together.

**Characteristics:**
- Clear to milky-white broth from collagen extraction
- Very long cooking (2-4 hours) for tender meat and rich broth
- Seasoned simply: soy sauce, garlic, salt — no gochugaru
- Eaten as a restorative meal, especially for recovery from illness or cold weather

*Galbi tang* is a separate restaurant category from galbi gui restaurants — the preparations, atmosphere, and clientele are entirely different.

---

## Restaurant Navigation

**"갈비" (galbi) on a menu indicates:**
- LA galbi 갈비: marinated grilled thin-cut — the standard
- 생갈비 (saeng galbi): raw, premium, unmarinated galbi
- 돼지갈비 (dwaeji galbi): pork ribs
- 갈비탕 (galbi tang): soup preparation

**Price indicators:** Saeng galbi is typically the most expensive (the quality of raw beef is exposed); LA galbi is mid-range; dwaeji galbi is usually the most affordable.

---

Galbi is less a single dish than a framework for understanding how Korean cuisine applies completely different techniques to the same source ingredient: the same short rib can be marinated and grilled in 6 minutes, braised for 4 hours as a celebratory dish, or simmered into a restorative soup. Each preparation exists in its own cultural context; knowing which is which prevents the bewilderment of ordering by mistake.
