**Short answer:** genuine San Marzano tomatoes carry the **DOP** mark and the name **"Pomodoro San Marzano dell'Agro Sarnese-Nocerino"**. They are sold peeled, whole or in fillets. A can that only says "San Marzano style" or "San Marzano variety" is not DOP. It may still be a good tomato, but you shouldn't pay a DOP price for it.

---

## What DOP Means

DOP (*Denominazione d'Origine Protetta*, the EU's Protected Designation of Origin) is a legal name tied to a place and a production rule. San Marzano DOP tomatoes must be grown in a defined area of the Sarno River valley in Campania, across parts of the provinces of Salerno, Naples and Avellino. They must also be the San Marzano type of plum tomato and be processed under the consortium's rules.

San Marzano is also the name of a tomato **variety**. Seeds are sold worldwide, so a tomato can be "San Marzano" by variety and grown anywhere. That is legal, and it is not DOP.

---

## Three Checks Before You Buy

1. **The full DOP name and the DOP logo.** Look for "Pomodoro San Marzano dell'Agro Sarnese-Nocerino" and the red-and-yellow EU DOP seal.
2. **The consortium mark and a certification number.** Genuine cans normally also carry the protection consortium's logo and a numbered certification label.
3. **Whole or fillets, peeled.** DOP San Marzano is sold as whole peeled tomatoes or fillets (*filetti*). If the can says crushed, diced, puréed or sauce, it isn't DOP San Marzano.

Wording such as "San Marzano style", "San Marzano type", "Italian San Marzano" or "grown in the USA" on its own means you're not looking at DOP.

## Are They Worth It?

For a sauce built on the tomato, like a plain marinara or the base of an [amatriciana](/journal/amatriciana-guide-recipe-roman-pasta-guanciale-tomato), a good San Marzano gives a sweeter, less acidic, meatier sauce. In a long-cooked ragù or a dish with lots of other flavors, the difference shrinks. A well-made can of whole peeled plum tomatoes, from Italy or California, does the job for much less.

Whatever you buy, **choose whole peeled over crushed**. Whole tomatoes tend to be the better fruit, and you control the texture. Crush them by hand or pass them through a food mill.
