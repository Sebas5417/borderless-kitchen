The Cuban sandwich was invented in Florida, not Cuba. Tampa's Cuban immigrant community, centered in the Ybor City neighborhood, was making pressed sandwiches by the late 1800s — when the neighborhood was home to Cuban cigar workers, along with Spanish and Italian immigrants. Miami's Cuban community developed its version later, driven by the 1959 Cuban refugee exodus.

This explains the single greatest controversy in Cuban sandwich history: Tampa includes Genoa salami; Miami does not. The Tampa version reflects the Ybor City mix of Cuban and Italian immigrant workers who shared food and ingredients. The Miami version reflects a more direct lineage from Havana's mixto sandwich.

In 2012, Tampa officially designated the Cuban sandwich the city's "signature sandwich." In 2016, it was named the "official sandwich of Tampa Bay" by the Tampa Bay Times. Miami considers this provincial.

## The Bread

Cuban bread is essential and not easily substituted. It is:
- A white bread made with lard (which gives it a flaky, tender crust)
- Shaped into long loaves (similar in shape to a baguette but wider and flatter)
- Scored with a palm frond or similar leaf before baking, which creates a slight ridge down the top
- Soft interior, thin crust — not a baguette-level crunch

Outside Tampa and Miami, Cuban bread is difficult to find. A close substitute: a soft Italian hoagie roll, or a French baguette that is not too crusty. (Too crusty means the pressing cracks the bread rather than compresses it.)

## The Layers

**Tampa-style (the "original"):**
1. Cuban bread, split
2. Yellow mustard on both sides
3. Ham (baked deli ham)
4. Roast pork (mojo-marinated, slow-roasted, thinly sliced)
5. Genoa salami
6. Swiss cheese
7. Dill pickles (bread-and-butter pickles are NOT traditional)

**Miami-style:**
Same as Tampa, minus the salami.

**What does NOT go in a Cuban:** lettuce, tomato, mayonnaise, hot sauce, avocado, onion. These additions exist at some restaurants but are not traditional.

## The Roast Pork (Mojo-Marinated)

The roast pork is what separates a great Cuban from a mediocre one. Traditional "lechón asado" uses a mojo marinade:

- Fresh orange juice + lime juice (Cuban oranges are sour; navel orange juice + lime juice approximates this)
- Garlic (lots — 8–10 cloves per pork shoulder)
- Olive oil
- Dried oregano, cumin, salt

The pork marinates 4–24 hours, then roasts low and slow until tender enough to slice thin or pull. It is not barbecue-style and does not have a sweet glaze or bark — the mojo gives a bright, garlicky, citrus flavor.

## The Press

The sandwich is pressed in a plancha — a flat-top grill press, essentially. The pressing flattens the sandwich by about half and achieves two things:
1. The cheese melts completely
2. The bread crisps and turns golden from the grill surface contact

A cast-iron skillet with another heavy skillet or pan pressed on top works at home. A panini press works but doesn't get quite as flat or even.

**Recipe: Tampa-Style Cuban Sandwich (Makes 2)**

**Mojo roast pork (make ahead):**
- 500g pork shoulder, trimmed
- Juice of 2 navel oranges + 2 limes
- 6 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano
- 1 teaspoon cumin
- Salt and black pepper

Marinate pork 4+ hours; roast at 160°C for 2–3 hours until tender. Let cool; slice thin.

**Assembly:**
- 2 sections of Cuban bread (25–30cm each), split
- Yellow mustard
- 200g baked ham, thinly sliced
- Mojo roast pork, thinly sliced
- 4 slices Genoa salami
- 4 slices Swiss cheese
- Dill pickle slices (from a jar, drained)
- Butter for the press

**Method:**

1. Spread yellow mustard on both cut sides of the bread.

2. Layer: ham, roast pork, salami, Swiss cheese, dill pickles.

3. Close sandwich.

4. Heat a cast-iron skillet or flat-top grill over medium-high heat. Butter the outside of the sandwich lightly.

5. Place sandwich in the pan; press firmly with another heavy pan or press. Weight it down.

6. Cook 3–4 minutes per side, pressing firmly, until bread is golden and cheese is completely melted.

7. Cut diagonally. Serve immediately.
