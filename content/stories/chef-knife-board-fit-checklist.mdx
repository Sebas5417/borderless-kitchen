If you’re choosing between a 6-inch and an 8-inch chef’s knife, start with your counter. A knife that suits your board, hand and normal prep will get used more often than one you have to rearrange the kitchen to use.

## Run a three-minute fit check

1. **Set up the board you actually use.** Put it where you normally prep, with a stable, clear work area. Set a small bowl nearby for chopped ingredients and see how much board remains.
2. **Try a familiar dinner with your current knife.** An onion, some herbs and a bell pepper are enough to tell you whether you want more room for longer cuts or more control in short ones. If the knife drags or crushes where it used to slice cleanly, sharpening may solve the problem.
3. **Check the handle and the board together.** If you can try a candidate in a shop, hold it as you would at home. Look for comfortable handle clearance while the edge is on a board, and enough room to work with the blade and food fully over the cutting surface.

## Use the result to narrow your choices

- **Choose a shorter blade** if you prep on a small board, work in a tight space, or feel more comfortable with a shorter reach.
- **Try an 8-inch blade** if your board has room and you regularly slice larger ingredients or batch-prep vegetables.
- **Keep your current knife** if it feels good and the only issue is a dull edge. Check the manufacturer’s sharpening advice or use a sharpening service.
- **Start with one knife** if you’re replacing a general-purpose blade. Add a second when a specific, recurring task calls for it.
