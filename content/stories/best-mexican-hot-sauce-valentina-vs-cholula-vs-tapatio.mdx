**Short answer:** if you buy one bottle, buy **Valentina**. It's thick, tangy, only moderately hot, and inexpensive, and it's the classic Mexican snack sauce. Keep **Cholula** for eggs and anything delicate, **Tapatío** if you want a little more kick, and **El Yucateco** green habanero when you want real heat.

---

## Side by Side

| | Style | Heat | Best on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Valentina** (orange label) | Thick, less vinegary | Mild–medium | Chips, fruit, popcorn, jícama, tortas |
| **Valentina Black Label** | Same sauce, hotter | Medium | Same, with more heat |
| **Cholula** | Thinner, spiced, uses árbol and piquín chiles | Mild | Eggs, tacos, pizza |
| **Tapatío** | Thin, garlicky | Medium | Tacos, soups, eggs |
| **El Yucateco** (green habanero) | Thin, bright, habanero | Hot | Seafood, cochinita pibil, anything that needs heat |

Valentina and Cholula are made in Mexico, in Jalisco. Tapatío is made in California, though it's named after the word for people from Guadalajara.

## Hot Sauce vs Salsa

Bottled table sauce is a vinegar-based condiment that keeps for months. **Salsa** is usually fresh or cooked chiles, tomato or tomatillo, onion and cilantro, made to eat within days. A good taco gets salsa; snacks and eggs get hot sauce. To make your own salsas, start with the dried chiles in [ancho vs guajillo vs pasilla](/journal/ancho-vs-guajillo-vs-pasilla-dried-chiles).
