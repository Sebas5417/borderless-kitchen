Prinsesstårta was created in the 1920s by Jenny Åkerström, a home economics teacher and cookbook author who ran a school for Swedish princesses. Her cookbook "Prinsessornas kokbok" (The Princesses' Cookbook) included the cake, which was a favorite of three of her royal students — Princesses Margaretha, Märtha, and Astrid. The cake was eventually named after them.

Sweden has a "Princess Cake Week" (Prinsessårka-veckan) in the last week of September, during which Swedes consume hundreds of thousands of prinsesstårtor nationwide. It is as close as any country comes to a nationally mandated dessert.

The cake's defining characteristics — the green marzipan dome, the hidden interior of cream and jam, the pink rose — have remained largely unchanged since the 1920s. It is a study in controlled elegance: the exterior is simple and identifiable; the interior is a reward for cutting.

## The Components

**Sponge (génoise):** Light, dry, egg-based sponge without butter — the lightness allows the cream to be the dominant texture. Three layers.

**Vanilla custard cream (crème pâtissière):** Thick, set custard made with egg yolks, sugar, milk, and vanilla. Not whipped cream — proper custard.

**Raspberry jam:** A thin layer between the bottom sponge and the custard. The jam's acidity balances the rich cream.

**Whipped cream:** A dome of firmly whipped cream encases the custard and jam. This cream dome creates the shape that the marzipan will cover.

**Marzipan:** A thin layer of pale green marzipan rolled to cover the entire dome. Dyed with a tiny amount of green food coloring. The Swedish commercial standard is a very pale, delicate green — not forest green.

**Pink marzipan rose:** Made from pink-tinted marzipan, placed on top. This is not negotiable.

**Recipe: Prinsesstårta (Serves 10–12)**

**Sponge:**
- 5 eggs
- 150g caster sugar
- 130g all-purpose flour
- 1 tablespoon potato starch (or cornstarch)

**Vanilla custard:**
- 4 egg yolks
- 60g caster sugar
- 25g cornstarch
- 300ml whole milk
- 1 vanilla pod (or 1 teaspoon vanilla extract)
- 100ml whipping cream, whipped to soft peaks and folded in when custard is cold

**Whipped cream dome:**
- 500ml cold whipping cream (35%+ fat)
- 2 tablespoons icing sugar

**Assembly:**
- 3–4 tablespoons raspberry jam
- 2 tablespoons simple syrup for moistening sponge

**Marzipan covering:**
- 400g marzipan
- Small amount of green food coloring (gel preferred)
- Pink food coloring for the rose
- Icing sugar for dusting

**Method — Sponge:**

1. Preheat oven to 180°C. Line a 24cm round cake tin.
2. Beat eggs and sugar for 8–10 minutes until very pale, thick, and tripled in volume.
3. Sift flour and potato starch over the egg mixture; fold in gently in three additions.
4. Pour into prepared tin; bake 20–25 minutes until a skewer comes out clean.
5. Cool completely; slice into three horizontal layers.

**Method — Custard:**

1. Heat milk with vanilla in a saucepan until nearly simmering.
2. Whisk egg yolks, sugar, and cornstarch together.
3. Pour hot milk into yolk mixture gradually, whisking. Return to saucepan.
4. Cook over medium heat, stirring constantly, until custard thickens. Boil 1 minute.
5. Cool to room temperature; refrigerate until cold. Fold in whipped cream.

**Assembly:**

1. Place first sponge layer on a serving plate. Brush with simple syrup. Spread with raspberry jam. Spread with a thin layer of custard.

2. Add second sponge layer; brush with syrup. Spread remaining custard in an even layer.

3. Add third sponge layer; brush with syrup.

4. Whip cream with icing sugar to firm peaks. Build a dome shape over the cake with the whipped cream — higher in the center, tapering to the edges. Refrigerate 30 minutes to firm up.

5. Knead green food coloring into the marzipan until evenly colored — pale green. Roll out to a large circle (3mm thick).

6. Drape marzipan over the cream dome; smooth gently, tucking edges under the cake.

7. Make a small pink marzipan rose; place on top.

8. Dust with icing sugar.

9. Refrigerate until serving. Slice with a wet knife.
