The Tigris River flows through Baghdad, and along its banks, masgouf restaurants have operated for millennia. Diners select a live carp from tanks or displays; the fish is scaled, butterflied open (the head left attached, the fish opened flat like a book along the spine), marinated, and then propped vertically on stakes beside a wood fire — not over the fire, but beside it, absorbing the slow, aromatic heat for 1–2 hours.

The technique is pre-Islamic in origin — ancient Mesopotamian texts describe grilled fish preparations along the rivers. Masgouf appears in Baghdad records as early as the Abbasid Caliphate (8th–13th centuries). Through every subsequent period of Iraqi history — Ottoman, British mandate, monarchy, republic — masgouf restaurants along the Tigris continued to serve the same preparation.

The dish is deeply connected to place. The carp from the Tigris and Euphrates have a specific flavor from the riverine environment; the fragrant wood smoke (typically citrus or pomegranate wood near Baghdad) perfumes the fish over the long, slow cook; and the riverside setting is part of the experience. Masgouf made anywhere else is a translation; masgouf in Baghdad is the original.

## The Carp

**Common carp (Cyprinus carpio):** The fish used for masgouf. Can be raised in freshwater and is available at some Asian grocery stores or specialty fish markets that serve Middle Eastern communities. A 1.5–2 kg whole fish is ideal.

**If carp is unavailable:** Other whole freshwater fish — striped bass, tilapia, or large trout — can approximate the dish. The technique translates to any firm-fleshed whole fish.

## The Butterflying

The fish is butterflied open — cut from the inside along the backbone, then opened flat. The head stays attached. The fish goes skin-side down and flesh-side toward the fire. This is a very specific preparation; the fish is not filleted.

## The Marinade

Traditional masgouf marinade:
- Olive oil
- Tamarind paste (gives tartness and color)
- Turmeric
- Salt
- Sometimes: coriander, cumin, dried lime (loomi), chili

Applied to the flesh side only; the skin remains relatively unmarinated.

## The Cooking

The traditional method: wooden stakes propped in the ground at an angle beside a wood fire (not directly over it). The fish sits skin-side-up in the stakes; the heat comes from beside and below. This indirect heat cooks the fish slowly and evenly.

At home: a charcoal grill with indirect heat (coals on one side, fish on the other) approximates the technique. Oven roasting is a distant approximation.

**Recipe: Masgouf (Adapted for Home, Serves 4)**

- 1 whole carp or large freshwater fish, 1.5–2 kg, butterflied and backbone removed (ask your fishmonger)
- 4 tablespoons olive oil
- 2 tablespoons tamarind paste
- 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
- 1 teaspoon ground coriander
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- Optional: 1 dried lime (loomi), ground

**Traditional accompaniments:**
- Grilled tomatoes and onions
- Fresh flatbread (pita or Iraqi khubz)
- Sliced fresh tomatoes and onions
- Lemon wedges
- White rice

**Method:**

1. Combine olive oil, tamarind paste, turmeric, coriander, salt, pepper, and ground dried lime (if using) into a marinade paste.

2. Score the flesh side of the fish in a diamond pattern to allow marinade penetration.

3. Brush the flesh side generously with the marinade. Reserve a little for basting. Let marinate 30 minutes at room temperature.

4. Set up charcoal grill for indirect cooking — coals banked to one side.

5. Place fish on grill, skin-side down, on the indirect (cool) side. Cover; cook 45–60 minutes depending on thickness. The fish should cook through slowly; the skin should become crispy and the flesh opaque.

6. After 30 minutes, baste flesh with remaining marinade.

7. When the flesh is fully cooked (opaque throughout, flakes easily), move fish directly over coals for 5–10 minutes skin-side-down to crisp the skin.

8. Serve on a large platter surrounded by grilled tomatoes, onions, and fresh bread. Provide lemon wedges.

*Oven adaptation: 180°C for 35–45 minutes, then 5 minutes under the broiler to crisp the skin. Less complex in flavor but produces a good approximation.*
