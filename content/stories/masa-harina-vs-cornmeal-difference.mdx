**Short answer:** masa harina is corn that has been cooked in an alkaline lime solution (nixtamalized), then dried and ground. Cornmeal is plain dried corn, ground. Nixtamalization changes the corn so it forms a dough with water. Cornmeal won't hold together, so you can't make tortillas, tamales or pupusas with it.

---

## Side by Side

| | Masa harina | Cornmeal |
|---|---|---|
| Process | Corn cooked and soaked in lime water (nixtamalized), dried, ground | Dried corn, ground |
| Texture | Fine, soft flour | Gritty; fine, medium or coarse |
| Add water and you get | A pliable dough | Wet sand |
| Taste | Distinct tortilla flavor | Sweet, plain corn |
| Used for | Tortillas, tamales, pupusas, gorditas, thickening pozole | Cornbread, polenta, muffins, breading |

## Masa Harina

Nixtamalization is the step that matters. Soaking corn in lime water loosens the hull, changes the corn's proteins and starches so the flour binds into dough, and adds the flavor everyone recognizes as "corn tortilla". It also makes the corn more nutritious. Why this mattered historically is in [Corn, Two Passports](/journal/corn-two-passports-korean-oksusu-and-mexican-masa).

Mix masa harina with warm water and a pinch of salt, rest it, and press it into tortillas. A spoonful also thickens [enchilada sauce](/journal/enchiladas-mexican-guide-recipe-red-green-mole-sauce), chili or pozole and gives it a toasted-corn flavor.

There is also a coarser masa harina made specifically for tamales. Regular masa harina works for tamales too.

## Cornmeal

Ground dried corn with nothing else done to it. The grind decides the use: fine for cornbread and muffins, coarse for polenta and grits. It's excellent at what it does. It just can't become dough.

A naming trap: in the US, "corn flour" usually means very finely ground cornmeal. In the UK, "cornflour" means cornstarch. Neither is masa harina.

---

## Can You Swap Them?

- **Cornmeal for masa harina:** no, for anything that has to hold together as dough.
- **Masa harina for cornmeal:** sometimes, in small amounts. It makes cornbread denser and gives it a tortilla flavor.
