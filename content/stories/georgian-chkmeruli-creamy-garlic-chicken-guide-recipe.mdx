Georgian cuisine is one of the most underappreciated great culinary traditions — a cooking philosophy built around walnuts, pomegranate, tarragon, fenugreek, and the herb mix khmeli suneli, and a conviction that anything is improved by more garlic. Chkmeruli takes that last principle to its logical extreme.

The dish originates in Racha, a mountainous region in northwest Georgia known for its particularly rich dairy products (particularly the local cream). The original preparation uses creamy, fatty Rachan dairy; the modern version uses heavy cream or a mix of cream and milk. The garlic is measured in heads, not cloves.

Chkmeruli is typically cooked and served in a ketsi — a shallow Georgian clay pot that goes directly from fire to table, retaining heat and continuing to cook the sauce as it sits. At home, any shallow oven-safe skillet or sauté pan works; a clay dish adds atmosphere.

## The Garlic

Raw garlic, not roasted or cooked down, is the cornerstone. The garlic is crushed (not minced — crushed releases more allicin and produces a different texture in the sauce) and added to the cream. A brief cook mellows the sharpest edge but the sauce should remain intensely garlicky — this is not a dish for garlic-shy cooks.

A typical Georgian chkmeruli uses 1 full head of garlic (10–12 cloves) for one chicken. This is not a typo.

## The Chicken

Spatchcocked — backbone removed, opened flat — for even cooking and maximum skin contact with the pan surface. This technique is central to chkmeruli; whole chicken would not develop the same crust.

**Recipe: Chkmeruli (Serves 4)**

- 1 whole chicken (1.3–1.5 kg), spatchcocked
- 1 head garlic (10–12 cloves), peeled and crushed (or very roughly chopped)
- 250ml heavy cream (or mix: 150ml cream + 100ml whole milk for slightly lighter version)
- 50g butter
- Salt and black pepper
- Vegetable oil for searing

**Optional:** Fresh coriander (cilantro) for garnish

**Method:**

1. Season the spatchcocked chicken generously on both sides with salt and black pepper.

2. Heat 2 tablespoons oil in a large heavy skillet or oven-safe sauté pan over medium-high heat. Add chicken skin-side down.

3. Cook undisturbed for 12–15 minutes, pressing occasionally with a spatula, until the skin is deeply golden and crispy. The pan will render a significant amount of fat from the chicken.

4. Flip chicken; cook 5 minutes on the flesh side.

5. Transfer to oven at 200°C (400°F); roast 15–20 minutes until juices run clear and internal temperature reaches 75°C (165°F) at the thickest part.

6. While chicken roasts: drain excess fat from the skillet, leaving about 1 tablespoon. Add butter; melt over medium heat. Add crushed garlic; cook 2–3 minutes, stirring, until fragrant (do not brown the garlic — it should stay pale).

7. Add cream; bring to a simmer. Season with salt. Cook 3–4 minutes until sauce thickens slightly and coats the back of a spoon.

8. Return roasted chicken to the sauce in the pan. Spoon sauce over the chicken. Optional: return to oven for 5 minutes to integrate.

9. Serve directly from the pan (ketsi-style) with crusty bread to mop the sauce, and fresh coriander if desired.

*The sauce should be thick with garlic and cream, slightly separated in the best way — pools of butter, white cream, and crushed garlic visible. Georgian bread (shoti or lavash) is the correct accompaniment for the sauce.*
