The Hong Kong street vendor with a gai daan zai mold works with two hinged cast-iron or aluminum plates, each containing a grid of small hemispherical molds. The plates are heated over charcoal or a gas flame; the batter is ladled in; the mold is closed; it is flipped once during cooking. The result comes out in perhaps three minutes: a golden oval of small connected domes that crack slightly at the top with a satisfying crispness and reveal soft, slightly eggy interiors.

The original serving is always the same: the vendor rolls the waffle into a half-moon or cone shape immediately after removing it from the mold (while it is still pliable enough to fold), and the customer holds the cone and eats from the outside inward. There is no plate. The charcoal versions are the most fragrant and most praised; the gas versions (the majority today) produce a similar texture but different aroma.

The batter is the dish's soul. It is richer in egg than a Belgian waffle batter, producing the eggy flavor and the tender interior pocket; it is thinner to flow into the mold's hemisphere shapes; and it typically contains evaporated milk (giving additional richness and slight caramel sweetness) and a small amount of custard powder (giving yellow color and vanilla-custard character in the Cantonese tradition).

## The Batter: What Makes It Distinctive

**Eggs:** The proportion is higher than most waffle batters — 2–3 eggs per 200g flour. This produces the eggy richness that gives the waffle its name and character.

**Evaporated milk:** The standard Cantonese addition — gives creaminess and slight sweetness without thinning the batter too much.

**Custard powder:** A small amount of Bird's Eye custard powder (or vanilla custard powder) — gives yellow color and a faint vanilla-custard background flavor. Characteristic of Hong Kong-style sweets (milk tea, egg tarts, polo buns).

**Sugar:** The batter is lightly sweet — not a dessert sweetness but a mild background sweetness.

**Oil:** A small amount in the batter; additional oil brushed on the mold prevents sticking.

**Wheat flour + cornstarch or tapioca starch:** The combination gives a crispier shell than all-wheat flour.

## Modern Variations

The global bubble waffle explosion has produced flavors far beyond the original plain:
- **Matcha** (green tea)
- **Black sesame**
- **Chocolate**
- **Taro**
- **Pandan**
- **Ice cream cone format** — the waffle folded into a cone and filled with ice cream scoops

The Hong Kong purist tradition prefers the plain original — egg, evaporated milk, light sugar, eaten hot from the hand.

## The Complete Recipe

**Makes:** 4 waffles | **Time:** 30 minutes

### Ingredients
- 200g all-purpose flour
- 30g cornstarch
- 2 tablespoons custard powder
- 1 teaspoon baking powder
- Pinch of salt
- 3 large eggs
- 150ml evaporated milk
- 80ml water
- 80g sugar
- 2 tablespoons neutral oil (plus more for the mold)
- ½ teaspoon vanilla extract

### Equipment
- Egg waffle / bubble waffle iron (cast iron or aluminum mold), or bubble waffle maker (electric versions available)

### Method

**1. Make batter:** Whisk together flour, cornstarch, custard powder, baking powder, and salt. In a separate bowl, beat eggs with sugar until sugar dissolves; add evaporated milk, water, oil, and vanilla; whisk. Combine wet and dry ingredients; whisk until smooth with no lumps. The batter should be thin and pourable. Rest 15 minutes.

**2. Heat mold:** Place the egg waffle mold over medium-high heat on the stovetop; heat both sides until hot. Brush both sides generously with neutral oil.

**3. Pour:** Pour 3–4 tablespoons of batter into one half of the mold (the bottom); close the mold. Flip immediately.

**4. Cook:** Cook over medium heat 2 minutes; flip again; cook 1–1.5 minutes more until golden on all the pocket domes and the edges are set. Open mold slightly to check color.

**5. Remove:** Gently remove waffle from mold; fold immediately into a half-moon or cone while still pliable and warm.

**Serve:** Immediately, hot. It softens rapidly as it cools.
