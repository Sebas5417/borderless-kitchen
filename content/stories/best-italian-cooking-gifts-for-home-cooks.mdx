Italian home cooking is mostly about a few ingredients being the real thing. Good tomatoes, real Parmigiano, pasta with a rough surface, olive oil that still tastes of olives. The tools matter less, but the few that do matter make a big difference: a pasta roller, a way to get a home oven hot enough for pizza, a proper grater.

Each item below changes something you can taste or see on the plate.

---

## Under $25: The Pantry

### San Marzano DOP tomatoes

→ [Shop San Marzano DOP tomatoes on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=san+marzano+dop+tomatoes&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

The tomato most Italian sauces are built on, and one of the most widely faked Italian foods. Real ones say "Pomodoro San Marzano dell'Agro Sarnese-Nocerino DOP" and carry the DOP seal and a consortium number. A few cans make a small gift; a case makes a generous one. How to spot the fakes is in [San Marzano tomatoes: how to buy the real thing](/journal/san-marzano-tomatoes-dop-how-to-buy).

### Bronze-die pasta from Gragnano

→ [Shop bronze-die pasta di Gragnano on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=pasta+di+gragnano+igp+bronze+die&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Pasta pushed through bronze dies has a rough, chalky surface that holds sauce; most supermarket pasta is extruded through Teflon and comes out smooth. Gragnano, near Naples, has made pasta for centuries and its pasta carries an IGP. A few bags in different shapes are an easy gift, and the difference shows up the first time it's tossed with sauce.

### Carnaroli rice

→ [Shop Carnaroli risotto rice on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=carnaroli+risotto+rice&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

The risotto rice many Italian cooks prefer over Arborio: it holds its shape better and is harder to overcook. A bag of Carnaroli is a small upgrade that anyone who makes risotto will notice. The full comparison is in [Arborio vs Carnaroli vs Vialone Nano](/journal/arborio-vs-carnaroli-vs-vialone-nano-risotto-rice).

### Colatura di alici

→ [Shop colatura di alici on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=colatura+di+alici+cetara&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Amber anchovy sauce from Cetara on the Amalfi Coast, made by layering anchovies with salt and collecting the liquid. Look for "Colatura di Alici di Cetara DOP" on the label. It's Italy's fish sauce: a few drops over spaghetti with garlic and oil is a classic Christmas Eve dish on that coast. It's a good gift for a cook who thinks they already have every Italian ingredient.

## $25–$60: The Tools

### Microplane rasp grater

→ [Shop Microplane rasp graters on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=microplane+premium+classic+zester+grater&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

The best way to grate hard cheese at the table. It turns Parmigiano into a fluffy snow that melts into pasta instead of clumping, and it zests lemons without the bitter pith. Pair it with a wedge of cheese for a complete gift.

### A wedge of Parmigiano Reggiano

→ [Shop Parmigiano Reggiano wedges on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=parmigiano+reggiano+24+month+wedge&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Buy a wedge with the rind on and the pin-dot "Parmigiano Reggiano" lettering visible, never pre-grated. 24 months is the all-round age. The rind goes into soups and broths, which is half the value. Our guide to [Parmigiano Reggiano vs Parmesan](/journal/parmigiano-reggiano-vs-parmesan-how-to-buy) shows what to look for on the label. If you're shipping it, choose a seller that ships it cold.

### Moka pot

→ [Shop Bialetti Moka Express pots on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=bialetti+moka+express&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

The stovetop coffee maker in most Italian kitchens, introduced by Alfonso Bialetti in 1933 and almost unchanged since. It makes strong, concentrated coffee rather than true espresso. The 3-cup and 6-cup sizes are the most useful; "cups" here are small espresso cups, not mugs.

### Chitarra or gnocchi board

→ [Shop pasta chitarra on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=pasta+chitarra+guitar+cutter&tag=borderlesskitchen-20) · [Shop gnocchi boards on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=gnocchi+board+wooden&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Two small, traditional wooden tools. The chitarra is a frame strung with wires; you press a sheet of fresh pasta through it to make spaghetti alla chitarra, as they do in Abruzzo. A gnocchi board is a ridged paddle that gives gnocchi the grooves that hold sauce. Both are inexpensive, beautiful, and unlike anything they already own.

## $60 and Up: The Heirlooms

### Manual pasta roller

→ [Shop Marcato Atlas 150 pasta machines on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=marcato+atlas+150+pasta+machine&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

The hand-cranked roller is the gift that turns someone into a fresh-pasta cook. The Marcato Atlas 150 is made in Italy and is the standard choice; it rolls sheets for lasagna and ravioli and cuts fettuccine and tagliolini. If they own a KitchenAid stand mixer, the roller attachment is the alternative. Which to choose is in our [pasta maker guide](/journal/best-pasta-maker-manual-vs-electric-vs-stand-mixer).

### Baking steel

→ [Shop baking steels on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=baking+steel+pizza&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

A slab of steel that sits on an upper rack of a home oven. Steel transfers heat into dough faster than a pizza stone, so a home oven can turn out a blistered, charred crust like the one above. It's heavy (about 15–16 pounds for the standard size), so check they have room to store it.

### Traditional balsamic vinegar

→ [Shop Aceto Balsamico Tradizionale DOP on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=aceto+balsamico+tradizionale+di+modena+dop&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

The real thing is labelled "Aceto Balsamico Tradizionale di Modena DOP" or "di Reggio Emilia DOP". It is cooked grape must aged at least 12 years in a series of wooden barrels, and it comes in a small bottle with a high price. It's used by the drop, on Parmigiano, strawberries or risotto. The everyday "Aceto Balsamico di Modena IGP" in supermarkets is a different, much simpler product. Both have their uses, but only the Tradizionale is an heirloom gift.

## What to Skip

- **Pasta-and-sauce gift baskets.** Mostly cheap pasta, jarred sauce and packaging. Two cans of real San Marzano and a bag of Gragnano pasta beat them.
- **"Balsamic glaze" sets.** Thickened vinegar with sugar or starch. Fine on a plate, but it's not a gift.
- **Pre-grated "Parmesan" in a tin.** It's not Parmigiano Reggiano, and it often contains anti-caking agents.
- **Electric pasta extruders as a first pasta tool.** They take up counter space and are hard to clean. Start with a manual roller.
