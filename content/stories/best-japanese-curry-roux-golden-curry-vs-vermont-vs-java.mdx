**Short answer:** for a classic, rich Japanese curry, buy **S&B Golden Curry** in medium hot. For a milder, sweeter curry that kids like, buy **House Vermont Curry** (it's made with apple and honey). For a spicier, more savory curry, try **House Java Curry**. Many Japanese home cooks mix two brands in one pot.

---

## Side by Side

| | Flavor | Sweetness | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S&B Golden Curry** | Spiced, savory, the classic | Medium | Everyday curry, katsu curry |
| **House Vermont Curry** | Mild, fruity (apple and honey) | Sweetest | Kids, people new to Japanese curry |
| **House Java Curry** | Bolder spice, deeper roast | Least sweet | Spice lovers, curry udon |
| **House Kokumaro** | Rich and mellow (onion and cream roux blend) | Medium | A thick, glossy sauce |

Each comes in heat levels, usually **mild, medium hot and hot** (Golden Curry also makes an extra hot). The numbers and names vary by brand, so read the box. Medium hot is the safest first buy.

## How Roux Works

A curry roux block is flour and fat cooked with curry powder and seasonings, then set into a bar. You simmer meat and vegetables in water until tender, **turn off the heat**, break in the blocks and stir until they melt, then simmer gently until the sauce thickens. Adding roux off the heat keeps it from clumping.

**Mix two brands.** A common home trick is half Golden Curry for spice and half Vermont for sweetness and body. Grated apple, a spoon of honey, a little soy sauce or a square of dark chocolate are other popular additions.

---

## Watch the Label

Most curry roux blocks contain **wheat** and often **dairy and soy**. They aren't gluten-free. Some brands sell allergen-free versions, such as a rice-flour Vermont made without the major allergens; check the allergen panel if it matters.
