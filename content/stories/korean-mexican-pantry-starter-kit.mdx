Korean and Mexican cooking look far apart on a map and very close in a pantry. Both build flavour from dried and fermented chili. Both lean on long, slow heat. A cook who stocks the right ten ingredients can cook either cuisine — and the dishes that live where they meet, like kimchi quesadillas, bulgogi tacos and gochujang-glazed carnitas.

Here is the kit, five from each side, and what each one unlocks.

---

## The Korean Five

### 1. Gochujang

→ [Shop gochujang on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=gochujang+korean+red+chili+paste+tub&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Fermented chili paste: heat, sweetness and savoury depth in one spoon. **Crossover use:** brush it on carnitas or stir it into a taco marinade. Not the same as gochugaru — see [gochujang vs gochugaru](/journal/gochujang-vs-gochugaru-difference).

### 2. Gochugaru

→ [Shop gochugaru on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=gochugaru+korean+red+pepper+flakes+coarse&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Sun-dried pepper flakes for kimchi, stews and seasoning. **Crossover use:** a pinch in salsa roja adds fruity warmth without extra heat.

### 3. Doenjang

→ [Shop doenjang on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=doenjang+korean+soybean+paste&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Fermented soybean paste for jjigae and dipping sauce. **Crossover use:** a spoonful in black beans deepens them the way a long braise would. See [how to use doenjang](/journal/korean-doenjang-uses-guide).

### 4. Toasted sesame oil

→ [Shop toasted sesame oil on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=korean+toasted+sesame+oil&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

A finishing oil, a few drops at the end. **Crossover use:** over elote-style corn with cotija.

### 5. Korean soy sauce (ganjang)

→ [Shop Korean soy sauce on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=korean+soy+sauce+jin+ganjang&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

The seasoning backbone: bulgogi, banchan, braises. Korean soy sauces come in several types for different jobs — our [ganjang guide](/journal/korean-soy-sauce-types-guide) explains which to buy first.

## The Mexican Five

### 6. Dried guajillo chiles

→ [Shop dried guajillo chiles on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=dried+guajillo+chiles&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Bright, tangy, moderate heat: the workhorse of red sauces and adobos. **Crossover use:** blend with gochujang for a marinade that is half Seoul, half Jalisco.

### 7. Dried ancho chiles

→ [Shop dried ancho chiles on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=dried+ancho+chiles&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Dried poblano — deep, raisin-sweet, mild. The base of many moles and enchilada sauces.

### 8. Chipotles in adobo

→ [Shop chipotles in adobo on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=chipotle+peppers+in+adobo+sauce&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Smoked jalapeños in a tangy sauce — the fastest route to smoky depth. **Crossover use:** a little in Korean barbecue sauce. Read [the science of chili pastes](/journal/chili-paste-science-gochujang-mexican-adobo) for why adobo and gochujang do the same job.

### 9. Masa harina

→ [Shop masa harina on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=masa+harina+for+tortillas&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Nixtamalized corn flour for tortillas, tamales and thickening. Corn is the other shared thread between the two kitchens — see [Corn, Two Passports](/journal/corn-two-passports-korean-oksusu-and-mexican-masa).

### 10. Mexican oregano

→ [Shop Mexican oregano on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=mexican+oregano+dried&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Citrusy and grassy, different from Mediterranean oregano. For pozole, salsas and beans.
