Japanese home cooking is simpler than its reputation. A good dashi, a few seasonings, rice done properly — most of the repertoire follows from there. The best Japanese cookbooks know this and teach the foundation before the showpieces.

Five books are worth owning.

---

## The Foundation

### Japanese Cooking: A Simple Art — Shizuo Tsuji

→ [Shop on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=japanese+cooking+a+simple+art+tsuji&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

The book that taught Japanese cooking to the English-speaking world, and still the clearest explanation of why each technique matters. Dense, thorough, and the reference you will return to for decades. Read it alongside our [complete dashi guide](/journal/japanese-dashi-complete-guide).

**Best for:** understanding, not just following.

### Japan: The Cookbook — Nancy Singleton Hachisu

→ [Shop on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=japan+the+cookbook+nancy+singleton+hachisu&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

A huge collection of Japanese home recipes from across the country. Sparse instructions, enormous range. Best once you have the basics.

**Best for:** breadth.

---

## Weeknight and Comfort Food

### Japanese Soul Cooking — Tadashi Ono and Harris Salat

→ [Shop on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=japanese+soul+cooking+tadashi+ono&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Ramen, curry, gyoza, katsu, donburi — the food Japanese people actually eat on a Tuesday. Friendly, practical, and the book most likely to get cooked from every week. Pair with our [gyoza guide](/journal/japanese-gyoza-guide) and the [history of Japanese curry](/journal/japanese-curry-history-guide).

**Best for:** the first book you will actually stain.

---

## The Flavour Logic

### Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat — Samin Nosrat

→ [Shop on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=salt+fat+acid+heat+samin+nosrat&tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Not a Japanese cookbook — but the best book on why food tastes good, and the logic transfers directly. Understand balance here and Japanese seasoning (soy for salt, mirin for sweetness, rice vinegar for acid) stops being a list of rules.

**Best for:** cooks who want to improvise.

### Tokyo Meets Tuscany — Borderless Kitchen Vol. I

→ [Get Tokyo Meets Tuscany on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0GY8H2TCQ?tag=borderlesskitchen-20)

Full disclosure: this is our book. Thirty recipes and six master sauces where the Japanese pantry meets the Italian one — miso in the risotto, dashi beside brodo, umami from two directions at once. Not a beginner's Japanese cookbook; a book for the cook who knows both cuisines and wants to see them work together. $19.99 paperback, $9.99 Kindle.

**Best for:** the cook who loves both Japanese and Italian food.
