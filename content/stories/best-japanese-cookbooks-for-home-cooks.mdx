A Japanese cookbook can be a guide to everyday dinners, a broad collection of traditional dishes or an invitation to combine Japanese ingredients with another cuisine. Start with the meals you want to cook rather than the size of the book.

## Decide what you want to learn

- **Comfort food:** look for dishes such as curry, gyoza and tonkatsu, plus explanations of the techniques they use.
- **A broader repertoire:** look for a collection that helps you explore unfamiliar dishes and ingredients over time.
- **Japanese-Italian fusion:** look for deliberate connections between the two pantries, alongside recipes you can preview.

Before buying, read a sample recipe. Can you follow the method with your current equipment? Does it explain the ingredients you do not recognize? Would you cook it on an ordinary evening?

## For Japanese comfort food: Japanese Soul Cooking

Tadashi Ono and Harris Salat's *Japanese Soul Cooking* focuses on Japanese comfort food. Its publisher describes recipes including gyoza, curry and tonkatsu. That scope makes it worth considering if those are the dishes you want to learn, rather than choosing a book mainly for sushi.

See the [publisher's book description and sample](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/218238/japanese-soul-cooking-by-tadashi-ono-and-harris-salat/) to judge the format for yourself.

## For a broad collection: Japan: The Cookbook

Nancy Singleton Hachisu describes *Japan: The Cookbook* as a collection of traditional Japanese recipes. Consider this route if your goal is to explore widely and you are comfortable building a pantry as you go. A large collection and a short first-course introduction answer different needs.

Read the [author's description of Japan: The Cookbook](https://www.nancysingletonhachisu.com/the-attic-loft) before choosing.

## For Japanese-Italian cooking: Tokyo Meets Tuscany

**This is our book.** *Tokyo Meets Tuscany* by Sebastian Dri combines Japanese and Italian cooking in thirty recipes and six master sauces. It is a fusion choice for someone curious about both cuisines. Start at the [book page](/tokyo-meets-tuscany) and browse the [Japanese-Italian recipe collection](/recipes/cuisine/japanese-italian).

## Check your pantry before buying

Review two or three sample recipes and list the ingredients you would need. Check whether the book explains how to use those ingredients again; one purchase becomes more useful when it serves several dishes. Our [Japanese pantry starter guide](/journal/best-japanese-pantry-starter-kit-ingredients) and [Japanese recipes](/recipes/cuisine/japanese) can help you explore before committing.

For a gift, ask whether the recipient wants everyday Japanese meals or new combinations. That distinction will help you choose more than a cover photograph will. Compare the [Italian cookbook guide](/journal/best-italian-cookbooks-for-home-cooks) if they enjoy both cuisines.
